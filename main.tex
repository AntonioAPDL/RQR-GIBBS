\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Probability content does not by itself determine interval placement. We study
the interval functional induced by relaxed quantile regression (RQR), whose
residual-product check loss estimates two unlabeled raw roots without
preassigning endpoint quantiles. Under explicit conditions, the unrestricted
population minimizer is the unique contiguous content-\(c\) interval whose
retained mean equals the population mean. A fixed mean tilt preserves content
while shifting that retained mean to \(\mu+\delta\). Interior admissible tilts
index all finite-root interior content-\(c\) windows; qualified one-sided
limits supply the boundary members. Equal-tailed and shortest-contiguous
intervals therefore have distribution-specific recovery tilts. We construct
loss-based generalized posteriors using a pseudo-asymmetric-Laplace
normal--exponential augmentation. One fixed-rate mean-tilted sampler covers
static regression under proper Gaussian priors, with ordinary RQR obtained
exactly at zero tilt. An ordinary-only conditional-Gaussian
regularized-horseshoe adapter using the Nishimura--Suchard augmentation
(RHS-NS) supplies regularized regression, and a frozen deep echo-state-network
feature matrix is a deterministic nonlinear-design specialization of the same
static scan. A dynamic linear extension then replaces coefficient blocks by
alternating root-specific forward-filtering backward-sampling steps: the
stacked state prior is Gaussian, but the joint augmented observation kernel is
quartic. Current software and empirical evidence concern ordinary RQR; the
nonzero-tilt algorithms are derived but not yet implemented or validated. All
updates concern interval-root functionals under a loss and prior, not a
response likelihood or posterior-predictive responses.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Interval forecasts matter when decisions depend on an uncertainty range rather
than on one location functional. A familiar construction fits the two
quantiles at \((1-c)/2\) and \((1+c)/2\)
\citep{KoenkerBassett1978}. That construction is appropriate for an
equal-tailed target, but content \(c\) alone leaves the interval unidentified:
equal-tailed, shortest-contiguous, and other intervals select different
members of the fixed-content class. Their evaluation should respect the
functional being targeted
\citep{BrehmerGneiting2021Intervals,FisslerEtAl2021SetValued}.

Relaxed quantile regression (RQR) instead estimates two interval roots directly
\citep{PouplinEtAl2024RQR}. It applies a check loss to
\((y-\eta_1)(y-\eta_2)\), whose sign records whether \(y\) lies between the
roots. The original article develops this loss as an optimization criterion.
Here we first identify its population interval functional and then develop a
loss-based generalized posterior and dynamic computation for it. Related
coverage-constrained empirical optimization directly trades width against an
empirical coverage constraint \citep{ChenEtAl2021PredictionIntervals}; that target
and training criterion are distinct from the expected RQR loss studied here.

The population score equations reveal more than the known content condition.
For an unrestricted continuous problem, the selected interval also preserves
the response mean after truncation. Profiling expected loss over its half-width
then identifies the mean-preserving window as the unique global minimizer
under explicit distributional conditions. A fixed mean tilt changes the
retained-mean equation while leaving the content equation unchanged. The tilt
therefore indexes interval functionals. It is fixed externally, or selected in
an external validation layer, rather than learned as an ordinary response
parameter.

Computation follows by exponentiating the declared loss
\citep{BissiriHolmesWalker2016GeneralBayes}. A
pseudo-asymmetric-Laplace normal--exponential representation, adapted from
Bayesian quantile-regression computation
\citep{yu2001bayesian,KozumiKobayashi2011Gibbs}, yields generalized
inverse-Gaussian latent scales and Gaussian conditional root blocks. The
augmentation acts on a pseudo-residual, not on the response distribution. A
fixed tilt changes only the Gaussian information vectors. This yields a
three-stage predictor hierarchy. First, static interval-root regression uses a
fixed design matrix and conditionally Gaussian coefficient blocks. Proper
Gaussian priors include ridge; at zero tilt, the implemented ordinary-RQR
branch also supports a root-specific RHS-NS regularization adapter
\citep{CarvalhoPolsonScott2010HS,PiironenVehtari2017RHS,
NishimuraSuchard2023SSS}. The nonzero-tilt derivation remains restricted to
proper Gaussian priors because propriety of the external random-scale adapter
has not been established. Second, substituting a deterministic, recorded DESN
feature design gives a frozen-feature specialization of this same static
regression and sampler
\citep{Jaeger2001EchoState,GallicchioMicheliPedrelli2018DeepESNDesign}.
The reservoir is a design object, not a posterior state.

Third, the dynamic extension replaces each coefficient vector by a linear
Gaussian state trajectory. Although the two trajectories have a jointly
Gaussian stacked prior, their joint augmented observation kernel is quartic.
Conditioning on either complete trajectory restores a linear Gaussian system
for the other, to which standard dynamic-linear-model forward-filtering
backward-sampling (FFBS) applies
\citep{GoncalvesMigonBastos2020DQLM}. Alternating those two path draws gives
exact root-blocked Gibbs updates for the declared fixed-joint RQR models.

The contributions are therefore: (i) a mean-preserving characterization and
global identification result for the ordinary RQR interval functional;
(ii) a fixed-content mean-tilted family that represents all admissible
interior windows, with boundary members obtained through qualified limits;
(iii) a first-order Cornish--Fisher approximation and finite-sample diagnostic
for fixed tilt initialization; (iv) a master fixed-rate mean-tilted Gibbs
construction whose zero-tilt member is the implemented ordinary-RQR sampler;
and (v) a predictor hierarchy from
regularized static regression, through a frozen-feature DESN specialization,
to a root-blocked dynamic linear extension. The ordinary-RQR ridge, RHS-NS,
frozen-DESN, and DLM branches are implemented.
Nonzero-tilt samplers, data-driven tilt selection, and variational
approximations remain proposed methodological developments.

The interpretation is deliberately narrow. The posterior-like update concerns
interval-root functionals under a loss and prior. It is not an ordinary
response likelihood and does not define posterior-predictive response draws.
Held-out loss, empirical coverage, width, and oracle endpoint recovery are
therefore primary evaluation quantities. Proper scoring rules remain useful
when their target matches the forecast object
\citep{GneitingRaftery2007,Gneiting2011,GneitingKatzfuss2014}; in particular,
the usual interval score canonically targets an equal-tailed interval and is a
secondary comparison here \citep{BrehmerGneiting2021Intervals}.
Learning-rate calibration and sandwich adjustment address distinct uncertainty
questions \citep{SyringMartin2019GeneralPosteriorCalibration,
Shaby2014OpenFacedSandwich}.

Section~\ref{sec:interval-functionals} defines the fixed-content class.
Sections~\ref{sec:rqr-loss} and~\ref{sec:mean-tilt} identify ordinary and
mean-tilted RQR. Section~\ref{sec:posterior} develops static regression,
ordinary RHS-NS regularization, and the frozen-DESN specialization.
Section~\ref{sec:dynamic} develops dynamic linear root states.
Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} states the evaluation strategy, and
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} concludes.

\section{Fixed-Content Interval Functionals}
\label{sec:interval-functionals}

Let \(Y\) have a nondegenerate continuous distribution with finite mean
\(\mu\) and strictly increasing quantile function \(Q\) on \((0,1)\). Modulo
probability-zero portions outside the support, every canonical contiguous
interval having content \(c\) can be represented by the probability window
\[
I_c(u)=[Q(u),Q(u+c)],\qquad 0\le u\le1-c.
\]
Define its retained mean and geometric width by
\[
M_c(u)=\frac{1}{c}\int_u^{u+c}Q(v)\,dv,\qquad
W_c(u)=Q(u+c)-Q(u).
\]
Probability content fixes the length of the window on the probability scale,
but not its lower-tail index \(u\). Table~\ref{tab:interval-functionals}
summarizes three principal placement rules.

\section{Ordinary RQR: The Zero-Tilt Foundation}
\label{sec:rqr-loss}

For generic ordered endpoints \(a<b\), define the residual product
\[
e(a,b;y)=(y-a)(y-b)
\]
and the check loss
\[
\rho_c(e)=e\{c-\ind(e<0)\}.
\]
Thus \(e(a,b;y)<0\) if and only if \(y\) lies between the endpoints. Write
\(a=m-h\) and \(b=m+h\), where \(h>0\). Then
\[
(Y-a)(Y-b)=(Y-m)^2-h^2.
\]
The half-width \(h\) controls interval content, while the midpoint \(m\)
controls which content-\(c\) interval is selected. The results below concern
an unrestricted pointwise population problem. Restricted regression classes
generally recover only design-weighted projections of these identities.

\begin{proposition}[Mean-preserving population target]
\label{prop:population-characterization}
Fix \(x\) and let \(a<b\). Suppose
\(\E(Y^2\mid X=x)<\infty\), the conditional distribution has no mass at \(a\)
or \(b\), differentiation may be interchanged with conditional expectation,
and \((a,b)\) is an interior stationary point over an unrestricted pointwise
endpoint class. Then the two population first-order conditions imply
\[
\Pr(a<Y<b\mid X=x)=c,
\]
and
\[
\E\{Y\ind(a<Y<b)\mid X=x\}
=c\,\E(Y\mid X=x).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\E(Y\mid a<Y<b,X=x)=\E(Y\mid X=x).
\]
\end{proposition}

The population content implication is due to
\citet{PouplinEtAl2024RQR}. The conditional first-moment equation is a further
consequence of the same first-order system and, to our knowledge, is not
reported in the original RQR article. It is equivalently zero conditional covariance
between \(Y\) and interval membership. It also balances the first-moment
contributions removed from the two tails rather than their probabilities:
\[
\int_{-\infty}^{a}\{\mu_x-y\}\,dF_x(y)
=
\int_b^\infty\{y-\mu_x\}\,dF_x(y),
\qquad \mu_x=\E(Y\mid X=x).
\]
The retained core and its excluded complement consequently have the same
conditional mean, although the endpoint midpoint need not equal that mean.
The supplement further shows that the retained core is a mean-preserving
contraction of the full distribution in convex order. A finite first moment is
enough for the retained-mean identity once the endpoint scores are valid. The
stronger second-moment assumption ensures that the product-check risk is finite
and supports the profiled-risk analysis. This distinction matters because the
loss grows quadratically in an extreme response.

\begin{theorem}[Quantile-window identification]
\label{prop:quantile-window}
In addition to the conditions of
Proposition~\ref{prop:population-characterization}, suppose that \(F_x\) is
continuous and strictly increasing on its support, and let \(Q_x=F_x^{-1}\).
Define
\[
M_{c,x}(u)=\frac{1}{c}\int_u^{u+c}Q_x(v)\,dv,
\qquad 0\le u\le1-c.
\]
Then \(M_{c,x}\) is strictly increasing and there is a unique index \(u_c(x)\)
such that \(M_{c,x}\{u_c(x)\}=\mu_x\). The nondegenerate unrestricted RQR
stationary interval is therefore
\[
a_c(x)=Q_x\{u_c(x)\},\qquad
b_c(x)=Q_x\{u_c(x)+c\}.
\]
If \(F_x\) is symmetric about \(\mu_x\), then
\(u_c(x)=(1-c)/2\), and the RQR and equal-tailed intervals coincide.
\end{theorem}

This theorem identifies the unique regular stationary window.
Proposition~\ref{prop:profiled-mean-tilt} below strengthens that statement to
the unique global expected-loss minimizer by profiling over the half-width.
The supplement proves these results and records the restricted score
equations. As \(c\) varies, the regular unrestricted population intervals form
a nested path that contracts to the conditional mean as \(c\) decreases to
zero. This path statement does not turn the midpoint at a fixed positive
content into a mean estimator. If the conditional law has endpoint atoms,
the corresponding directional conditions give
\[
\Pr(a<Y<b\mid X=x)\le c\le\Pr(a\le Y\le b\mid X=x),
\]
so exact open-interval coverage requires endpoint continuity. Coincident roots
reduce the loss to \(c(Y-a)^2\) and target the conditional mean rather than a
nontrivial interval. Exact retained-mean equality is not asserted at endpoint
atoms without a separate joint subgradient and mass-allocation analysis.

This motivates RQR as a direct interval target that can adapt to asymmetric
conditional error structure without assigning endpoint quantile levels in
advance. These equations characterize an unrestricted population target only
under the stated conditions. With linear or otherwise restricted root classes,
optimization instead enforces design-weighted score equations; it need not
give conditional coverage or mean preservation at every covariate value. We do
not use the original article's finite-sample unbiasedness argument: fitted
endpoints are data dependent, and the empirical objective is nonsmooth when an
observation coincides with a root. A valid finite-sample or asymptotic
generalization result requires a separate empirical-process analysis.

\section{Mean-Tilted RQR}
\label{sec:mean-tilt}

To move through the content-\(c\) family without changing the coverage score,
consider a fixed retained-mean tilt \(\delta\), measured in response units, and
define
\[
\ell_{c,\delta}(m,h;y)
=
\rho_c\{(y-m)^2-h^2\}-2c\delta(m-y).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\ell_{c,\delta}(a,b;y)
=
\rho_c\{(y-a)(y-b)\}-c\delta(a+b-2y).
\]
The second form shows that the loss remains invariant to exchanging the two
roots.
We call this fixed-target family \emph{mean-tilted RQR} (MT-RQR). Ordinary RQR
is exactly its \(\delta=0\) member.

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-content mean tilt]
\label{prop:mean-tilt}
Under the regularity conditions of
Proposition~\ref{prop:population-characterization}, a nondegenerate interior
stationary point of the mean-tilted population risk satisfies
\[
\Pr(a_\delta<Y<b_\delta)=c,\qquad
\E(Y\mid a_\delta<Y<b_\delta)=\mu+\delta.
\]
If \(F\) is continuous and strictly increasing on its support, then for every
\(\delta\) strictly between \(M_c(0)-\mu\) and
\(M_c(1-c)-\mu\), its unique interior quantile-window index solves
\[
M_c(u_\delta)=\mu+\delta.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[Profiled identification of the interior target]
\label{prop:profiled-mean-tilt}
Suppose that the support closure is an interval (possibly with infinite
endpoints), \(F\) is absolutely continuous and strictly increasing between its
support endpoints, its density is positive and continuous on the support
interior, and \(\E(Y^2)<\infty\). Extend \(F\) by zero and one beyond finite
support endpoints. Then, for every finite midpoint \(m\), there is a unique
radius \(h_c(m)>0\) satisfying
\(\Pr\{|Y-m|<h_c(m)\}=c\). Define the profiled risk
\[
\overline R_{c,\delta}(m)
=
\E\{\ell_{c,\delta}(m,h_c(m);Y)\}.
\]
Let \(u_c(m)=F\{m-h_c(m)\}\), with \(u_c(m)=0\) or \(1-c\) when the
corresponding root extends beyond a finite support endpoint. Then
\[
\overline R_{c,\delta}'(m)
=
2c\left[M_c\{u_c(m)\}-\mu-\delta\right]
\]
where the derivative exists, with the analogous one-sided statement at a
support transition. Consequently, for
\[
M_c(0)-\mu<\delta<M_c(1-c)-\mu,
\]
the expected mean-tilted loss has a unique global minimizer over finite ordered
roots. Its index \(u_\delta\in(0,1-c)\) is the unique solution of
\[
M_c(u_\delta)=\mu+\delta.
\]
Nondegeneracy gives \(M_c(0)<\mu<M_c(1-c)\), so ordinary RQR is the interior
special case \(\delta=0\). Exchanging the two raw root labels leaves the target
unchanged.
\end{theorem}

The supplement proves the radius existence and uniqueness, the envelope
derivative, and the one-sided behavior when an interval root crosses a finite
support endpoint. Finite second moments make both endpoint tilt values finite, even when a
support endpoint is infinite. At an endpoint tilt, the canonical interval is
defined by the corresponding one-sided probability-window limit. If the
support endpoint is infinite, this member is semi-infinite and is not attained
by finite root coefficients, although the limiting population-risk infimum is
finite. If the support endpoint is finite, roots beyond that endpoint are
inactive and the population loss does not identify a unique root
representation without an additional support constraint. Tilts outside the
closed admissible range make the unrestricted population risk unbounded below
in the corresponding escaping-root direction, although a proper Gaussian
prior can still yield a
proper finite-sample generalized posterior.

\begin{corollary}[Recovery tilts for familiar interval functionals]
\label{cor:recovery-tilts}
Ordinary RQR is recovered by \(\delta_{\mathrm{RQR}}=0\). Define
\[
\mathcal U_{\mathrm{SH}}
=
\argmin_{0\le u\le1-c}W_c(u),\qquad
\Delta_{\mathrm{SH}}
=
\{M_c(u)-\mu:u\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{SH}}\}.
\]
The population recovery tilt for the equal-tailed interval is
\[
\delta_{\mathrm{ET}}=M_c\{(1-c)/2\}-\mu.
\]
Each \(u\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{SH}}\) has recovery tilt
\(\delta_{\mathrm{SH}}(u)=M_c(u)-\mu\). Singular notation is used only when the
shortest-contiguous window is unique or one minimizer has been selected.
\end{corollary}

For an interior tilt,
\[
\frac{du_\delta}{d\delta}=\frac{c}{W_c(u_\delta)},\qquad
\frac{dW_c(u_\delta)}{d\delta}
=\frac{c\,W_c'(u_\delta)}{W_c(u_\delta)}.
\]
Thus an interior width minimum along the tilt path satisfies the usual
equal-endpoint-density condition.
Figure~\ref{fig:mean-tilt-map} visualizes this population map before adding
any approximation layer.

For initialization and external tilt screening, the same identities suggest a
simple first-order approximation. Let \(\gamma_1\) denote standardized
skewness and set
\[
q_c=\Phi^{-1}\{(1+c)/2\},\qquad K_c=q_c\phi(q_c)/c.
\]
A Cornish--Fisher expansion around a standardized Normal law gives the
shortest-oriented and equal-tailed standardized tilt anchors
\[
d_{\mathrm{SH}}^{\mathrm{CF}}=-\gamma_1K_c,\qquad
d_{\mathrm{ET}}^{\mathrm{CF}}=\frac{1}{3}d_{\mathrm{SH}}^{\mathrm{CF}},
\qquad d=\delta/\operatorname{SD}(Y).
\]
These formulas are not new interval targets; they are local skewness-based
approximations to the population recovery tilts. Right skewness gives a
negative shortest-oriented tilt, while left skewness gives a positive tilt.
The approximation is useful because it gives the sign and approximate
magnitude of the tilt from a single population summary. Figure~\ref{fig:mean-tilt-cf-anchors}
therefore places the CF anchors on the same tilt and width scales using the
true skewness of the illustrative population, not sample estimates. In applied
work a plug-in skewness estimate would be only a fixed initialization or
screening candidate, to be recorded outside the Gibbs target.

These are population and oracle statements. A universal tilt does not recover
the same interval functional across distributions, and conditional recovery
generally requires a covariate-dependent \(\delta(x)\). The tilt should
initially be fixed or selected in an external training/validation layer;
sampling it as an ordinary parameter would mix distinct interval targets. The
target is equivariant under positive affine changes of response scale when
\(\delta\) and the fixed learning rate are transformed accordingly, but it is
not invariant to arbitrary monotone response transformations. The current
RQR-DLM implementation and validation protocol use \(\delta=0\). Mean-tilted
computation and data-driven tilt selection require their own code and
validation before empirical claims are made.

The population score equations do not by themselves establish propriety of a
finite-sample generalized posterior under an arbitrary root prior. For a fixed
learning rate, a finite design or finite state horizon, and proper Gaussian
root priors, the tilt is linear in the root parameters and Gaussian prior tails
make the tilted kernel integrable. This argument covers the initial ridge and
Gaussian-state implementations; heavier-tailed shrinkage priors require a
separate propriety analysis before nonzero tilt is enabled.

\section{Static Interval-Root Regression and Generalized-Bayes Computation}
\label{sec:posterior}

\subsection{Static target and model-family status}

Let \(\mathcal O\) index the observed rows of
\(\mat X\in\R^{n\times p}\), with \(n_{\mathrm{obs}}=|\mathcal O|\), and
define two raw-root readouts
\[
\vect\eta_k=\mat X\vect\beta_k,\qquad k=1,2.
\]
Pointwise reporting uses
\[
L_i=\min(\eta_{1i},\eta_{2i}),\quad
U_i=\max(\eta_{1i},\eta_{2i}),\quad
M_i=(L_i+U_i)/2,\quad W_i=U_i-L_i.
\]
For priors \(\pi_1,\pi_2\), a fixed externally declared
\(\vect\delta\), and a fixed learning rate \(\omega_{\mathrm R}>0\), define
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\Pi_{\vect\delta}
(d\vect\beta_1,d\vect\beta_2\mid\vect y,\mat X)
\propto{}&
\pi_1(d\vect\beta_1)\pi_2(d\vect\beta_2)\\
&\times\exp\left\{-\omega_{\mathrm R}
\sum_{i\in\mathcal O}
\left[\rho_c(e_i)
-c\delta_i(\eta_{1i}+\eta_{2i}-2y_i)\right]\right\},
\end{split}
\label{eq:fixed-mt-target}
\end{equation}
where \(e_i=(y_i-\eta_{1i})(y_i-\eta_{2i})\). This is a
loss-based generalized posterior, not a response-likelihood posterior. The
tilt is fixed target metadata rather than a sampled response parameter.
Nonzero tilt uses a fixed learning rate and a proper Gaussian prior,
including a ridge prior,
in the present derivation.

\subsection{Loss-kernel augmentation and Gaussian blocks}
\label{sec:gibbs}

Let
\[
r_i=y_i^2,\qquad
m_i^{\mathrm R}=y_i(\eta_{1i}+\eta_{2i})-\eta_{1i}\eta_{2i}.
\]
Then \(r_i-m_i^{\mathrm R}=e_i\). With
\[
\sigma_{\mathrm R}=\omega_{\mathrm R}^{-1},\qquad
\xi_c=\frac{1-2c}{c(1-c)},\qquad
\phi_c=\frac{2}{c(1-c)},
\]
the exponentiated loss contribution is proportional to
\[
\int_0^\infty
\Normal\!\left(
r_i\mid m_i^{\mathrm R}+\xi_c v_i,\,
\phi_c\sigma_{\mathrm R}v_i
\right)
\Exp(v_i\mid \text{rate}=1/\sigma_{\mathrm R})\,dv_i .
\]
This identity is a computational representation of the loss kernel. It should
not be read as a response model for \(y_i\). Under the
three-parameter convention
\[
\GIG(p,a,b):\quad
p(v)\propto v^{p-1}\exp\{-(av+b/v)/2\},
\]
the observed latent scales have full conditionals
\[
v_i\mid\cdot\sim
\GIG\left(
\frac12,\,
\frac{\omega_{\mathrm R}}{2c(1-c)},\,
\frac{\omega_{\mathrm R}c(1-c)e_i^2}{2}
\right),
\qquad i\in\mathcal O.
\]
The ordinary learned-scale convention instead sets
\(\sigma_{\mathrm R}=s_L/\kappa\) and follows the separate partially collapsed
order stated in the following subsection and in the supplement.

Conditional on \(\vect\beta_2\), the pseudo-observation equation is linear in
\(\vect\beta_1\):
\[
\mat A_1=\diag(\vect y_{\mathcal O}-\vect\eta_{2,\mathcal O})
\mat X_{\mathcal O},\qquad
\vect z_1=\vect y_{\mathcal O}\circ
(\vect y_{\mathcal O}-\vect\eta_{2,\mathcal O})
-\xi_c\vect v_{\mathcal O}.
\]
With
\(\mat W_v=\diag\{(\phi_c\sigma_{\mathrm R}v_i)^{-1}:i\in\mathcal O\}\)
and Gaussian prior
canonical parameters \((\mat Q_{01},\vect h_{01})\),
\[
\mat Q_1=\mat Q_{01}+\mat A_1^\top\mat W_v\mat A_1,\qquad
\vect h_1=\vect h_{01}+\mat A_1^\top\mat W_v\vect z_1
+\omega_{\mathrm R}c\mat X_{\mathcal O}^{\top}\vect\delta_{\mathcal O},
\]
so
\[
\vect\beta_1\mid\cdot\sim
\Normal(\mat Q_1^{-1}\vect h_1,\mat Q_1^{-1}).
\]
The update for \(\vect\beta_2\) is identical after swapping root labels and
conditioning on the newly drawn first root. The tilt changes only the two
Gaussian information vectors: the latent-scale conditionals and Gaussian
precision matrices are unchanged.

\subsection{Regularized coefficient blocks and the static scan}

The fixed-design calculation is also the foundation for regularized ordinary
RQR. Let \(\vect\psi_k\) denote a root-specific prior state and suppose that,
conditional on that state, root \(k\) has Gaussian canonical parameters
\(\{\mat Q_{0k}(\vect\psi_k),\vect h_{0k}(\vect\psi_k)\}\). The implemented
ordinary-RQR MCMC adapter supports RHS-NS
\citep{CarvalhoPolsonScott2010HS,PiironenVehtari2017RHS,
NishimuraSuchard2023SSS}. As an input requirement for this branch, the first
design column must be the declared intercept and is excluded from shrinkage.
For every remaining coefficient \(j\), the current conditional prior precision is
\[
d_{kj}
=\frac{1}{\tau_k^2\lambda_{kj}^2}+\frac{1}{\zeta_k^2},
\]
with a separately declared Gaussian precision for the unshrunk intercept.
The local, global, auxiliary, and shoulder-scale quantities in
\(\vect\psi_k\) are updated after the corresponding coefficient draw. The two
roots carry distinct, identically specified prior states, and a root-label
swap moves \((\vect\beta_k,\vect\psi_k)\) as one complete block.

This is an external conditional-Gaussian adapter, not a native rederivation of
the marginal RHS-NS hierarchy. The RQR API accepts this \(\text{RHS-NS}\)
branch, rather than a generic \(\mathrm{RHS}\) mode. It is implemented only for
ordinary RQR, \(\vect\delta=\vect 0\). The Gaussian
moment-generating-function argument that establishes fixed-rate nonzero-tilt
propriety does not establish the same result after random-scale mixing.

\begin{algorithmblock}{Static fixed-rate RQR/MT-RQR root-blocked Gibbs scan}
\label{alg:fixed-design-gibbs}
\item \textbf{Declared target and applicability.} Target
\eqref{eq:fixed-mt-target} with fixed \(\omega_{\mathrm R}>0\), fixed
\(\vect\delta\), and proper Gaussian root priors, including ridge priors; or
the implemented ordinary-only branch with
\(\vect\delta=\vect 0\) and the conditional-Gaussian RHS-NS adapter. The scan
applies to fixed, recorded design rows; it does not define a response
likelihood.
\item \textbf{Inputs and fixed quantities.} Supply
\(\vect y,\mat X,\mathcal O,c,\omega_{\mathrm R},\vect\delta_{\mathcal O}\),
iteration controls, and a recorded label-swap rule. Supply either two Gaussian
prior canonical parameter pairs or two identically specified RHS-NS prior
states and their fixed hyperparameters. Missing rows are excluded from every
loss, latent-scale, and tilt site.
\item \textbf{Initialization.} Choose finite
\(\vect\beta_1,\vect\beta_2\) and positive placeholder \(v_i\) for every
\(i\in\mathcal O\). Initialize valid root-specific prior states when using
RHS-NS.
\item \textbf{One complete sweep.} Repeat for every warm-up and sampling
iteration:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=1.8em,itemsep=0.15em,topsep=0.15em]
\item compute \(e_i\) and draw every observed \(v_i\) from the GIG
conditional;
\item draw \(\vect\beta_1\) conditional on \(\vect\beta_2\) and its current
prior state from the Gaussian system with shift
\(\omega_{\mathrm R}c\mat X_{\mathcal O}^{\top}
\vect\delta_{\mathcal O}\);
\item in the ordinary RHS-NS branch, update \(\vect\psi_1\) conditional on the
new \(\vect\beta_1\);
\item rebuild the system and draw \(\vect\beta_2\) conditional on the new
\(\vect\beta_1\) and its current prior state, using the same shift;
\item in the ordinary RHS-NS branch, update \(\vect\psi_2\) conditional on the
new \(\vect\beta_2\); and
\item if authorized by exchangeability, swap both complete root labels and
all associated root-specific states together.
\end{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Stored quantities and label handling.} Retain raw roots only
when required for diagnostics, and report ordered endpoints \(L_i,U_i\),
widths, midpoints, losses, and complete target metadata. The optional
acceptance-one swap is omitted unless the two prior and hyperstate blocks are
exchangeable.
\item \textbf{Rate and computational status.} This is a fixed-rate scan.
At \(\vect\delta=\vect 0\) it is exactly the ordinary-RQR scan; the separate
ordinary learned-\(\kappa\) scan is Algorithm~S1.
\item \textbf{Implementation and validation status.} The conditional blocks
are exact for the declared fixed-rate proper-Gaussian generalized target.
Zero-tilt ridge and RHS-NS branches are implemented. Nonzero tilt is derived
for proper Gaussian priors, including ridge, but is not yet implemented or
empirically validated; the RHS-NS nonzero-tilt branch remains unresolved.
\end{algorithmblock}

At \(\vect\delta=\vect 0\), Algorithm~\ref{alg:fixed-design-gibbs} reduces
exactly to the fixed-rate ordinary-RQR scan. At zero tilt, the implemented
RHS-NS adapter updates the separate root-specific states displayed above.
That external adapter remains ordinary-only. It may also be used within the
ordinary learned-\(\kappa\) target and partial-collapse order below; this does
not establish either fixed- or learned-rate nonzero-tilt propriety.

\subsection{Frozen-feature DESN specialization}
\label{sec:desn}

Let \(\mat X_{\mathrm{DESN}}\) be the complete deterministic feature design
produced by a deep echo-state network, including any declared intercept column,
with fixed and recorded reservoir weights, scaling, washout, preprocessing,
tuning decisions, and random seeds
\citep{Jaeger2001EchoState,GallicchioMicheliPedrelli2018DeepESNDesign,
McDermottWikle2018DeepESNUQ}. Substituting
\(\mat X=\mat X_{\mathrm{DESN}}\) in
Algorithm~\ref{alg:fixed-design-gibbs} defines the RQR-DESN readout. No new loss
or Gibbs sampler is introduced. A ridge readout supports the proposed
fixed-rate nonzero-tilt Gaussian blocks; the implemented RHS-NS readout and
all current evidence concern ordinary RQR only.

Future root functionals use a frozen future design
\(\mat X_{\mathrm{DESN,fut}}\) constructed under the same recorded reservoir
contract, without using held-out responses to refit its features. Ordered
endpoints, midpoints, and widths are functions of the readout draws. No
response-simulation distribution is implied, and reservoir or feature
selection occurs outside the generalized-posterior scan.

\subsection{Ordinary-RQR learned inverse-loss scale}

A separate ordinary-only convention uses a positive reference loss scale
\(s_L\), sets \(\sigma_{\mathrm R}=s_L/\kappa\), writes
\(\mathcal L_{n,c}(\vartheta)=
\sum_{i\in\mathcal O}\rho_c(e_i)\), and declares
\[
\Pi_{\mathrm R}(d\vartheta,d\kappa\mid\vect y)
\propto
\pi(d\vartheta)\kappa^{n_{\mathrm{obs}}}\pi_\kappa(d\kappa)
\exp\{-\kappa\mathcal L_{n,c}(\vartheta)/s_L\},
\]
the \emph{pseudo-residual-normalized learned-scale target}. The factor
\(\kappa^{n_{\mathrm{obs}}}\) arises from normalizing an asymmetric-Laplace
kernel in the abstract pseudo-residual \(e_i\); it does not normalize a
response density in \(y_i\). Here \(\vartheta\) collects both roots and, when
present, their proper root-specific prior or evolution states. If
\(\kappa\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(a_\kappa,b_\kappa)\) in shape--rate form, its
collapsed conditional is
\[
\kappa\mid\vartheta,\vect y
\sim\mathrm{Gamma}\left(a_\kappa+n_{\mathrm{obs}},
b_\kappa+\mathcal L_{n,c}(\vartheta)/s_L\right).
\]
The factor \(\kappa^{n_{\mathrm{obs}}}\) is part of the declared target rather
than an irrelevant constant when \(\kappa\) is learned. A pure-loss sensitivity
target that omits this factor is kept separate.

The learned \(\kappa\) is part of this declared hierarchical generalized
target. Coherence for a fixed loss scale does not establish that \(\kappa\) is
an ordinary jointly learnable response parameter or that its generalized
posterior calibrates endpoint uncertainty
\citep{LeeLiuNicholls2025LearningRate,WuMartin2023LearningRateComparison}.
We therefore use fixed-rate sensitivity analyses as the primary specification
and treat learned-scale inference as an additional convention. When \(s_L\) is
computed from training responses, its calculation sample and variance
convention are recorded and \(s_L\) is frozen before simulation; the resulting
target is data dependent in an empirical-Bayes sense.

This target can be paired only with an otherwise valid ordinary fixed-design
or fixed-joint root adapter. Its partially collapsed scan draws \(\kappa\),
immediately refreshes every observed latent scale at the new value, and only
then updates roots 1 and 2; Supplement Algorithm~S1 gives the complete order.
For any proper ordinary root/prior-state law \(\pi(\vartheta)\), joint
integrability follows because the loss is nonnegative,
\(\exp\{-\kappa\mathcal L_{n,c}(\vartheta)/s_L\}\leq1\), and the Gamma prior
has a finite \(n_{\mathrm{obs}}\)th moment. This covers the declared ordinary
RHS-NS adapter while preserving its complete prior-state updates.
The mean-tilted loss may be negative, and a proper normalized joint target
with random rate has not been established. The Gamma update above must not be
copied to nonzero tilt.

\section{Dynamic Linear Root States}
\label{sec:dynamic}

The MT-RQR-DLM represents each raw root by a linear Gaussian state trajectory,
\[
\eta_{kt}=\vect f_t^\top\vect\theta_{kt},\qquad
\vect\theta_{k0}\sim\Normal(\vect m_0,\mat C_0),\qquad
\vect\theta_{kt}=\mat G_t\vect\theta_{k,t-1}+\vect\epsilon_{kt},
\qquad \vect\epsilon_{kt}\sim\Normal(\vect 0,\mat W_t),
\qquad k=1,2.
\]
The two trajectories can be written as one stacked state with block-diagonal
evolution matrices. That representation does not yield one Gaussian FFBS
update. Jointly in both root states, the augmented observation kernel contains
the square of
\[
(y_t-\vect f_t^\top\vect\theta_{1t})
(y_t-\vect f_t^\top\vect\theta_{2t}),
\]
and hence fourth-order cross-terms. Conditional on either complete root path,
the same expression is linear in the other path. Partitioning the stacked
state by root and drawing the paths sequentially therefore gives exact
full-conditional updates for the declared fixed-joint target. The concern is
Markov-chain mixing, not an approximation of either conditional.

For \(t\in\mathcal O\), conditional on the second root path and the RQR latent
scales, the first path has the pseudo-observation system
\[
z_{1t}=y_t(y_t-\eta_{2t})-\xi_c v_t,\qquad
H_{1t}=(y_t-\eta_{2t})\vect f_t^\top,\qquad
V_t=\phi_c\sigma_{\mathrm R}v_t.
\]
The second root is analogous and conditions on the newly drawn first path.
Under fixed-rate MT-RQR, the tilt adds the canonical linear site
\[
\vect g_t=\omega_{\mathrm R}c\delta_t\vect f_t
\]
to each root-state conditional. This information-form update never divides by
\(y_t-\eta_{\mathrm{other},t}\) and remains well defined when that quantity is
zero. Equivalently, if the ordinary
pseudo-observation update at time \(t\) gives mean
\(\vect m_t^{(0)}\) and covariance \(\mat C_t\), the tilted filter uses
\[
\vect m_t=\vect m_t^{(0)}+\mat C_t\vect g_t
\]
and the same \(\mat C_t\). This shifted mean is propagated before the next
update. At a missing response, the loss, \(v_t\), and \(\vect g_t\) are all
absent. In full-path canonical notation the same operation is
\(\vect m_{\mathrm{path}}^{(0)}+\mat Q_{\mathrm{path}}^{-1}\vect g\), which
provides a direct implementation check.

The evolution specification is part of the target. Fixed \(\mat W_t\) gives a
Gaussian state prior. A frozen discount template first maps declared
component-specific discounts to a complete \(\mat W_{1:T}\) sequence through
a reference covariance recursion and then holds that sequence fixed throughout
MCMC. Conditional on the frozen template, alternating root-specific FFBS is
exact for the stated prior; a template constructed from training data is
recorded as an empirical-Bayes specification.

\begin{algorithmblock}{Fixed-rate MT-RQR-DLM root-blocked Gibbs scan}
\label{alg:dynamic-gibbs}
\item \textbf{Declared target and applicability.} Use the fixed-rate
mean-tilted loss with two Gaussian root-state priors and either fixed
\(\mat W_{1:T}\) or one precomputed, frozen discount template. The scan does
not cover adaptive conditional discounts or random shared component scales.
\item \textbf{Inputs and fixed quantities.} Supply the responses and observed
index \((\vect y,\mathcal O)\), \(c,\omega_{\mathrm R},\delta_{1:T}\), all
design vectors \(\vect f_{1:T}\), all evolution matrices \(\mat G_{1:T}\),
proper Gaussian initial laws, fixed or frozen \(\mat W_{1:T}\), iteration
controls, and the label-swap rule.
\item \textbf{Initialization.} Choose finite root paths
\(\vect\theta_{1,1:T},\vect\theta_{2,1:T}\) and positive placeholder latent
scales at observed times. Do not instantiate a latent or tilt site at a
missing response.
\item \textbf{One complete sweep.} Repeat for every warm-up and sampling
iteration:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=1.8em,itemsep=0.15em,topsep=0.15em]
\item compute the current product residual and draw \(v_t\) at every
\(t\in\mathcal O\) from the GIG conditional;
\item conditional on \(\vect\theta_{2,1:T}\), build root 1's scalar
pseudo-observation system, add
\(\vect g_t=\omega_{\mathrm R}c\delta_t\vect f_t\) at observed times, and draw
\(\vect\theta_{1,1:T}\) by FFBS;
\item rebuild the system conditional on the new first path and draw
\(\vect\theta_{2,1:T}\) by the analogous tilted FFBS update; and
\item if all initial laws, evolutions, and root-specific states are
exchangeable, swap the two complete root blocks together.
\end{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Stored quantities and label handling.} Retain ordered
time-local endpoints, widths, midpoints, selected state functionals, losses,
and complete target/evolution metadata. These are root functionals, not
simulated responses.
\item \textbf{Rate and computational status.} This is a fixed-rate,
fixed-joint algorithm. At \(\delta_{1:T}=0\) it reduces exactly to the
implemented ordinary fixed-\(\mat W_t\) or frozen-template RQR-DLM scan.
\item \textbf{Implementation and validation status.} Sequential root-specific
FFBS gives exact full-conditional draws for the declared modes. Nonzero tilt
is derived but unimplemented; adaptive discount remains a working update, and
the random shared-scale extension remains ordinary-only.
\end{algorithmblock}

At \(\delta_{1:T}=0\), Algorithm~\ref{alg:dynamic-gibbs} reduces exactly to
the implemented fixed-rate ordinary-RQR-DLM scan for fixed \(\mat W_t\) or a
frozen template. A learned-rate tilted target has not been normalized and is
not obtained by inserting the ordinary Gamma update. Future-root propagation
uses a separate frozen forecast contract with recorded future
\(\vect f_t,\mat G_t,\mat W_t\); it neither changes the training Gibbs sweep
nor defines response simulation.

\subsection{Ordinary-only evolution variants}

For the implemented ordinary model, an exact component-specific alternative
partitions the state into blocks and sets
\[
\mat W_t(\vect q)=\operatorname{blockdiag}
\{q_1\mat Q_{1t},\ldots,q_J\mat Q_{Jt}\},
\qquad q_j\sim\operatorname{IG}(a_j,b_j),
\]
where the \(\mat Q_{jt}\) are fixed positive-definite templates. The multipliers are
shared across the two roots to preserve prior exchangeability. Sampling the
time-zero states restores the innovation factorization and yields conjugate
inverse-Gamma updates for each \(q_j\). The implemented component-scale kernel
first conditions on one root and integrates the other root through its
Gaussian pseudo-observation model. A slice transition updates the log scales
under this Kalman marginal, after which FFBS redraws the integrated root at the
accepted scales. The second root is then updated conditionally. Finally, the
sampler interweaves the centered inverse-Gamma update with a noncentered slice
update: state innovations are divided by \(\sqrt{q_j}\), the log scales are
updated conditional on those standardized innovations, and both root paths
are reconstructed. The global label swap makes the nominal choice of
integrated root symmetric across iterations. These partially collapsed and
interweaving transitions leave the same ordinary generalized posterior
invariant
\citep{PapaspiliopoulosRobertsSkold2007,YuMeng2011ASIS}. The complete
derivation and scan order appear in the supplement.

This component-scale interweaving is not silently generalized to nonzero
tilt. In the noncentered parameterization the linear tilt depends on
\(\sqrt{q_j}\) through the reconstructed state path, so the ordinary slice
target must be modified and validated; propriety of the resulting tilted
joint target must also be established. Likewise, the implemented
pseudo-residual-normalized learned-\(\kappa\) update remains ordinary-only.

By contrast, recomputing \(\mat W_t\) from each changing conditional filter
covariance makes the evolution depend on the other root and latent-scale
blocks. We retain that exdqlm-compatible discount recursion only as an
experimental working update. A two-time scalar mixed-derivative argument in
the supplement shows that its two advertised conditional FFBS kernels are
generally incompatible with one common positive smooth joint density.

\section{Evaluation Strategy and Evidence Scope}
\label{sec:evaluation}

The evaluation separates population identities, sampler validation, and
comparative forecasting evidence. Deterministic population calculations test
the fixed-content and mean-tilt characterizations without Monte Carlo error.
Small reference problems compare Gibbs output with quadrature, dense Gaussian
conditionals, analytic scale updates, and exact checkpoint continuation. These
checks establish target and implementation agreement for the stated fixtures;
they do not establish empirical coverage calibration or forecasting
superiority.

The matched ordinary-RQR study uses common data-generating mechanisms,
training and forecast windows, content levels, seeds, and scoring rules across
fixed-design RQR, RQR-DLM, separately fitted quantile intervals, and empirical
baselines. Its estimands, factors, Monte Carlo replication, failures, and
precision targets are frozen before execution following simulation-study
reporting principles \citep{MorrisWhiteCrowther2019Simulation}. Primary
summaries are held-out RQR loss, empirical coverage error,
width at comparable coverage, and endpoint recovery when a population oracle
is available. The equal-tailed interval score is secondary because it targets
a different placement principle. Dynamic diagnostics use time-local and
future ordered roots, widths, midpoints, losses, learning scales, and
component scales rather than only time averages.

Current computational evidence concerns ordinary RQR. Nonzero mean tilt
requires zero-tilt equivalence checks, independent small-target references,
and fixed-design validation at prespecified population tilts before any
data-driven selection rule is evaluated. Variational inference likewise
requires its own target, derivation, and uncertainty assessment. The frozen
RQR-DESN readout is evaluated as a static-design specialization: its reservoir
is recorded before the generalized posterior updates the root coefficients.
Matched nonlinear-readout evidence remains deferred. Detailed software status,
runtime attestation, and validation ledgers belong to the reproducibility
documentation rather than to the statistical argument.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Probability content alone determines a class, not an interval placement.
Ordinary RQR selects its mean-preserving member under the stated population
conditions. The mean tilt exposes the larger geometry: each admissible
interior value specifies a retained mean and hence one contiguous
content-\(c\) window. Equal-tailed and shortest-contiguous intervals are
distribution-specific members of this family rather than universally named
tilt values.

This interpretation also clarifies the role of Bayesian computation. The
normal--exponential representation is an augmentation of an exponentiated
loss in a pseudo-residual. It makes static root-regression blocks conditionally
Gaussian, but it neither supplies a response likelihood nor licenses simulated
responses. The ordinary RHS-NS adapter regularizes those coefficient blocks;
a frozen DESN feature design invokes the same static scan rather than defining
a separate interval model. The learned inverse-loss scale \(\kappa\) is a
convention within a declared normalized generalized target, not a response
variance or an automatic endpoint-calibration parameter.

The dynamic extension then preserves the interval target while replacing
static coefficients by evolving root states. A Gaussian stacked prior does
not overcome the quartic joint observation term; exact computation instead
alternates the two Gaussian root-path conditionals. Fixed evolution
covariances, frozen discount templates, and ordinary-RQR shared component
scales define fixed-joint targets. The nonzero-tilt component-scale
interweaving extension remains disabled. Recomputing discount covariances from
each changing conditional filter remains a working recursion, not an exact
Gibbs sampler for a demonstrated joint density.

Important limitations remain. The population results are unrestricted and
pointwise, whereas fitted regression and state-space classes impose
projections. Boundary tilts can yield semi-infinite limits or inactive roots,
and tilts outside the admissible range have no finite unrestricted target.
Nonzero-tilt algorithms, external tilt selection, variational approximations,
and matched nonlinear-readout evidence require separate validation. The
ordinary-RQR study is therefore designed to evaluate interval-root loss,
empirical coverage, width, and endpoint recovery without conflating any of
them with posterior-predictive response calibration.
