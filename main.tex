\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Prediction intervals built from two fitted conditional quantiles fix the
endpoint probabilities before determining which fixed-content interval
functional is scientifically relevant. Relaxed quantile regression (RQR)
instead estimates two exchangeable interval roots through a residual-product
loss. We show that, under regularity, the unrestricted nondegenerate population
target is the unique contiguous interval of the declared content whose
truncation preserves the response mean. We then introduce a fixed mean tilt
that changes the retained-mean condition while leaving the coverage equation
unchanged, thereby indexing the contiguous fixed-content interval family.
Ordinary and fixed-tilt RQR admit generalized-posterior formulations based on
a pseudo-asymmetric-Laplace normal--exponential augmentation: the latent scales
have generalized inverse-Gaussian conditionals, and each root has a Gaussian
conditional given the other. For a fixed learning rate, the tilt changes only
the Gaussian information vectors. The currently implemented target is ordinary
RQR, including fixed nonlinear readouts and a dynamic extension with sequential
root-specific forward-filtering backward-sampling. All reported draws summarize
interval-root functionals under a loss-based update, not posterior-predictive
responses.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Interval forecasts are central when decisions depend on uncertainty ranges
rather than on a conditional mean or a single quantile. A common construction
fits two quantile models, for example at probabilities \(\alpha/2\) and
\(1-\alpha/2\), and reports the resulting lower and upper curves. That
workflow is natural when equal-tailed quantiles are the target, but probability
content alone does not identify a unique interval. Equal-tailed, shortest, and
other asymmetric intervals select among fixed-content intervals by imposing
different balance conditions and should be evaluated against the functional
they are intended to estimate \citep{BrehmerGneiting2021Intervals}.

Relaxed quantile regression (RQR) provides a different route by estimating two
interval roots directly \citep{PouplinEtAl2024RQR}. For a target coverage
level \(c\in(0,1)\), RQR applies a check loss to the product
\((y-\eta_1)(y-\eta_2)\). The sign of this product records whether the
response lies between the two roots, so the loss targets an interval rather
than a preassigned pair of endpoint quantiles. The original RQR construction
is optimization-based. This paper develops a Bayesian generalized-posterior
route for the same core loss.

We first characterize the unrestricted RQR target. In addition to the known
coverage equation, the population first-order system implies that truncating
the response to the selected interval preserves its conditional mean. A
quantile-window representation then identifies a unique interval under
continuity and strict monotonicity. This distinguishes ordinary RQR from both
the equal-tailed interval, which balances omitted probabilities, and the
shortest contiguous interval, which minimizes geometric width. We also study a
fixed mean tilt that changes the retained-mean equation while leaving the
coverage equation unchanged. The tilt is a target index, not an automatically
learned response parameter.

The contribution is also computational. By exponentiating the RQR loss in the
generalized-Bayes sense of
\citet{BissiriHolmesWalker2016GeneralBayes}, the residual-product loss can be
written with the normal--exponential augmentation used for asymmetric Laplace
quantile regression \citep{yu2001bayesian,KozumiKobayashi2011Gibbs}. The
augmentation is applied to a pseudo-residual, not to the original response.
Conditional on the latent scales and one root, the other root enters linearly.
This gives Gaussian block updates and makes Gibbs sampling feasible for
high-dimensional readouts with ridge or regularized-horseshoe shrinkage
\citep{CarvalhoPolsonScott2010HS,PiironenVehtari2017RHS}. For a fixed mean
tilt and fixed learning rate, the same augmentation remains available and the
tilt adds a deterministic term to each Gaussian information vector.

The fixed-feature version embeds this sampler in nonlinear dynamic
representations. In RQR-DESN, a deep echo state network supplies a deterministic
feature map and posterior computation is performed only on the two interval
readouts \citep{Jaeger2001EchoState,LukoseviciusJaeger2009ReservoirComputing,
GallicchioMicheliPedrelli2018DeepESNDesign,McDermottWikle2018DeepESNUQ}.
The same algebra also suggests a linear dynamic RQR model: conditional on one
root trajectory and the latent scales, the other root trajectory has a Gaussian
state-space observation equation and can be sampled by FFBS. This extension is
important because it places RQR beside dynamic quantile and state-space
forecasting methods while preserving the direct-interval target
\citep{GoncalvesMigonBastos2020DQLM}.

The interpretation is deliberately limited. The generalized posterior updates
beliefs about interval endpoint functions under the declared RQR loss and
prior. It is not an ordinary response likelihood and does not define posterior
predictive response draws. Empirical validation should therefore focus on
held-out RQR loss, empirical coverage, width, endpoint recovery when oracle
RQR roots are known, root-switching diagnostics, and sensitivity to the
generalized-Bayes learning rate. The usual interval score is informative as a
secondary comparator but targets equal-tailed intervals and is not neutral
between all intervals having the same coverage
\citep{GneitingRaftery2007,Gneiting2011,GneitingKatzfuss2014,
SyringMartin2019GeneralPosteriorCalibration,Shaby2014OpenFacedSandwich}.

Section~\ref{sec:rqr-loss} characterizes the ordinary RQR functional, and
Section~\ref{sec:mean-tilt} relates it to other fixed-content intervals.
Sections~\ref{sec:posterior} and~\ref{sec:gibbs} develop the generalized
posterior and Gibbs sampler. Section~\ref{sec:embeddings} gives the DESN and
state-space embeddings, Section~\ref{sec:validation} states the validation
contract, and Section~\ref{sec:discussion} concludes.

\section{Relaxed Quantile Loss}
\label{sec:rqr-loss}

Let \(y_t\) be the response and let \(\vect x_t\in\R^p\) be a fixed design row.
For a coverage level \(c\in(0,1)\), define two exchangeable root readouts
\[
\eta_{1t}=\vect x_t^\top\vect\beta_1,\qquad
\eta_{2t}=\vect x_t^\top\vect\beta_2.
\]
The reported interval endpoints are ordered after fitting:
\[
L_t=\min(\eta_{1t},\eta_{2t}),\qquad
U_t=\max(\eta_{1t},\eta_{2t}).
\]
The root labels are not lower and upper labels; they are exchangeable
computational labels.

Define the residual product
\[
e_t=(y_t-\eta_{1t})(y_t-\eta_{2t})
\]
and the check loss
\[
\rho_c(e)=e\{c-\ind(e<0)\}.
\]
Then \(e_t<0\) if and only if \(y_t\) lies between the two roots. The empirical
RQR loss is
\[
\mathcal L_{T,c}(\vect\beta_1,\vect\beta_2)
=\sum_{t=1}^T \rho_c(e_t).
\]
For population interpretation, write the ordered endpoints as
\(a=m-h\) and \(b=m+h\), where \(h>0\). Then
\[
(Y-a)(Y-b)=(Y-m)^2-h^2.
\]
The half-width \(h\) controls interval content, while the midpoint \(m\)
controls which content-\(c\) interval is selected.

\begin{proposition}[Mean-preserving population target]
\label{prop:population-characterization}
Fix \(x\) and let \(a<b\). Suppose
\(\E(Y^2\mid X=x)<\infty\), the conditional distribution has no mass at \(a\)
or \(b\), differentiation may be interchanged with conditional expectation,
and \((a,b)\) is an interior stationary point over an unrestricted pointwise
endpoint class. Then the two population first-order conditions imply
\[
\Pr(a<Y<b\mid X=x)=c,
\]
and
\[
\E\{Y\ind(a<Y<b)\mid X=x\}
=c\,\E(Y\mid X=x).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\E(Y\mid a<Y<b,X=x)=\E(Y\mid X=x).
\]
\end{proposition}

The population coverage implication is due to
\citet{PouplinEtAl2024RQR}. The conditional first-moment equation is a further
consequence of the same first-order system and, to our knowledge, was not
stated in the original article. It is equivalently zero conditional covariance
between \(Y\) and interval membership. It also balances the first-moment
contributions removed from the two tails rather than their probabilities:
\[
\int_{-\infty}^{a}\{\mu_x-y\}\,dF_x(y)
=
\int_b^\infty\{y-\mu_x\}\,dF_x(y),
\qquad \mu_x=\E(Y\mid X=x).
\]
The retained core and its excluded complement consequently have the same
conditional mean, although the endpoint midpoint need not equal that mean.
The supplement further shows that the retained core is a mean-preserving
contraction of the full distribution in convex order. This interpretation is
tail sensitive: the product loss grows quadratically in an extreme response,
so the finite-second-moment condition is substantive rather than merely
technical.

\begin{proposition}[Quantile-window identification]
\label{prop:quantile-window}
In addition to the conditions of
Proposition~\ref{prop:population-characterization}, suppose that \(F_x\) is
continuous and strictly increasing, and let \(Q_x=F_x^{-1}\). Define
\[
M_{c,x}(u)=\frac{1}{c}\int_u^{u+c}Q_x(v)\,dv,
\qquad 0\le u\le1-c.
\]
Then \(M_{c,x}\) is strictly increasing and there is a unique index \(u_c(x)\)
such that \(M_{c,x}\{u_c(x)\}=\mu_x\). The nondegenerate unrestricted RQR
stationary interval is therefore
\[
a_c(x)=Q_x\{u_c(x)\},\qquad
b_c(x)=Q_x\{u_c(x)+c\}.
\]
If \(F_x\) is symmetric about \(\mu_x\), then
\(u_c(x)=(1-c)/2\), and the RQR and equal-tailed intervals coincide.
\end{proposition}

The supplement proves both propositions and records the restricted score
equations. As \(c\) varies, the regular unrestricted population intervals form
a nested path that contracts to the conditional mean as \(c\) decreases to
zero. This path statement does not turn the midpoint at a fixed positive
content into a mean estimator. If the conditional law has endpoint atoms,
the corresponding directional conditions give
\[
\Pr(a<Y<b\mid X=x)\le c\le\Pr(a\le Y\le b\mid X=x),
\]
so exact open-interval coverage requires endpoint continuity. Coincident roots
reduce the loss to \(c(Y-a)^2\) and target the conditional mean rather than a
nontrivial interval.

This motivates RQR as a direct interval target that can adapt to asymmetric
conditional error structure without assigning endpoint quantile levels in
advance. These equations characterize an unrestricted population target only
under the stated conditions. With linear or otherwise restricted root classes,
optimization instead enforces design-weighted score equations; it need not
give conditional coverage or mean preservation at every covariate value. We do
not use the original article's finite-sample unbiasedness argument: fitted
endpoints are data dependent, and the empirical objective is nonsmooth when an
observation coincides with a root. A valid finite-sample or asymptotic
generalization result requires a separate empirical-process analysis.

\section{Interval Selection and Mean Tilt}
\label{sec:mean-tilt}

Fix \(x\) and suppress it from the notation. Every contiguous interval having
content \(c\) can be written as
\[
I_c(u)=[Q(u),Q(u+c)],\qquad 0\le u\le1-c.
\]
In addition to the retained mean \(M_c(u)\), define its width
\[
W_c(u)=Q(u+c)-Q(u).
\]
The equal-tailed, ordinary RQR, and shortest-contiguous intervals select \(u\)
by different criteria:
\begin{center}
\TableStyle
\begin{tabularx}{0.95\textwidth}{@{}lXX@{}}
\toprule
Target & Selection equation & Interpretation\\
\midrule
Equal-tailed & \(u=(1-c)/2\) & Equal omitted probabilities\\
Ordinary RQR & \(M_c(u)=\mu\) & Mean-preserving truncation\\
Shortest contiguous & \(u\in\argmin_v W_c(v)\) & Minimum geometric width\\
Mean-tilted RQR & \(M_c(u)=\mu+\delta\) & Fixed retained-mean placement\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
For a regular interior shortest interval under a positive density,
\(f\{Q(u)\}=f\{Q(u+c)\}\). Under multimodality, a highest-density region may
be disconnected, whereas the intervals considered here are contiguous
\citep{BrehmerGneiting2021Intervals}.
Figure~\ref{fig:three-interval-principles} displays these distinct balance
conditions for one right-skewed population distribution.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/generated/fig01_three_balance_principles.png}
\caption{\textbf{Three interval-selection principles under right skewness.}
All panels use a standardized \(\mathrm{Gamma}(2,1)\) population distribution
and content \(c=0.80\). Equal-tailed (ET) intervals balance omitted
probabilities, ordinary RQR balances omitted first moments, and shortest
contiguous (SH) intervals minimize geometric width. These deterministic
population/oracle calculations do not provide finite-sample, calibration, or
comparative-performance evidence.}
\label{fig:three-interval-principles}
\end{figure}

To move through the content-\(c\) family without changing the coverage score,
consider a fixed mean tilt \(\delta\), measured in response units, and define
\[
\ell_{c,\delta}(m,h;y)
=
\rho_c\{(y-m)^2-h^2\}-2c\delta(m-y).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\ell_{c,\delta}(a,b;y)
=
\rho_c\{(y-a)(y-b)\}-c\delta(a+b-2y).
\]
The second form shows that the loss remains invariant to exchanging the two
roots.

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-coverage mean tilt]
\label{prop:mean-tilt}
Under the regularity conditions of
Proposition~\ref{prop:population-characterization}, a nondegenerate interior
stationary point of the mean-tilted population risk satisfies
\[
\Pr(a_\delta<Y<b_\delta)=c,\qquad
\E(Y\mid a_\delta<Y<b_\delta)=\mu+\delta.
\]
If \(F\) is continuous and strictly increasing, then for every \(\delta\)
strictly between \(M_c(0)-\mu\) and \(M_c(1-c)-\mu\), its unique interior
quantile-window index solves
\[
M_c(u_\delta)=\mu+\delta.
\]
The closure of this path, obtained by allowing finite endpoint tilts, spans the
full contiguous content-\(c\) interval family; endpoint windows are boundary
solutions governed by one-sided conditions.
\end{proposition}

Ordinary RQR is recovered by \(\delta_{\mathrm{RQR}}=0\). The oracle tilts
\[
\delta_{\mathrm{ET}}=M_c\{(1-c)/2\}-\mu,\qquad
\delta_{\mathrm{SH}}=M_c(u_{\mathrm{SH}})-\mu,
\]
recover the equal-tailed and shortest-contiguous intervals, respectively,
where \(u_{\mathrm{SH}}\in\argmin_u W_c(u)\). Moreover,
\[
\frac{du_\delta}{d\delta}=\frac{c}{W_c(u_\delta)},\qquad
\frac{dW_c(u_\delta)}{d\delta}
=\frac{c\,W_c'(u_\delta)}{W_c(u_\delta)}.
\]
Thus an interior width minimum along the tilt path satisfies the usual
equal-endpoint-density condition.
Figure~\ref{fig:mean-tilt-map} traces this one-to-one interior map and its
width profile for the same population benchmark.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/generated/fig03_mean_tilt_recovery_map.png}
\caption{\textbf{Population mean-tilt recovery map.}
For a \(\mathrm{Gamma}(2,1)\) distribution and \(c=0.80\), the retained-window
mean \(M_c(u)\) maps each interior lower-tail index \(u\) to a unique
standardized tilt \(d=\delta/\operatorname{SD}(Y)\). The marked oracle tilts

deterministic population/oracle theory and do not constitute an estimated-tilt
procedure.}
\label{fig:mean-tilt-map}
\end{figure}

These are population and oracle statements. A universal tilt does not recover
the same interval functional across distributions, and conditional recovery
generally requires a covariate-dependent \(\delta(x)\). The tilt should
initially be fixed or selected in an external training/validation layer;
sampling it as an ordinary parameter would mix distinct interval targets. The
target is equivariant under positive affine changes of response scale when
\(\delta\) and the fixed learning rate are transformed accordingly, but it is
not invariant to arbitrary monotone response transformations. The current
RQR-DLM implementation and validation protocol use \(\delta=0\). Mean-tilted
computation and data-driven tilt selection require their own code and
validation before empirical claims are made.

The population score equations do not by themselves establish propriety of a
finite-sample generalized posterior under an arbitrary root prior. For a fixed
learning rate, a finite design or finite state horizon, and proper Gaussian
root priors, the tilt is linear in the root parameters and Gaussian prior tails
make the tilted kernel integrable. This argument covers the initial ridge and
Gaussian-state implementations; heavier-tailed shrinkage priors require a
separate propriety analysis before nonzero tilt is enabled.

\section{Generalized Posterior and Augmentation}
\label{sec:posterior}

For priors \(\pi_\beta(\vect\beta_1)\) and
\(\pi_\beta(\vect\beta_2)\), define the generalized posterior
\[
\Pi_{\mathrm R}
(d\vect\beta_1,d\vect\beta_2\mid\vect y,\mat X)
\propto
\pi_\beta(d\vect\beta_1)\pi_\beta(d\vect\beta_2)
\exp\{-\omega_{\mathrm R}\mathcal L_{T,c}(\vect\beta_1,\vect\beta_2)\},
\]
where \(\omega_{\mathrm R}>0\) is a learning rate. A learned-scale version
uses a positive reference loss scale \(s_L\), sets
\(\omega_{\mathrm R}=\lambda/s_L\), and places a Gamma prior on \(\lambda\).
We call

\[
\Pi_{\mathrm R}(d\vartheta,d\lambda\mid\vect y)
\propto
\pi(d\vartheta)\lambda^{T}\pi_\lambda(d\lambda)
\exp\{-\lambda\mathcal L_{T,c}(\vartheta)/s_L\},
\]
the \emph{pseudo-residual-normalized learned-scale target}. The factor
\(\lambda^T\) arises from normalizing an asymmetric-Laplace kernel in the
abstract pseudo-residual \(e_t\); it does not normalize a response density in
\(y_t\). Here \(\vartheta\) collects both roots. If
\(\lambda\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(a_\lambda,b_\lambda)\) in shape--rate form, its
collapsed conditional is

\[
\lambda\mid\vartheta,\vect y
\sim\mathrm{Gamma}\left(a_\lambda+T,
b_\lambda+\mathcal L_{T,c}(\vartheta)/s_L\right).
\]
The factor \(\lambda^T\) is part of the declared target rather than an
irrelevant constant when \(\lambda\) is learned. A pure-loss sensitivity target
that omits this factor is kept separate.

The learned \(\lambda\) is part of this declared hierarchical generalized
target. Coherence for a fixed loss scale does not establish that \(\lambda\) is
an ordinary jointly learnable response parameter or that its generalized
posterior calibrates endpoint uncertainty
\citep{LeeLiuNicholls2025LearningRate,WuMartin2023LearningRateComparison}.
We therefore use fixed-rate sensitivity analyses as the primary specification
and treat learned-scale inference as an additional convention. When \(s_L\) is
computed from training responses, its calculation sample and variance
convention are recorded and \(s_L\) is frozen before simulation; the resulting
target is data dependent in an empirical-Bayes sense.

Let
\[
r_t=y_t^2,\qquad
m_t^{\mathrm R}=y_t(\eta_{1t}+\eta_{2t})-\eta_{1t}\eta_{2t}.
\]
Then \(r_t-m_t^{\mathrm R}=e_t\). With
\[
\sigma_{\mathrm R}=s_L/\lambda=\omega_{\mathrm R}^{-1},\qquad
\xi_c=\frac{1-2c}{c(1-c)},\qquad
\phi_c=\frac{2}{c(1-c)},
\]
the exponentiated loss contribution is proportional to
\[
\int_0^\infty
\Normal\!\left(
r_t\mid m_t^{\mathrm R}+\xi_c v_t,\,
\phi_c\sigma_{\mathrm R}v_t
\right)
\Exp(v_t\mid \text{rate}=1/\sigma_{\mathrm R})\,dv_t .
\]
This identity is a computational representation of the loss kernel. It should
not be read as a response model for \(y_t\).
Figure~\ref{fig:gibbs-computational-schematic} summarizes the resulting scan
without assigning a response-generating interpretation to the augmentation.

\section{Gibbs Sampling}
\label{sec:gibbs}

Conditional on \(\vect\beta_2\), the pseudo-observation equation is linear in
\(\vect\beta_1\):
\[
\mat A_1=\diag(\vect y-\vect\eta_2)\mat X,\qquad
\vect z_1=\vect y\circ(\vect y-\vect\eta_2)-\xi_c\vect v.
\]
With \(W_v=\diag\{(\phi_c\sigma_{\mathrm R}v_t)^{-1}\}\) and Gaussian prior
canonical parameters \((\mat Q_{01},\vect h_{01})\),
\[
\mat Q_1=\mat Q_{01}+\mat A_1^\top W_v\mat A_1,\qquad
\vect h_1=\vect h_{01}+\mat A_1^\top W_v\vect z_1,
\]
so
\[
\vect\beta_1\mid\cdot\sim
\Normal(\mat Q_1^{-1}\vect h_1,\mat Q_1^{-1}).
\]
The update for \(\vect\beta_2\) is identical after swapping root labels. The
latent scales have generalized inverse Gaussian full conditionals. For the
learned-scale target, one valid partially collapsed scan draws \(\lambda\) from
its collapsed Gamma conditional, refreshes all latent scales using that new
draw, and then updates roots 1 and 2. The refresh order is part of the sampler
contract. Adaptive shrinkage priors use separate shrinkage states for the two
root coefficient vectors.

For completeness, the fixed mean tilt in
Section~\ref{sec:mean-tilt} preserves these conditional families. Let
\(\vect\delta=(\delta_1,\ldots,\delta_T)^\top\) be a fixed tilt vector; a
scalar tilt is expanded as \(\vect\delta=\delta\vect{1}_T\). Under a fixed
learning rate, the first Gaussian information vector becomes
\[
\vect h_1^{\mathrm{MT}}
=\vect h_1+\omega_{\mathrm R}c\mat X^\top\vect\delta,
\]
and the second root receives the same shift. The latent-scale conditional and
the Gaussian precision matrices are unchanged. This algebra does not extend
the current learned-scale Gamma update: the tilted loss need not be
nonnegative, and a normalized joint target with random learning rate must be
derived separately. The initial mean-tilted implementation must therefore use
a fixed learning rate.

\section{Model Embeddings}
\label{sec:embeddings}

The Gibbs update applies to any fixed design. In a linear regression embedding,
\(\mat X\) contains ordinary covariates. In RQR-DESN, \(\mat X\) contains
deterministic deep echo state features computed from a recorded reservoir
architecture, seed, scaling rule, and washout convention. The posterior
conditions on those features and updates only the two readout surfaces.

For an RQR-DLM or state-space embedding, each root can be represented by a
latent state path, for example
\[
\eta_{kt}=F_t^\top\theta_{kt},\qquad
\theta_{kt}=G_t\theta_{k,t-1}+\epsilon_{kt},
\qquad \epsilon_{kt}\sim\Normal(0,W_{kt}),
\qquad k=1,2.
\]
The two trajectories can be written as one stacked state with block-diagonal
evolution matrices. That representation does not, however, give one Gaussian
FFBS update. Jointly in the two root states, the augmented observation kernel
contains the square of
\[
(y_t-F_t^\top\theta_{1t})(y_t-F_t^\top\theta_{2t}),
\]
and therefore includes fourth-order cross-terms. Conditional on either complete
root path, the same expression is linear in the other path. The sampler
accordingly partitions the stacked state by root and uses two sequential FFBS
blocks. These are exact full-conditional updates for the declared fixed-joint
target; the issue is potential Markov-chain mixing, not posterior
approximation.

Conditional on the second root path and the RQR latent scales, the first root
path enters through
\[
z_{1t}=y_t(y_t-\eta_{2t})-\xi_c v_t,\qquad
H_{1t}=(y_t-\eta_{2t})F_t^\top,qquad
V_t=\phi_c s_Lv_t/\lambda.
\]
This is a scalar-observation Gaussian state-space system, so the complete root
path can be sampled by forward-filtering backward-sampling. The second root
path is analogous and conditions on the newly drawn first path.

The evolution specification matters to the target. Fixed \(W_{kt}\) defines a
Gaussian state prior and gives exact alternating FFBS. A frozen discount
template first maps component-specific discounts to a complete \(W_{1:T}\)
sequence through a declared reference covariance recursion and then holds that
sequence fixed throughout MCMC. Conditional on that template, this gives an
exact sampler for the stated prior; a template estimated from training data is
recorded as an empirical-Bayes specification. By contrast, recomputing \(W_t\)
from each conditional filter covariance
makes it depend on the other root and latent-scale blocks. We retain that
exdqlm-compatible recursion only as an experimental working update. A
two-time scalar mixed-derivative argument in the supplement shows that its two
advertised conditional FFBS kernels are generally incompatible with a common
positive smooth joint density.

For an exact component-specific alternative, partition the state into blocks
and set
\[
W_t(\vect q)=\operatorname{blockdiag}
\{q_1Q_{1t},\ldots,q_JQ_{Jt}\},
\qquad q_j\sim\operatorname{IG}(a_j,b_j),
\]
where \(Q_{jt}\) are fixed positive-definite templates. The multipliers are
shared across the two roots to preserve prior exchangeability. Sampling the
time-zero states restores the innovation factorization and yields conjugate
inverse-Gamma updates for each \(q_j\); the complete derivation appears in the
supplement.

The implementation distinguishes mathematical target status, numerical
execution, and reproducibility eligibility. Fixed and component-scale modes
have a declared joint target, but they are target-and-numerical eligible only
when every Gaussian factorization succeeds without repair. The default policy
rejects jitter and negative-eigenvalue projection. An audit policy may instead
record the matrix role, time, root and iteration where applicable, jitter, and
eigenvalue diagnostics for every repair. Symmetry and definiteness tolerances
are relative to the actual matrix scale; an exactly zero matrix uses a
separately recorded absolute jitter fallback only under the audit policy.
Promotion additionally requires a clean checkout at an explicitly expected
repository commit.

\section{Validation Plan}
\label{sec:validation}

The standalone simulation study will compare fixed-design RQR, RQR-DESN,
RQR-DLM, quantile-derived intervals, and empirical interval baselines under
matched data-generating mechanisms, samples, train/test splits, seeds, and
forecast windows. Primary summaries are held-out RQR loss, empirical coverage
and coverage error, width at comparable coverage, and endpoint recovery against
oracle RQR roots. Midpoint error, root switching, learned-scale diagnostics,
and the standard interval score are secondary summaries. Results will be
stratified by data-generating mechanism and forecast horizon rather than
collapsed into unsupported global claims. VB may be useful for screening or
initialization, but calibrated VB uncertainty will not be promoted until it
receives its own derivation and validation.

This matched study concerns ordinary RQR, equivalently the zero-tilt target.
Mean-tilted RQR will follow a separate staged protocol: deterministic oracle
calculations, intercept-only quadrature checks, exact zero-tilt equivalence,
fixed-design Gibbs validation at prespecified oracle tilts, and only then an
independent training/validation/test study of tilt selection. Oracle recovery
of an equal-tailed or shortest interval will not be described as evidence that
the corresponding tilt can be estimated from finite data.

The standard interval score remains secondary because its canonical endpoints
are the corresponding equal-tailed quantiles, whereas RQR does not preassign
endpoint probabilities. Promotion-grade computation uses versioned target,
data, numerical-policy, RNG, environment, and continuation records; the full
provenance and attestation contract is documented in the supplement and
implementation notes. Terminal states and component-scale draws are retained
for root forecasting with explicit future covariance templates.

A bounded intercept-only learned-scale fixture cross-checks the production
partially collapsed scan against a separately coded fully augmented scan and
event-boundary-aware root quadrature after analytically integrating the loss
scale. Four
chains per scan satisfy the predeclared rank-normalized convergence and
effective-sample-size gates, all mean and CDF comparisons satisfy their
Monte Carlo error bounds, and no numerical repair is used. This verifies the
implemented small-fixture target; it does not establish frequentist coverage
calibration or authorize the production simulation.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

RQR-GIBBS is best viewed as a generalized-Bayes workflow for direct interval
targets. Ordinary RQR selects the mean-preserving member of the contiguous
fixed-content interval family, rather than an unspecified interval or an
equal-tailed pair. The fixed mean tilt clarifies how this target relates at the
population level to other tail allocations, but it does not yet supply an
automatic finite-sample shortest-interval procedure. The native fixed-design
and state-space paths currently implement ordinary RQR and share the same loss,
scale, and root-ordering contracts. Adaptive discounting has been resolved as a
working rather than exact conditional recursion, while the shared
component-scale construction provides a coherent alternative. Production
evidence remains gated on a frozen matched simulation protocol; no
response-predictive interpretation follows from the loss update.
