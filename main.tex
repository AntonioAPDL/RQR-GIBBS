\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Probability content does not uniquely determine the placement of an interval.
Relaxed quantile regression (RQR) estimates two interval roots directly by
applying check loss to their residual product. We show that, under regularity
conditions, ordinary RQR selects the unique fixed-content interval whose
retained mean equals the population mean. Mean-tilted RQR (MT-RQR) replaces
this balance condition by a fixed retained-mean offset, thereby indexing the
family of contiguous intervals with the prescribed content. Equal-tailed and
shortest-contiguous intervals arise at distribution-specific tilts. We update
proper Gaussian priors by the MT-RQR loss and derive a pseudo-asymmetric-Laplace
normal--exponential augmentation with generalized-inverse-Gaussian latent
scales and conditionally Gaussian root updates. For fixed content, tilt, and
learning rate, the same augmentation gives a deterministic
expectation/conditional-maximization mode computation based on exact inverse
latent-scale moments. The resulting Gibbs construction covers
ridge-regularized static regression and frozen-feature deep echo state network
readouts, and extends to dynamic linear root states through alternating
forward-filtering backward-sampling updates. Population calculations,
Cornish--Fisher diagnostics, and reproducible single-data illustrations clarify
target placement and computation. We also formulate scan-calibrated and
pilot-selected exact-spacing tolerance constructions for continuous iid data.
The proposed tolerance actions are empirical order-statistic intervals, not
generalized-posterior summaries; action-matched scan certification and transfer
of validity to posterior summaries remain open. These are loss-based
generalized posteriors for interval functionals, not posteriors derived from
response likelihoods, and hence do not by themselves define posterior
predictive distributions for future responses.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

An interval with prescribed probability content is not uniquely determined.
Equal-tailed, shortest-contiguous, and other intervals can contain the same
population fraction while having different endpoints and widths. Tolerance
intervals add a repeated-sampling requirement: with confidence
\(1-\alpha\), the reported random set should contain at least content \(c\)
of the population distribution
\citep{Wilks1941ToleranceLimits,Wilks1942StatisticalPrediction,Robbins1944DistributionFreeTolerance}.
A familiar regression construction fits the two conditional quantiles at
\((1-c)/2\) and \((1+c)/2\) \citep{KoenkerBassett1978}. It is appropriate for
an equal-tailed target, but it chooses a placement that content alone leaves
unspecified. Evaluation must therefore be aligned with the interval functional
being estimated
\citep{BrehmerGneiting2021Intervals,FisslerEtAl2021SetValued}.

Relaxed quantile regression (RQR) estimates two interval roots directly
\citep{PouplinEtAl2024RQR}. Its nonnegative loss is constructed from
\((y-\eta_1)(y-\eta_2)\), whose sign records whether \(y\) lies between the
roots. Equivalently, it is a check loss applied to this product residual, not
to either response residual separately. The original article develops the
loss as an optimization criterion. Here its population characterization leads
to a mean-tilted family, generalized posteriors, and Gibbs samplers for static,
frozen-feature, and dynamic root models. A separate scan-calibrated
construction addresses tolerance calibration. Coverage-constrained empirical
optimization instead trades width directly against an empirical coverage
constraint \citep{ChenEtAl2021PredictionIntervals}; its target and training
criterion differ from the expected RQR loss considered below.

For an unrestricted continuous problem, the population score equations imply
both the prescribed content and preservation of the response mean after
truncation. Profiling expected loss over the half-width identifies this
mean-preserving window as the unique global minimizer under explicit
distributional conditions. A fixed mean tilt changes the retained-mean
equation while leaving the content equation unchanged. The tilt therefore
indexes interval functionals and is fixed externally, or selected in a
separate validation layer, rather than learned as a response parameter.

For tolerance use, the proposed procedure first calibrates a retained count
with a distribution-free scan statistic, selects the shortest empirical
window containing that count, and records its MT-RQR tilt. The content and
tilt are then held fixed during generalized-Bayes computation. The empirical
window remains the tolerance action; generalized-posterior summaries describe
the associated fixed interval-root target and do not supply the tolerance
certificate.

Following generalized Bayes, a prior is updated by
\(\exp\{-\omega_{\mathrm R}\sum_i\ell_i\}\) to define a generalized posterior
\citep{BissiriHolmesWalker2016GeneralBayes}. Introducing latent scales through
a pseudo-asymmetric-Laplace normal--exponential representation, adapted from
Bayesian quantile-regression computation
\citep{yu2001bayesian,KozumiKobayashi2011Gibbs}, yields generalized
inverse-Gaussian latent-scale full conditionals and Gaussian root full
conditionals. This latent-variable representation augments the generalized
posterior through a pseudo-residual; it does not specify a sampling
distribution for the response. A fixed tilt changes only the Gaussian
information vectors. The same conditional structure supports static
interval-root regression, frozen deep echo state network (DESN) feature readouts
\citep{Jaeger2001EchoState,GallicchioMicheliPedrelli2018DeepESNDesign},
and dynamic linear root states.
For deterministic computation at a fixed target, the augmentation also yields
an ECM algorithm \citep{DempsterLairdRubin1977EM,MengRubin1993ECM}: the
E-step uses inverse latent-scale moments, and two conditional maximization
steps solve the same Gaussian root systems. As in asymmetric-Laplace
quantile-regression EM computations \citep{ZhaoLian2016EMBayesianQR}, this is
a computational device for a fixed loss-defined target, not a response
likelihood.

The dynamic extension replaces each coefficient vector by a linear Gaussian
state trajectory. The two trajectories have a jointly Gaussian stacked prior,
but their joint contribution to the augmented generalized posterior is
quartic. Conditioning on either complete trajectory restores a linear
Gaussian system for the other, to which dynamic-linear-model
forward-filtering backward-sampling (FFBS) applies
\citep{GoncalvesMigonBastos2020DQLM}. Alternating those two path draws gives
exact root-blocked Gibbs updates for the specified fixed-joint generalized
posterior.

The statistical and computational contributions are fourfold. First, ordinary
RQR is identified
as the mean-preserving member of the fixed-content interval class, and a fixed
mean tilt indexes the remaining admissible windows; the resulting theory also
gives exact fractional empirical balance relations. Second, a pseudo-AL Gibbs
sampler is derived for fixed-rate static targets and extended to regularized
static regression, frozen-feature DESN readouts, and root-blocked dynamic
linear states. Third, a fixed-target MT-RQR-ECM optimizer is derived as a fast
deterministic mode computation using exact inverse latent-scale moments and
safeguarded objective descent. Fourth, scan-calibrated shortest-window and
pilot-selected exact-spacing tolerance actions are formulated while their
repeated-sampling guarantees are kept separate from generalized-posterior and
ECM summaries. The available nonzero-tilt computation uses proper Gaussian
priors, a fixed learning rate, and fixed or frozen dynamic evolution
covariances. Random shrinkage scales, learned-rate tilt, adaptive or
component-scale dynamic tilt, data-driven tilt selection, variational
approximations, and regression-family tolerance calibration require additional
theory and validation.

The generalized posterior concerns interval-root functionals defined by a loss
and prior. It is not derived from an ordinary response likelihood and does not
define posterior predictive draws of future responses. Held-out loss,
empirical coverage, width, and oracle endpoint recovery are therefore the
primary evaluation quantities. Proper scoring rules remain useful
when their target matches the forecast object
\citep{GneitingRaftery2007,Gneiting2011,GneitingKatzfuss2014}; in particular,
the usual interval score canonically targets an equal-tailed interval and is a
secondary comparison here \citep{BrehmerGneiting2021Intervals}.
Learning-rate calibration and sandwich adjustment address distinct uncertainty
questions \citep{SyringMartin2019GeneralPosteriorCalibration,
Shaby2014OpenFacedSandwich}.

\section{Fixed-Content Interval Functionals}
\label{sec:interval-functionals}

Let \(Y\) have a nondegenerate continuous distribution with finite mean
\(\mu\) and strictly increasing quantile function \(Q\) on \((0,1)\). Apart
from probability-zero subsets outside the support, every contiguous interval
of content \(c\) can be represented by the probability window
\[
I_c(u)=[Q(u),Q(u+c)],\qquad 0\le u\le1-c.
\]
Define its retained mean and geometric width by
\[
M_c(u)=\frac{1}{c}\int_u^{u+c}Q(v)\,dv,\qquad
W_c(u)=Q(u+c)-Q(u).
\]
Probability content fixes the length of the window on the probability scale,
but not its lower-tail index \(u\). Table~\ref{tab:interval-functionals}
summarizes three principal placement rules.

\section{Ordinary RQR: Product Loss and Population Target}
\label{sec:rqr-loss}

Fix a desired interval content \(c\in(0,1)\) and ordered endpoints \(a<b\).
The pointwise ordinary-RQR loss is
\begin{equation}
\ell_c(a,b;y)=
\begin{cases}
(1-c)(y-a)(b-y), & a<y<b,\\
c(y-a)(y-b), & y\le a\ \text{or}\ y\ge b.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:rqr-loss-piecewise}
\end{equation}
Both branches are nonnegative and meet continuously at zero when \(y=a\) or
\(y=b\). An observation inside the interval is weighted by \(1-c\), whereas a
miss is weighted by \(c\). The two factors also encode distance from both
endpoints, so this is not a binary coverage penalty. Increasing \(c\) raises
the relative cost of a miss; at a regular population optimum, this weighting
produces the content equation established below. For data
\(y_1,\ldots,y_n\), ordinary RQR minimizes
\(\sum_{i=1}^n\ell_c(a,b;y_i)\), or the corresponding regression loss when the
two endpoints depend on predictors.

The familiar check-loss notation gives an equivalent compact representation.
For a scalar argument \(r\), let
\[
\rho_c(r)=r\{c-\ind(r<0)\}
=
\begin{cases}
(1-c)|r|, & r<0,\\
cr, & r\ge0.
\end{cases}
\]
Now define the product residual
\[
e(a,b;y)=(y-a)(y-b).
\]
Because \(e(a,b;y)<0\) exactly when \(a<y<b\),
\begin{equation}
\ell_c(a,b;y)=\rho_c\{e(a,b;y)\}.
\label{eq:rqr-check-representation}
\end{equation}
This representation also makes clear that the loss is symmetric in its two
roots; the ordering \(a<b\) is imposed only to interpret them as lower and
upper endpoints.
The check loss therefore acts on the scalar product residual, not on
\(y-a\) or \(y-b\) separately. In particular, \(c\) is the desired interval
content; it is neither an assigned response-quantile level for either endpoint
nor the generalized-Bayes learning rate.

The midpoint--half-width parameterization makes the interval geometry
explicit. Write \(a=m-h\) and \(b=m+h\), where \(h>0\). Then
\[
e(a,b;Y)=(Y-m)^2-h^2,
\]
so \(e<0\) means that \(Y\) is within distance \(h\) of \(m\). The half-width
and midpoint are learned jointly by minimizing the expected loss
\(R_c(a,b)=\E\{\ell_c(a,b;Y)\}\). Its first-order conditions determine both
the interval content and its placement. The loss defines this population
estimand; the generalized-Bayes learning rate introduced in
Section~\ref{sec:posterior} separately controls how strongly the loss updates
the prior. The results below concern an unrestricted pointwise population
problem. Restricted regression classes generally recover only design-weighted
projections of these identities.

\begin{proposition}[Mean-preserving population target]
\label{prop:population-characterization}
Fix \(x\) and let \(a<b\). Suppose
\(\E(Y^2\mid X=x)<\infty\), the conditional distribution has no mass at \(a\)
or \(b\), differentiation may be interchanged with conditional expectation,
and \((a,b)\) is an interior stationary point over an unrestricted pointwise
endpoint class. Then the two population first-order conditions imply
\[
\Pr(a<Y<b\mid X=x)=c,
\]
and
\[
\E\{Y\ind(a<Y<b)\mid X=x\}
=c\,\E(Y\mid X=x).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\E(Y\mid a<Y<b,X=x)=\E(Y\mid X=x).
\]
\end{proposition}

The population content implication is due to
\citet{PouplinEtAl2024RQR}. The conditional first-moment equation is a further
consequence of the same first-order system. Equivalently, it implies zero
conditional covariance between \(Y\) and interval membership. It also balances
the first-moment contributions removed from the two tails rather than their
probabilities:
\[
\int_{-\infty}^{a}\{\mu_x-y\}\,dF_x(y)
=
\int_b^\infty\{y-\mu_x\}\,dF_x(y),
\qquad \mu_x=\E(Y\mid X=x).
\]
The retained core and its excluded complement consequently have the same
conditional mean, although the endpoint midpoint need not equal that mean.
The supplement further shows that the retained core is a mean-preserving
contraction of the full distribution in convex order. A finite first moment is
enough for the retained-mean identity once the endpoint scores are valid. The
stronger second-moment assumption ensures that the expected ordinary-RQR loss
is finite and supports the profiled-risk analysis. This distinction matters
because the loss grows quadratically in an extreme response.

\begin{theorem}[Quantile-window identification]
\label{prop:quantile-window}
In addition to the conditions of
Proposition~\ref{prop:population-characterization}, suppose that \(F_x\) is
continuous and strictly increasing on its support, and let \(Q_x=F_x^{-1}\).
Define
\[
M_{c,x}(u)=\frac{1}{c}\int_u^{u+c}Q_x(v)\,dv,
\qquad 0\le u\le1-c.
\]
Then \(M_{c,x}\) is strictly increasing and there is a unique index \(u_c(x)\)
such that \(M_{c,x}\{u_c(x)\}=\mu_x\). The nondegenerate unrestricted RQR
stationary interval is therefore
\[
a_c(x)=Q_x\{u_c(x)\},\qquad
b_c(x)=Q_x\{u_c(x)+c\}.
\]
If \(F_x\) is symmetric about \(\mu_x\), then
\(u_c(x)=(1-c)/2\), and the RQR and equal-tailed intervals coincide.
\end{theorem}

This theorem identifies the unique regular stationary window.
Proposition~\ref{prop:profiled-mean-tilt} below strengthens that statement to
the unique global expected-loss minimizer by profiling over the half-width.
The supplement proves these results and records the restricted score
equations. Under the connected-support and local positive-density conditions
used in Theorem~\ref{prop:profiled-mean-tilt}, the regular unrestricted
population intervals form a nested path. If the conditional quantile function
is continuous at \(p_{\mu,x}=F_x(\mu_x)\) and
\(Q_x(p_{\mu,x})=\mu_x\), the two endpoints converge to \(\mu_x\) as
\(c\downarrow0\). Without this local support condition, the zero-content
limit may straddle a support gap. This path statement does not turn the
midpoint at a fixed positive content into a mean estimator. If the
conditional law has endpoint atoms,
the corresponding directional conditions give
\[
\Pr(a<Y<b\mid X=x)\le c\le\Pr(a\le Y\le b\mid X=x),
\]
so exact open-interval coverage requires endpoint continuity. Coincident roots
reduce the loss to \(c(Y-a)^2\) and target the conditional mean rather than a
nontrivial interval. Exact retained-mean equality is not asserted at endpoint
atoms without a separate joint subgradient and mass-allocation analysis.

RQR therefore targets an interval directly and can accommodate asymmetric
conditional distributions without assigning endpoint quantile levels in
advance. The characterization is pointwise and unrestricted; it holds only
under the stated conditions. Linear and other restricted root classes instead
satisfy design-weighted score equations and need not attain conditional
coverage or mean preservation at every covariate value. Nor does the
finite-sample unbiasedness argument for fixed endpoints apply automatically to
fitted endpoints: the endpoints depend on the data, and the empirical
objective is nonsmooth when an observation coincides with a root. Finite-sample
or asymptotic guarantees require a separate empirical-process argument.

\section{Mean-Tilted RQR}
\label{sec:mean-tilt}

To move through the content-\(c\) family without changing the coverage score,
consider a fixed retained-mean tilt \(\delta\), measured in response units, and
define
\[
\ell_{c,\delta}(m,h;y)
=
\rho_c\{(y-m)^2-h^2\}-2c\delta(m-y).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\ell_{c,\delta}(a,b;y)
=
\rho_c\{(y-a)(y-b)\}-c\delta(a+b-2y).
\]
The second form shows that the loss remains invariant to exchanging the two
roots.
We call this fixed-target family \emph{mean-tilted RQR} (MT-RQR). Ordinary RQR
is exactly its \(\delta=0\) member.

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-content mean tilt]
\label{prop:mean-tilt}
Under the regularity conditions of
Proposition~\ref{prop:population-characterization}, a nondegenerate interior
stationary point of the mean-tilted population risk satisfies
\[
\Pr(a_\delta<Y<b_\delta)=c,\qquad
\E(Y\mid a_\delta<Y<b_\delta)=\mu+\delta.
\]
If \(F\) is continuous and strictly increasing on its support, then for every
\(\delta\) strictly between \(M_c(0)-\mu\) and
\(M_c(1-c)-\mu\), its unique interior quantile-window index solves
\[
M_c(u_\delta)=\mu+\delta.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[Profiled identification of the interior target]
\label{prop:profiled-mean-tilt}
Suppose that the support closure is an interval (possibly with infinite
endpoints), \(F\) is absolutely continuous and strictly increasing between its
support endpoints, its density is positive and continuous on the support
interior, and \(\E(Y^2)<\infty\). Extend \(F\) by zero and one beyond finite
support endpoints. Then, for every finite midpoint \(m\), there is a unique
radius \(h_c(m)>0\) satisfying
\(\Pr\{|Y-m|<h_c(m)\}=c\). Define the profiled risk
\[
\overline R_{c,\delta}(m)
=
\E\{\ell_{c,\delta}(m,h_c(m);Y)\}.
\]
Let \(u_c(m)=F\{m-h_c(m)\}\), with \(u_c(m)=0\) or \(1-c\) when the
corresponding root extends beyond a finite support endpoint. Then
\[
\overline R_{c,\delta}'(m)
=
2c\left[M_c\{u_c(m)\}-\mu-\delta\right]
\]
where the derivative exists, with the analogous one-sided statement at a
support transition. Consequently, for
\[
M_c(0)-\mu<\delta<M_c(1-c)-\mu,
\]
the expected mean-tilted loss has a unique global minimizer over finite ordered
roots. Its index \(u_\delta\in(0,1-c)\) is the unique solution of
\[
M_c(u_\delta)=\mu+\delta.
\]
Nondegeneracy gives \(M_c(0)<\mu<M_c(1-c)\), so ordinary RQR is the interior
special case \(\delta=0\). Exchanging the two raw root labels leaves the target
unchanged.
\end{theorem}

The supplement proves existence and uniqueness of the radius, the envelope
derivative, and the one-sided behavior when a root crosses a finite support
endpoint. Finite second moments make both endpoint tilt values finite, even
when a support endpoint is infinite. At an endpoint tilt, the canonical
interval is the corresponding one-sided probability-window limit. An infinite
support endpoint gives a semi-infinite interval that finite root coefficients
cannot attain, although the limiting population-risk infimum is finite. At a
finite support endpoint, roots beyond the support are inactive, so the loss
does not identify a unique root representation without an additional support
constraint. Outside the closed admissible tilt range, the unrestricted risk is
unbounded below along the corresponding escaping-root direction. A proper
Gaussian prior can nevertheless yield a proper finite-sample generalized
posterior.

\begin{corollary}[Recovery tilts for familiar interval functionals]
\label{cor:recovery-tilts}
Ordinary RQR is recovered by \(\delta_{\mathrm{RQR}}=0\). Define
\[
\mathcal U_{\mathrm{SH}}
=
\argmin_{0\le u\le1-c}W_c(u),\qquad
\Delta_{\mathrm{SH}}
=
\{M_c(u)-\mu:u\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{SH}}\}.
\]
The population recovery tilt for the equal-tailed interval is
\[
\delta_{\mathrm{ET}}=M_c\{(1-c)/2\}-\mu.
\]
Each \(u\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{SH}}\) has recovery tilt
\(\delta_{\mathrm{SH}}(u)=M_c(u)-\mu\). Singular notation is used only when the
shortest-contiguous window is unique or one minimizer has been selected.
\end{corollary}

At regular interior points where the endpoint quantiles are differentiable
and have positive densities,
\[
\frac{du_\delta}{d\delta}=\frac{c}{W_c(u_\delta)},\qquad
\frac{dW_c(u_\delta)}{d\delta}
=\frac{c\,W_c'(u_\delta)}{W_c(u_\delta)}.
\]
Thus an interior width minimum along the tilt path satisfies the usual
equal-endpoint-density condition.
Figure~\ref{fig:mean-tilt-map} visualizes the same recovery map across
symmetric and skewed population laws before adding any finite-sample
approximation layer.

For initialization and external tilt screening, the same identities suggest a
simple first-order approximation. Let \(\gamma_1\) denote standardized
skewness and set
\[
q_c=\Phi^{-1}\{(1+c)/2\},\qquad K_c=q_c\phi(q_c)/c.
\]
A Cornish--Fisher expansion around a standardized Normal law
\citep{CornishFisher1937} gives the
shortest-oriented and equal-tailed standardized tilt anchors
\[
d_{\mathrm{SH}}^{\mathrm{CF}}=-\gamma_1K_c,\qquad
d_{\mathrm{ET}}^{\mathrm{CF}}=\frac{1}{3}d_{\mathrm{SH}}^{\mathrm{CF}},
\qquad d=\delta/\operatorname{SD}(Y).
\]
These formulas are not new interval targets; they are local skewness-based
approximations to the population recovery tilts. Right skewness gives a
negative shortest-oriented tilt, while left skewness gives a positive tilt.
They supply cheap, interpretable starting anchors for tilt exploration, not
automatic shortest-interval estimators. Figure~\ref{fig:mean-tilt-cf-anchors}
places the two approximations on the exact population tilt path using true
population skewness rather than a sample estimate.

Table~\ref{tab:mean-tilt-cf-mini-study} shows why the approximation should
remain an anchor rather than a target definition. It is exact at symmetry and
close for the near-Normal Gamma laws. Its errors are larger for lognormal and
bounded Beta shapes and for the exponential law, whose shortest interval is a
support-boundary solution. The reflected Beta rows verify the expected sign
reversal but also show that skewness alone does not determine approximation
accuracy. Across these examples the ET anchor is more accurate than the SH
anchor, but that comparison is descriptive rather than a general ordering.
Diagnostic code therefore records moment shape,
probability-window boundary behavior, and bootstrap sensitivity whenever a
sample plug-in anchor is constructed.

The recovery tilts above are population quantities. A universal tilt does not
recover the same interval functional across distributions, and conditional
recovery generally requires a covariate-dependent \(\delta(x)\). The tilt is
therefore fixed or selected in a separate training/validation layer; sampling
it as an ordinary parameter would mix distinct interval targets. The target is
equivariant under positive affine changes of response scale when \(\delta\)
and the fixed learning rate are transformed accordingly, but it is not
invariant to arbitrary monotone response transformations. Fixed nonzero tilt
is currently supported for fixed-rate ridge regression and RQR-DESN readouts,
and for RQR-DLM with fixed evolution covariance or a frozen discount template.
Other prior, learning-rate, and evolution-mode combinations require separate
propriety arguments and validation. Data-driven tilt selection likewise
requires an external protocol before empirical claims are made.

The population score equations do not by themselves establish propriety of a
finite-sample generalized posterior under an arbitrary root prior. With a
fixed learning rate, a finite design or state horizon, and proper Gaussian
root priors, the tilt is linear in the root parameters and the Gaussian tails
make the generalized posterior proper. This argument covers ridge regression
and Gaussian state models. Heavier-tailed shrinkage priors require a separate
propriety analysis before nonzero tilt is enabled.

\section{Scan Calibration and a Proposed Tolerance Action}
\label{sec:tcsp}

The preceding theory fixes population content and interval placement. A
tolerance interval adds a repeated-sampling requirement. For iid continuous
univariate observations, the target statement is
\[
\Pr_F^n\{C_F(\widehat T_n)\ge c\}\ge 1-\alpha,\qquad
C_F([L,U])=F(U)-F(L),
\]
where \(\widehat T_n\) is a data-dependent interval. Five quantities must be
kept distinct:
\[
c \text{ is the required content},\quad
1-\alpha \text{ is the tolerance confidence},\quad
q=k/n \text{ is the fitted content},
\]
\[
\delta \text{ is the fixed MT-RQR placement tilt},\qquad
\omega_{\mathrm R} \text{ is the generalized-Bayes learning rate}.
\]
Neither \(\omega_{\mathrm R}\) nor a posterior credible level determines the
tolerance confidence.

Let \(Y_{(1)}\le\cdots\le Y_{(n)}\). For content \(c\), define the uniform scan
statistic
\[
M_n(c)=\max_{0\le s\le 1-c}\#\{i:U_i\in[s,s+c]\},
\qquad U_i\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\mathrm{Unif}(0,1).
\]
The calibrated retained count is
\[
k_{n,c,\alpha}
=\min\{k:\Pr(M_n(c)<k)\ge 1-\alpha\}.
\]
The formal action is the canonical closed order-statistic window
\[
\widehat T_n=
[Y_{(\widehat j)},Y_{(\widehat j+k-1)}],
\qquad
\widehat j=\min\argmin_{1\le j\le n-k+1}
\{Y_{(j+k-1)}-Y_{(j)}\},
\]
with \(k=k_{n,c,\alpha}\). This convention is closed and contains exactly
\(k\) sample observations under continuity. It differs from the half-open
\((Y_{(j)},Y_{(j+k)}]\) convention sometimes used for order-statistic
tolerance limits, so a result proved for one convention cannot be transferred
without reconciling the indices and endpoint rule. The fitted MT-RQR content
and its content buffer are
\[
q_{n,c,\alpha}=k_{n,c,\alpha}/n,\qquad
t_{n,c,\alpha}=q_{n,c,\alpha}-c.
\]
The empirical shortest-path tilt is
\[
\widehat\delta_{\mathrm{SH}}(q)
=
\frac1k\sum_{\ell=\widehat j}^{\widehat j+k-1}Y_{(\ell)}
-\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nY_i.
\]
After fixing \(q\) and \(\widehat\delta_{\mathrm{SH}}\), the MT-RQR update is
\[
\Pi_{q,\delta,\omega_{\mathrm R}}(d\theta\mid D_n)
\propto
\exp\{-\omega_{\mathrm R}L_{q,\delta,n}(\theta)\}\Pi_0(d\theta).
\]
This generalized posterior quantifies uncertainty about a fixed interval-root
functional. It is neither the tolerance certificate nor a response model.
The formal tolerance action remains the empirical order-statistic window
\(\widehat T_n\). Because \(\widehat\delta_{\mathrm{SH}}\) is computed from
the same sample, these posterior summaries are conditional on the selected
target and do not propagate shortest-window selection uncertainty. An
unconditional interpretation requires a plug-in stability theorem or a
separate target-selection sample.

A sample-split alternative removes this same-sample placement issue for the
univariate tolerance action. Randomly divide the observations into an
independent pilot sample and an independent main sample. The pilot chooses a
lower-tail placement \(\widehat u\). In the main sample, with order statistics
\(Z_{(1)}\le\cdots\le Z_{(N)}\), fix indices \(r<s\) after the pilot and write
\(d=s-r\). The spacing law
\[
F(Z_{(s)})-F(Z_{(r)})\sim \mathrm{Beta}(d,N+1-d)
\]
then gives an exact conditional distribution-free calibration
\citep{DavidNagaraja2003OrderStatistics}. The closed interval contains
\(d+1\) observed main-sample positions, so the probability-spacing index and
the closed-count index differ by one. The pilot may use empirical shortest
windows, Cornish--Fisher placement, or fixed-target ECM as a diagnostic
placement engine. It affects efficiency and width, not the conditional Beta
validity of the fixed main-sample spacing. This first split construction is
continuous-iid-univariate; regression-family tolerance calibration remains a
separate theorem.

Numerical evaluation of \(k_{n,c,\alpha}\) is consequential because it sets the
reported interval. The numerical procedure uses Monte Carlo calibration of
the uniform scan distribution with a simultaneous
Massart--DKW empirical-CDF lower band for \(\Pr\{M_n(c)<k\}\) across all
candidate retained counts, together with a conservative Massart--DKW fallback
\citep{Massart1990DKW}. The fallback is not an exact scan recursion.
Accordingly, we treat exact critical-count
calculation and the finite-sample theorem for this closed-window action as
open proof and numerical-certification tasks rather than as established
results.

Table~\ref{tab:tcsp-theory-scope} separates established MT-RQR results from
the claims that still require an action-matched proof. This separation allows
the empirical action, numerical calibration, and conditional generalized
posterior to be studied without treating implementation as a mathematical
guarantee.

The population shortest-window path gives a useful geometry for moving across
contents. Suppose that the density is differentiable and strictly unimodal,
and that the shortest window
\(I_q=[L_q,U_q]=[Q(u_q),Q(u_q+q)]\) is unique and interior over a range of
\(q\). Equal endpoint densities then satisfy
\[
f(L_q)=f(U_q)=\lambda_q.
\]
Writing \(a_q=f'(L_q)>0\) and \(b_q=f'(U_q)<0\), formal differentiation of
the content and equal-density equations gives
\[
L_q'=\frac{b_q}{\lambda_q(a_q-b_q)}<0,
\qquad
U_q'=\frac{a_q}{\lambda_q(a_q-b_q)}>0.
\]
Thus the first-order shares of added probability assigned to the left and
right boundaries are
\[
p_L(q)=\frac{-b_q}{a_q-b_q},\qquad
p_R(q)=\frac{a_q}{a_q-b_q},\qquad
p_L(q)+p_R(q)=1.
\]
Only under local symmetry are the two shares equal to one half. The width
satisfies
\[
W_{\mathrm{SH}}'(q)=\frac{1}{\lambda_q},
\qquad
W_{\mathrm{SH}}''(q)
=-\frac{a_qb_q}{\lambda_q^3(a_q-b_q)}>0.
\]
Hence additional content becomes increasingly expensive as the endpoints move
into lower-density regions. If
\(m_q=q^{-1}\int_{u_q}^{u_q+q}Q(s)\,ds
=\mu+\delta_{\mathrm{SH}}(q)\), then
\[
\delta_{\mathrm{SH}}'(q)
=\frac{p_L(q)L_q+p_R(q)U_q-m_q}{q}.
\]
These derivative calculations motivate numerical continuation and diagnose
its behavior. Full regularity, boundary, and nonuniqueness arguments are
reserved for a formal shortest-path theorem.

The path algorithm starts from a globally solved content, predicts each next
window by nested expansion of the previous window, applies local correction in
a trust region, expands the region when the local optimum lands on its
boundary, and finally verifies the formal action by a global scan. The global
check, not the local predictor, defines the selected window. Failure modes
include infeasible calibration
\((k>n)\), ties or atoms that violate the continuous-sample convention,
nonunique shortest windows, response-scale dependence of geometric width, and
unsupported attempts to attach the scan certificate to posterior summaries.

The construction therefore retains two distinct outputs: an empirical
tolerance action and a fixed-target generalized posterior. Finite-sample scan
validity requires an action-matched proof and certified critical count;
first-order tolerance or oracle-width statements additionally require
same-sample shortest-window asymptotics. Any transfer to posterior mean or
median endpoints requires a separate action-equivalence or direct-calibration
result. Posterior credibility is not tolerance confidence, and
\(\omega_{\mathrm R}\) is not a tolerance factor.

\section{Finite-Sample Balance and Static Uncertainty}
\label{sec:static-theory-scope}

The population identities do not imply smooth finite-sample score equations:
fitted endpoints depend on the observations, and the empirical ordinary-RQR
loss is nonsmooth when an observation lies on a root. The corresponding
finite-sample result is fractional. For intercept-only MT-RQR with fixed
\(\delta\), let
\((\widehat a,\widehat b)\), \(\widehat a<\widehat b\), be an interior
subgradient-stationary empirical solution. Then there exist allocations
\(\widehat q_i\in[0,1]\), equal to one for observations strictly inside the
fitted interval, zero for observations strictly outside it, and fractional
only at fitted roots, such that
\[
\sum_{i=1}^n \widehat q_i=nc,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^n \widehat q_i y_i
=nc(\overline y+\delta).
\]
Thus the training interval has exact fractional content and exact fractional
retained-mean balance. This is a score identity for the fitted training
sample, not an exact future-coverage statement. If \(B_n\) observations lie
on fitted roots, then
\[
P_n(\widehat a<Y<\widehat b)\le c\le
P_n(\widehat a\le Y\le\widehat b),
\qquad
\left|P_n(\widehat a<Y<\widehat b)-c\right|\le B_n/n.
\]
In the iid intercept-only case, this combines with the
Dvoretzky--Kiefer--Wolfowitz inequality to give a conservative
high-probability population-content bound; the supplement gives the explicit
display. Regression fits satisfy analogous design-weighted score equations;
when a global ordering is valid, their coverage component contains
\(\sum_i X_i\widehat w_i(c-\widehat q_i)=0\), with
\(\widehat w_i=\widehat U_i-\widehat L_i\). This is width-weighted
calibration in the chosen design span, not pointwise conditional coverage.

A separate static large-sample theory concerns iid random-design observations
\(Z_i=(X_i,Y_i)\), fixed coefficient dimension, fixed \(c\), fixed
\(\delta(X)\), and fixed \(\omega_{\mathrm R}\). It assumes a globally
labelable ordered chart with uniformly separated roots
\(a_0(X)=X^\top\beta_{L0}<b_0(X)=X^\top\beta_{U0}\), positive endpoint
densities, a unique population target, positive-definite curvature, and a
proper prior positive near the target. Pointwise sorting of crossing raw
linear roots is not covered by this ordered-chart theorem; nor are selected
DESN reservoirs, deterministic fixed-design triangular arrays, dependent time
series, growing feature dimension, learned \(\kappa\), or same-sample tilt
selection.

Under these static regularity conditions, write
\(\theta_0=(\beta_{L0}^\top,\beta_{U0}^\top)^\top\). Let \(H\) be the
expected-risk Hessian and \(J=\operatorname{Var}\{\psi_0(Z)\}\) the variance
of the endpoint score vector; the supplement records their block forms. The
empirical minimizer has asymptotic sandwich covariance
\[
\Sigma_F=H^{-1}JH^{-1},
\]
whereas the fixed-rate generalized posterior has local curvature covariance
\[
\Sigma_G=(\omega_{\mathrm R}H)^{-1}.
\]
Consequently a scalar learning rate reproduces the full joint endpoint
covariance only under the information-proportionality condition
\(J=\omega_{\mathrm R}^{-1}H\). When consistent estimators
\(\widehat H\) and \(\widehat J\) are available and \(H,J\) are positive
definite, an open-faced-sandwich transformation
\[
\theta^{\mathrm{adj}}
=\widehat\theta+
\widehat\Sigma_F^{1/2}\widehat\Sigma_G^{-1/2}
(\theta-\widehat\theta)
\]
calibrates static coefficient and differentiable endpoint-function summaries
to first order. This is a post-processing calibration of a generalized
posterior, not a response likelihood and not a proof of dynamic or DESN
forecast calibration.

\subsection{Static target and model-family scope}

Let \(\mathcal O\) index the observed rows of
\(\mat X\in\R^{n\times p}\), with \(n_{\mathrm{obs}}=|\mathcal O|\), and
define two raw-root readouts
\[
\vect\eta_k=\mat X\vect\beta_k,\qquad k=1,2.
\]
Pointwise reporting uses
\[
L_i=\min(\eta_{1i},\eta_{2i}),\quad
U_i=\max(\eta_{1i},\eta_{2i}),\quad
M_i=(L_i+U_i)/2,\quad W_i=U_i-L_i.
\]
These ordered summaries are posterior functionals. The static asymptotic
result above is narrower: it assumes one globally labelable, uniformly
separated endpoint ordering.
When the two root priors are exchangeable, the raw labels \(k=1,2\) are not
identified. The sampler may therefore apply a complete-root label swap as an
acceptance-one symmetry move; this exchanges all root-specific coefficient and
prior-state variables but does not assign lower- or upper-endpoint meaning to
the raw labels. Coefficient-level lower/upper summaries are reported only as a
post-processing chart on a declared reference design, and only when one complete
root block is separated below the other across that design. If the fitted
linear roots cross on the reference domain or the keep/swap assignment is
ambiguous, the canonical coefficient chart is not reported; pointwise ordered
endpoints remain well defined.

For each observed row, define
\[
e_i=(y_i-\eta_{1i})(y_i-\eta_{2i}),\qquad
\ell_{c,\delta_i}(\eta_{1i},\eta_{2i};y_i)
=\rho_c(e_i)-c\delta_i(\eta_{1i}+\eta_{2i}-2y_i).
\]
For priors \(\pi_1,\pi_2\), a fixed externally declared
\(\vect\delta\), and a fixed learning rate \(\omega_{\mathrm R}>0\), the
generalized posterior is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\Pi_{\vect\delta}
(d\vect\beta_1,d\vect\beta_2\mid\vect y,\mat X)
\propto{}&
\pi_1(d\vect\beta_1)\pi_2(d\vect\beta_2)\\
&\times\exp\left\{-\omega_{\mathrm R}
\sum_{i\in\mathcal O}
\ell_{c,\delta_i}(\eta_{1i},\eta_{2i};y_i)\right\},
\end{split}
\label{eq:fixed-mt-target}
\end{equation}
Thus the prior is updated by the cumulative interval loss. At
\(\delta_i=0\), each contribution is the nonnegative ordinary-RQR loss in
\eqref{eq:rqr-loss-piecewise}--\eqref{eq:rqr-check-representation}.
Equation~\eqref{eq:fixed-mt-target} is a generalized posterior, not one derived
from a response likelihood. The tilt indexes the interval functional and is
fixed before posterior computation rather than sampled as a response
parameter.
Nonzero tilt uses a fixed learning rate and a proper Gaussian prior,
including a ridge prior,
in the present derivation.

\subsection{Latent-variable augmentation and Gaussian full conditionals}
\label{sec:gibbs}

Let
\[
r_i=y_i^2,\qquad
m_i^{\mathrm R}=y_i(\eta_{1i}+\eta_{2i})-\eta_{1i}\eta_{2i}.
\]
Then \(r_i-m_i^{\mathrm R}=e_i\). With
\[
\sigma_{\mathrm R}=\omega_{\mathrm R}^{-1},\qquad
\xi_c=\frac{1-2c}{c(1-c)},\qquad
\phi_c=\frac{2}{c(1-c)},
\]
the single-observation factor in the generalized posterior kernel is
proportional to
\[
\int_0^\infty
\Normal\!\left(
r_i\mid m_i^{\mathrm R}+\xi_c v_i,\,
\phi_c\sigma_{\mathrm R}v_i
\right)
\Exp(v_i\mid \text{rate}=1/\sigma_{\mathrm R})\,dv_i .
\]
This identity defines a latent-variable augmentation of that generalized
posterior factor. It is not a sampling model for \(y_i\). Under the
three-parameter convention
\[
\GIG(p,a,b):\quad
p(v)\propto v^{p-1}\exp\{-(av+b/v)/2\},
\]
the observed latent scales have the following full conditional distributions
under the augmented generalized posterior:
\[
v_i\mid\cdot\sim
\GIG\left(
\frac12,\,
\frac{\omega_{\mathrm R}}{2c(1-c)},\,
\frac{\omega_{\mathrm R}c(1-c)e_i^2}{2}
\right),
\qquad i\in\mathcal O.
\]
The ordinary learned-scale convention instead sets
\(\sigma_{\mathrm R}=s_L/\kappa\) and follows the separate partially collapsed
order stated in the following subsection and in the supplement.

Conditional on \(\vect\beta_2\), the pseudo-observation equation is linear in
\(\vect\beta_1\):
\[
\mat A_1=\diag(\vect y_{\mathcal O}-\vect\eta_{2,\mathcal O})
\mat X_{\mathcal O},\qquad
\vect z_1=\vect y_{\mathcal O}\circ
(\vect y_{\mathcal O}-\vect\eta_{2,\mathcal O})
-\xi_c\vect v_{\mathcal O}.
\]
With
\(\mat W_v=\diag\{(\phi_c\sigma_{\mathrm R}v_i)^{-1}:i\in\mathcal O\}\)
and Gaussian prior
canonical parameters \((\mat Q_{01},\vect h_{01})\),
\[
\mat Q_1=\mat Q_{01}+\mat A_1^\top\mat W_v\mat A_1,\qquad
\vect h_1=\vect h_{01}+\mat A_1^\top\mat W_v\vect z_1
+\omega_{\mathrm R}c\mat X_{\mathcal O}^{\top}\vect\delta_{\mathcal O},
\]
so the Gaussian full conditional is
\[
\vect\beta_1\mid\cdot\sim
\Normal(\mat Q_1^{-1}\vect h_1,\mat Q_1^{-1}).
\]
The update for \(\vect\beta_2\) is identical after swapping root labels and
conditioning on the newly drawn first root. The tilt changes only the two
Gaussian information vectors: the latent-scale conditionals and Gaussian
precision matrices are unchanged.

For a deterministic fixed-target mode, the same augmentation gives an ECM
algorithm. At the current roots, for nonzero residual product \(e_i\),
\[
\tau_i=E(v_i^{-1}\mid\cdot)=\frac{1}{c(1-c)|e_i|}.
\]
Holding the second root fixed, let
\[
\mat A_1=\diag(\vect y_{\mathcal O}-\vect\eta_{2,\mathcal O})
\mat X_{\mathcal O},\qquad
\vect c_1=\vect y_{\mathcal O}\circ
(\vect y_{\mathcal O}-\vect\eta_{2,\mathcal O}).
\]
Replacing \(v_i^{-1}\) by \(\tau_i\) in the expected complete-data criterion
gives a Gaussian conditional maximization with precision
\[
\mat Q_1^{\mathrm{ECM}}
=\mat Q_{01}
+\frac{1}{\phi_c\sigma_{\mathrm R}}
\mat A_1^\top\diag(\vect\tau)\mat A_1
\]
and canonical vector
\[
\vect h_1^{\mathrm{ECM}}
=\vect h_{01}
+\frac{1}{\phi_c\sigma_{\mathrm R}}
\mat A_1^\top\{\diag(\vect\tau)\vect c_1-\xi_c\vect 1\}
+\omega_{\mathrm R}c\mat X_{\mathcal O}^\top\vect\delta_{\mathcal O}.
\]
The first conditional maximization sets
\(\vect\beta_1=(\mat Q_1^{\mathrm{ECM}})^{-1}\vect h_1^{\mathrm{ECM}}\);
the second uses the updated first root and the same outer E-step
\(\vect\tau\). This is ECM rather than a single EM maximization because the
augmented pseudo-residual is quartic jointly in the two root blocks but
quadratic in either block conditional on the other. The implemented algorithm
uses a declared residual-product safeguard at \(e_i=0\), exact observed
objective backtracking, and deterministic multi-start selection. Its endpoint
output is a fixed-target mode summary and is not a tolerance certificate.

\subsection{Regularized coefficient blocks and the static Gibbs sweep}

The fixed-design calculation also supports regularized ordinary RQR. Let
\(\vect\psi_k\) denote a root-specific prior state and suppose that,
conditional on this state, root \(k\) has Gaussian canonical parameters
\(\{\mat Q_{0k}(\vect\psi_k),\vect h_{0k}(\vect\psi_k)\}\). The ordinary-RQR
MCMC adapter supports RHS-NS
\citep{CarvalhoPolsonScott2010HS,PiironenVehtari2017RHS,
NishimuraSuchard2023SSS}. As an input requirement for this branch, the first
design column must be the declared intercept and is excluded from shrinkage.
For every remaining coefficient \(j\), the conditional prior precision is
\[
d_{kj}
=\frac{1}{\tau_k^2\lambda_{kj}^2}+\frac{1}{\zeta_k^2},
\]
with a separately declared Gaussian precision for the unshrunk intercept.
The local, global, auxiliary, and shoulder-scale quantities in
\(\vect\psi_k\) are updated after the corresponding coefficient draw. The two
roots carry distinct, identically specified prior states, and a root-label
swap moves \((\vect\beta_k,\vect\psi_k)\) as one complete block.

This conditionally Gaussian adapter reuses the external RHS-NS hierarchy
rather than rederiving its marginal prior. The RQR interface names this option
\(\text{RHS-NS}\), rather than a generic \(\mathrm{RHS}\) mode. It is
available only for ordinary RQR, \(\vect\delta=\vect 0\). The Gaussian
moment-generating-function argument that establishes fixed-rate nonzero-tilt
propriety does not establish the same result after random-scale mixing.

\begin{algorithmblock}{Static fixed-rate RQR/MT-RQR root-blocked Gibbs sampler}
\label{alg:fixed-design-gibbs}
\item \textbf{Specify the generalized posterior.} Fix
\(\vect y,\mat X,\mathcal O,c,\omega_{\mathrm R}\), the target tilt
\(\vect\delta_{\mathcal O}\), proper root priors, and the label-swap rule.
Missing rows contribute no generalized-posterior factor, latent scale, or tilt
term.
\item \textbf{Initialize.} Choose finite
\(\vect\beta_1,\vect\beta_2\), positive \(v_i\) for \(i\in\mathcal O\), and
valid root-specific prior states when the ordinary RHS-NS adapter is used.
\item \textbf{Sweep.} For each iteration:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=1.8em,itemsep=0.15em,topsep=0.15em]
\item compute \(e_i\) and draw every observed \(v_i\) from its GIG full
conditional;
\item draw \(\vect\beta_1\) from its Gaussian full conditional given
\(\vect\beta_2\), with tilt shift
\(\omega_{\mathrm R}c\mat X_{\mathcal O}^{\top}
\vect\delta_{\mathcal O}\);
\item update root 1's ordinary RHS-NS prior state when that adapter is active;
\item rebuild the system and draw \(\vect\beta_2\) conditionally on the new
\(\vect\beta_1\), using the same tilt shift;
\item update root 2's ordinary RHS-NS prior state when active; and
\item when the two complete root blocks are exchangeable, apply the
acceptance-one label swap.
\end{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Report ordered functionals.} Store ordered endpoints, widths,
midpoints, losses, raw root-label diagnostics, and target metadata. Optional
canonical coefficient summaries are post-processing outputs and require a
passed global labelability check on the declared reference design. The sampler leaves the
specified fixed-rate proper-Gaussian generalized posterior invariant; at
\(\vect\delta=\vect 0\) it
reduces to ordinary RQR. The ordinary learned-\(\kappa\) sweep and RHS-NS
applicability details are given in the supplement.
\end{algorithmblock}

At \(\vect\delta=\vect 0\), Algorithm~\ref{alg:fixed-design-gibbs} is the
fixed-rate ordinary-RQR Gibbs sampler. The RHS-NS adapter then updates the two
root-specific prior states separately. It can also be used with the ordinary
learned-\(\kappa\) generalized posterior and the partial-collapse order below.
Neither use establishes fixed- or learned-rate propriety at nonzero tilt.

\subsection{Frozen-feature DESN specialization}
\label{sec:desn}

Let \(\mat X_{\mathrm{DESN}}\) be the complete deterministic feature design
produced by a deep echo-state network, including any declared intercept column,
with fixed and recorded reservoir weights, scaling, washout, preprocessing,
tuning decisions, and random seeds
\citep{Jaeger2001EchoState,GallicchioMicheliPedrelli2018DeepESNDesign,
McDermottWikle2018DeepESNUQ}. Substituting
\(\mat X=\mat X_{\mathrm{DESN}}\) in
Algorithm~\ref{alg:fixed-design-gibbs} defines the RQR-DESN readout. It uses the
same generalized posterior and Gibbs sampler. A ridge readout supports the
fixed-rate nonzero-tilt Gaussian full conditionals implemented through the
static sampler;
the RHS-NS readout and matched evidence concern ordinary RQR only.

Future root functionals use a frozen future design
\(\mat X_{\mathrm{DESN,fut}}\) constructed under the same recorded reservoir
contract, without using held-out responses to refit its features. Ordered
endpoints, midpoints, and widths are functions of the readout draws. No
response-simulation distribution is implied, and reservoir or feature
selection occurs outside the generalized-posterior update. This computational
equivalence to a fixed static design does not, by itself, establish the iid
random-design asymptotic theory for serially dependent DESN rows or for
data-selected reservoir features.

\subsection{Ordinary-RQR learned inverse-loss scale}

A separate ordinary-only convention uses a positive reference loss scale
\(s_L\), sets \(\sigma_{\mathrm R}=s_L/\kappa\), writes
\(\mathcal L_{n,c}(\vartheta)=
\sum_{i\in\mathcal O}\rho_c(e_i)\), and declares
\[
\Pi_{\mathrm R}(d\vartheta,d\kappa\mid\vect y)
\propto
\pi(d\vartheta)\kappa^{n_{\mathrm{obs}}}\pi_\kappa(d\kappa)
\exp\{-\kappa\mathcal L_{n,c}(\vartheta)/s_L\},
\]
the \emph{pseudo-residual-normalized learned-scale generalized posterior}.
The factor
\(\kappa^{n_{\mathrm{obs}}}\) arises from normalizing an asymmetric-Laplace
kernel in the abstract pseudo-residual \(e_i\); it does not normalize a
response density in \(y_i\). Here \(\vartheta\) collects both roots and, when
present, their proper root-specific prior or evolution states. If
\(\kappa\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(a_\kappa,b_\kappa)\) in shape--rate form, its
collapsed full conditional is
\[
\kappa\mid\vartheta,\vect y
\sim\mathrm{Gamma}\left(a_\kappa+n_{\mathrm{obs}},
b_\kappa+\mathcal L_{n,c}(\vartheta)/s_L\right).
\]
The factor \(\kappa^{n_{\mathrm{obs}}}\) is part of this generalized posterior
rather than an irrelevant constant when \(\kappa\) is learned. A pure-loss
sensitivity generalized posterior that omits this factor is kept separate.

The learned \(\kappa\) is part of this hierarchical generalized posterior.
Coherence for a fixed loss scale does not establish that \(\kappa\) is
an ordinary jointly learnable response parameter or that its generalized
posterior calibrates endpoint uncertainty
\citep{LeeLiuNicholls2025LearningRate,WuMartin2023LearningRateComparison}.
We therefore use fixed-rate sensitivity analyses as the primary specification
and treat learned-scale inference as an additional convention. When \(s_L\) is
computed from training responses, its calculation sample and variance
convention are recorded and \(s_L\) is frozen before simulation; the resulting
generalized posterior is data dependent in an empirical-Bayes sense.

This hierarchical generalized posterior can be paired only with an otherwise
valid ordinary fixed-design or fixed-joint root model. Its partially collapsed
Gibbs sampler draws \(\kappa\),
immediately refreshes every observed latent scale at the new value, and only
then updates roots 1 and 2; Supplement Algorithm~S1 gives the complete order.
For any proper ordinary root/prior-state law \(\pi(\vartheta)\), joint
integrability follows because the loss is nonnegative,
\(\exp\{-\kappa\mathcal L_{n,c}(\vartheta)/s_L\}\leq1\), and the Gamma prior
has a finite \(n_{\mathrm{obs}}\)th moment. This covers the declared ordinary
RHS-NS adapter while preserving its complete prior-state updates.
The mean-tilted loss may be negative, and a proper normalized joint generalized
posterior with random rate has not been established. The Gamma full conditional
above must not be copied to nonzero tilt.

\section{Dynamic Linear Root States}
\label{sec:dynamic}

The MT-RQR-DLM represents each raw root by a linear Gaussian state trajectory,
\[
\eta_{kt}=\vect f_t^\top\vect\theta_{kt},\qquad
\vect\theta_{k0}\sim\Normal(\vect m_0,\mat C_0),\qquad
\vect\theta_{kt}=\mat G_t\vect\theta_{k,t-1}+\vect\epsilon_{kt},
\qquad \vect\epsilon_{kt}\sim\Normal(\vect 0,\mat W_t),
\qquad k=1,2.
\]
The two trajectories can be written as one stacked state with block-diagonal
evolution matrices. That representation does not yield one Gaussian FFBS
update. Jointly in both root states, the augmented generalized posterior
contains the square of
\[
(y_t-\vect f_t^\top\vect\theta_{1t})
(y_t-\vect f_t^\top\vect\theta_{2t}),
\]
and hence fourth-order cross-terms. Conditional on either complete root path,
the same expression is linear in the other path. Partitioning the stacked
state by root and drawing the paths sequentially therefore gives exact
full-conditional updates for the specified fixed-joint generalized posterior.
The resulting computational concern is Markov-chain mixing, not approximation
of either full conditional.

For \(t\in\mathcal O\), conditional on the second root path and the RQR latent
scales, the first path has the pseudo-observation system
\[
z_{1t}=y_t(y_t-\eta_{2t})-\xi_c v_t,\qquad
H_{1t}=(y_t-\eta_{2t})\vect f_t^\top,\qquad
V_t=\phi_c\sigma_{\mathrm R}v_t.
\]
The second root is analogous and conditions on the newly drawn first path.
Under fixed-rate MT-RQR, the tilt adds the canonical linear site
\[
\vect g_t=\omega_{\mathrm R}c\delta_t\vect f_t
\]
to each root-state conditional. This information-form update never divides by
\(y_t-\eta_{\mathrm{other},t}\) and remains well defined when that quantity is
zero. Equivalently, if the ordinary
pseudo-observation update at time \(t\) gives mean
\(\vect m_t^{(0)}\) and covariance \(\mat C_t\), the tilted filter uses
\[
\vect m_t=\vect m_t^{(0)}+\mat C_t\vect g_t
\]
and the same \(\mat C_t\). This shifted mean is propagated before the next
update. At a missing response, the loss, \(v_t\), and \(\vect g_t\) are all
absent. In full-path canonical notation the same operation is
\(\vect m_{\mathrm{path}}^{(0)}+\mat Q_{\mathrm{path}}^{-1}\vect g\), which
provides a direct implementation check.

The evolution specification is part of the generalized posterior. Fixed
\(\mat W_t\) gives a Gaussian state prior. A frozen discount template first
maps declared component-specific discounts to a complete
\(\mat W_{1:T}\) sequence through a reference covariance recursion and then
holds that sequence fixed throughout MCMC. Conditional on the frozen template,
alternating root-specific FFBS samples from the exact root-path full
conditionals. A template constructed from training data is recorded as an
empirical-Bayes specification.

\begin{algorithmblock}{Fixed-rate MT-RQR-DLM root-blocked Gibbs sampler}
\label{alg:dynamic-gibbs}
\item \textbf{Specify the joint generalized posterior.} Fix
\((\vect y,\mathcal O)\), \(c,\omega_{\mathrm R}\), \(\delta_{1:T}\),
\(\vect f_{1:T}\), \(\mat G_{1:T}\), proper Gaussian initial laws, and either
fixed \(\mat W_{1:T}\) or one precomputed frozen discount template.
\item \textbf{Initialize.} Choose finite root paths
\(\vect\theta_{1,1:T},\vect\theta_{2,1:T}\) and positive initial latent
scales at observed times. Missing responses contribute no generalized-posterior
factor, latent scale, or tilt term.
\item \textbf{Sweep.} For each iteration:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=1.8em,itemsep=0.15em,topsep=0.15em]
\item compute the current product residual and draw \(v_t\) at every
\(t\in\mathcal O\) from the GIG conditional;
\item conditional on \(\vect\theta_{2,1:T}\), build root 1's scalar Gaussian
pseudo-observation system, add
\(\vect g_t=\omega_{\mathrm R}c\delta_t\vect f_t\) at observed times, and draw
\(\vect\theta_{1,1:T}\) by FFBS;
\item rebuild the system conditional on the new first path and draw the second
path by the analogous tilted FFBS update; and
\item when all root-state priors and evolution contracts are exchangeable,
apply the acceptance-one complete-root label swap.
\end{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Report root functionals.} Retain ordered time-local endpoints,
widths, midpoints, selected state functionals, losses, and complete
target/evolution metadata. These are not simulated responses. If a dynamic
lower/upper label is requested beyond pointwise ordered endpoints, the
post-processing decision is one complete path assignment per retained draw,
not a separate relabeling at each time. The sampler targets the exact
generalized posterior for fixed \(W_t\) and frozen-template fixed-joint modes;
adaptive discounts and ordinary shared
component scales are separate extensions described in the supplement.
\end{algorithmblock}

At \(\delta_{1:T}=0\), Algorithm~\ref{alg:dynamic-gibbs} reduces exactly to
the fixed-rate ordinary-RQR-DLM sampler for fixed \(\mat W_t\) or a
frozen template. A learned-rate tilted generalized posterior has not been
normalized and cannot be obtained by inserting the ordinary Gamma full
conditional. Future-root propagation
uses a separate frozen forecast contract with recorded future
\(\vect f_t,\mat G_t,\mat W_t\); it neither changes the training Gibbs sweep
nor defines response simulation.

\subsection{Ordinary-only evolution variants}

For ordinary RQR, an exact component-specific alternative
partitions the state into blocks and sets
\[
\mat W_t(\vect q)=\operatorname{blockdiag}
\{q_1\mat Q_{1t},\ldots,q_J\mat Q_{Jt}\},
\qquad q_j\sim\operatorname{IG}(a_j,b_j),
\]
where the \(\mat Q_{jt}\) are fixed positive-definite templates and the
multipliers are shared across the two roots to preserve exchangeability. The
ordinary component-scale sampler uses rootwise partial collapse
and centered/noncentered interweaving; the supplement gives the invariant
transition and update order
\citep{PapaspiliopoulosRobertsSkold2007,YuMeng2011ASIS}. This extension is
not silently generalized to nonzero tilt. In the noncentered parameterization
the tilt depends on \(\sqrt{q_j}\) through the reconstructed state path, so a
tilted component-scale generalized posterior requires separate normalization,
propriety, and validation. Likewise, recomputing \(\mat W_t\) from each
changing conditional filter covariance is retained only as an experimental
working update, not as
an exact Gibbs sampler for a common joint generalized posterior.

\section{Computational Illustrations and Evaluation Scope}
\label{sec:evaluation}

Population identities, sampler validation, and comparative forecasting
evidence answer different questions. Deterministic calculations verify the
fixed-content and mean-tilt characterizations without Monte Carlo error. Small
reference problems compare generalized-posterior draws with quadrature, dense
Gaussian full conditionals, analytic scale updates, and exact checkpoint
continuation. Agreement on these fixtures validates the stated computational
target; it does not establish repeated-sample coverage or comparative
forecasting performance.

Figures~\ref{fig:oracle-tilt-fixed-design-fit}
and~\ref{fig:oracle-tilt-dlm-fit} illustrate the fixed-target construction in
a static readout and a dynamic root model. The population law and recovery
tilts are fixed before fitting. In both examples,
innovations follow an affinely standardized asymmetric-Laplace law with source
index \(0.80\), producing pronounced left skewness while retaining adequate
information in both endpoint tails. The three recovery tilts are computed
directly as the conditional retained mean of each population window minus the
population mean. The resulting innovation-scale tilts are \(0\), \(0.0561\),
and \(0.1147\) for RQR, ET, and SH, respectively; no Cornish--Fisher
approximation is used. The fitted intervals are generalized-posterior
summaries of ordered root draws, and the shaded regions are pointwise 95\%
generalized-posterior credible bands for the
endpoint functionals. They are not posterior predictive intervals for future
responses. The examples show how ordinary RQR, equal-tailed recovery, and
shortest-window recovery enter the
fixed-rate Gibbs sampler at content \(c=0.95\). They are not a simulation
study. All 27 chains and six family--target cells passed the numerical,
provenance, conditional-reference, finite-output, and broad-recovery
requirements with zero numerical repairs. Five cells also passed every strict
diagnostic. Dynamic SH retained five prespecified bulk-ESS warnings
(\(801\)--\(996\), relative to a threshold of \(1{,}000\)) and narrowly missed
one width-contrast diagnostic. Its largest \(\widehat R\) was \(1.0065\), its
tail ESS exceeded \(1{,}166\), and its MCSE-to-standard-deviation ratio was
below \(0.037\). We retained this fit without changing a threshold, replacing
a seed, or selectively extending a chain. The supplement reports the compact
recovery table and endpoint-error summaries.

The matched ordinary-RQR study protocol uses common data-generating mechanisms,
training and forecast windows, content levels, seeds, and scoring rules across
fixed-design RQR, RQR-DLM, separately fitted quantile intervals, and empirical
baselines. Its estimands, factors, Monte Carlo replication, failures, and
precision targets are frozen before execution following simulation-study
reporting principles \citep{MorrisWhiteCrowther2019Simulation}. Primary
summaries are held-out RQR loss, empirical coverage error,
width at comparable coverage, and endpoint recovery when a population oracle
is available. The equal-tailed interval score is secondary because it targets
a different placement principle. Dynamic diagnostics use time-local and
future ordered roots, widths, midpoints, losses, learning scales, and
component scales rather than only time averages.

The current comparative computational evidence concerns ordinary RQR. Fixed-rate
nonzero mean tilt has verified full-conditional algebra, zero-tilt equivalence
checks, dense conditional references, and the single-data oracle-tilt
illustrations above for ridge static readouts and fixed-\(W\) dynamic roots.
These bounded checks do not validate repeated-sample coverage or a data-driven
tilt-selection rule. Such a rule requires an externally evaluated protocol.
Variational inference likewise requires its own objective,
generalized-posterior approximation, and uncertainty assessment. The frozen
RQR-DESN readout is evaluated as a static-design specialization: its reservoir
is recorded before the generalized posterior updates the root coefficients.
Matched nonlinear-readout evidence remains deferred. Software status, runtime
attestation, and validation records are provided in the reproducibility
documentation.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Probability content alone determines a class, not an interval placement.
Ordinary RQR selects its mean-preserving member under the stated population
conditions. The mean tilt exposes the larger geometry: each admissible
interior value specifies a retained mean and hence one contiguous
content-\(c\) window. Equal-tailed and shortest-contiguous intervals are
distribution-specific members of this family rather than universally named
tilt values.

The generalized-Bayes computation follows directly from this target. The
normal--exponential representation augments the generalized posterior with one
latent scale per observed pseudo-residual. It yields Gaussian root full
conditionals, but it neither supplies a response likelihood nor licenses
simulated responses. The ordinary RHS-NS adapter regularizes the coefficient
blocks. A frozen DESN feature design invokes the same static sampler and does
not define a separate interval model. The learned inverse-loss scale
\(\kappa\) is a convention within a specified hierarchical generalized
posterior, not a response variance or an automatic endpoint-calibration
parameter.

The dynamic extension preserves the interval target while replacing static
coefficients by evolving root states. A Gaussian stacked prior does not
overcome the quartic joint term; exact computation instead alternates the two
Gaussian root-path conditionals. Fixed evolution
covariances, frozen discount templates, and ordinary-RQR shared component
scales define fixed-joint generalized posteriors. Nonzero-tilt component-scale
interweaving is not yet available. Recomputing discount covariances from each
changing conditional filter remains a working recursion, not an exact Gibbs
sampler for a demonstrated joint density.

The tolerance proposal adds a distinct frequentist layer. A scan statistic
chooses an empirical retained count, after which the shortest closed
order-statistic window at that count is reported. Generalized-Bayes
computation begins only after the content and tilt are fixed. This ordering
prevents posterior credibility from being mistaken for tolerance confidence.
Publication-level finite-sample wording still requires an action-matched scan
proof and certified critical-count calculation. Replacing the empirical
window by a posterior summary would require an additional transfer or direct
calibration result.

The limitations delimit several next steps. Population identification is
unrestricted and pointwise, whereas regression and state-space models impose
restricted predictor classes. Static covariance statements require
fixed-dimensional iid random design and a globally separated ordered chart;
they do not cover crossing raw linear roots after pointwise sorting, serial
dependence, selected reservoirs, or large-\(T\) state asymptotics. Boundary
tilts can yield semi-infinite limits or inactive roots, and tilts outside the
admissible range have no finite unrestricted target. Broader nonzero-tilt
priors and evolution modes, external tilt selection, variational
approximations, and matched nonlinear-readout evidence require separate
analysis. Evaluation should therefore retain distinct summaries of
interval-root loss, empirical coverage, width, and endpoint recovery rather
than interpreting any of them as posterior-predictive response calibration.
