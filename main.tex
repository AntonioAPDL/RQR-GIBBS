\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Intervals with the same probability content can have different endpoint
placements and widths. This ambiguity matters for regression and tolerance
inference because equal-tailed, mean-preserving, and shortest-contiguous
intervals answer different statistical questions. Pouplin et al. introduced a
residual-product criterion under the name Relaxed Quantile Regression. We show
that, under regularity conditions, its unrestricted regular minimizer is the
unique fixed-content interval whose retained mean equals the population mean;
we therefore call this target the mean-preserving interval (MPI). The
mean-tilted interval (MTI) family generalizes MPI by replacing zero
retained-mean balance with a fixed retained-mean offset: \(\delta=0\)
recovers MPI, and nonzero tilts index other contiguous fixed-content windows.
The equal-tailed and shortest-contiguous intervals are distribution-specific
members of this family.

To estimate these targets, we develop a loss-based generalized-Bayes update for
the two interval endpoints. A pseudo-asymmetric-Laplace normal--exponential
augmentation gives closed-form Gibbs computation: latent scales have
generalized-inverse-Gaussian full conditionals, and the interval-root updates
are conditionally Gaussian. Exact inverse-scale moments give a deterministic
expectation/conditional-maximization mode calculation. These constructions cover
ridge-regularized static regression, endpoint regression with fixed deep echo
state network features, and dynamic linear root states.

Because the shortest-contiguous member is the minimum-width interval in the
fixed-content class, the same target geometry also motivates calibrated
minimum-width tolerance actions. We use TCSP for the resulting
tolerance-calibrated shortest-path action: it selects the shortest closed
order-statistic interval at a calibrated retained count, giving the
minimum-width member of the calibrated empirical scan class. A direct
Dirichlet-process response-distribution layer gives exact fixed-interval Beta
content probabilities, and a Bayesian-constrained scan action uses those
probabilities to select eligible windows. Tolerance confidence is supplied by
scan calibration, while posterior credibility describes the fitted generalized
posterior. MTI endpoint summaries diagnose endpoint uncertainty;
response-predictive draws require a response-distribution layer.
A prespecified iid validation study at tolerance confidence \(0.95\) compares
the TCSP scan action, a profile-calibrated MTI-ECM generalized-Bayes
comparator, and the Young--Mathew and Wilks order-statistic competitors across
feasible content--sample-size cells and eight distributional families. The
simulation design excludes settings where even the full sample range cannot
support the requested two-sided distribution-free statement.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

A prescribed probability content defines a class of possible intervals.
Equal-tailed, shortest-contiguous, and other intervals can contain the
same population fraction while placing their endpoints in different parts of
the distribution. Tolerance intervals add a second requirement: with confidence
\(1-\alpha\), the reported random set should contain at least content \(c\) of
the population distribution
\citep{Wilks1941ToleranceLimits,Wilks1942StatisticalPrediction,Robbins1944DistributionFreeTolerance}.
Thus content, placement, and repeated-sampling confidence must be kept
separate. Fitting the two conditional quantiles at \((1-c)/2\) and
\((1+c)/2\) is appropriate for a central equal-tailed target
\citep{KoenkerBassett1978}. Shortest-interval and mean-preserving questions
require different functionals. Evaluation should therefore match the interval
functional being estimated
\citep{BrehmerGneiting2021Intervals,FisslerEtAl2021SetValued}.

Pouplin et al. introduced a residual-product criterion under the name Relaxed
Quantile Regression \citep{PouplinEtAl2024RQR}. The loss is built from
\((y-\eta_1)(y-\eta_2)\), whose sign records whether \(y\) lies between the two
roots. It is therefore a check loss applied to a product residual, not to a
lower or upper response residual separately. The original work develops this
criterion as an optimization tool. Here the population score equations identify
the unrestricted regular target: among contiguous intervals of content \(c\),
the minimizer is the interval whose retained mean equals the population mean.
We call this target the mean-preserving interval (MPI). The terminology is
descriptive; the original contribution remains the Pouplin et al.
residual-product criterion.

The same score structure gives a direct generalization. The mean-tilted
interval (MTI) family keeps the MPI content score and replaces retained-mean
equality by a fixed retained-mean offset \(\delta\). Thus MPI is the
\(\delta=0\) member of the family, while nonzero tilts select where the
interval sits within the content-\(c\) class. Equal-tailed and
shortest-contiguous intervals correspond to distribution-specific tilts.
Consequently, \(\delta\) is part of the target specification, either fixed
externally or selected by a separate validation rule.

The computational construction follows generalized Bayes. A prior is updated by
\(\exp\{-\omega_{\mathrm R}\sum_i\ell_i\}\) to define a generalized posterior
\citep{BissiriHolmesWalker2016GeneralBayes}. A
pseudo-asymmetric-Laplace normal--exponential augmentation, adapted from
Bayesian quantile-regression computation
\citep{yu2001bayesian,KozumiKobayashi2011Gibbs}, gives
generalized-inverse-Gaussian latent-scale full conditionals and Gaussian root
full conditionals. The augmentation represents the product-residual loss factor
in the generalized posterior; the response distribution for \(Y\) enters only
through a separately specified response model. A fixed tilt changes only the
Gaussian information vectors. The same conditional structure supports
static interval-root regression, endpoint regression with fixed deep echo state
network (DESN) features
\citep{Jaeger2001EchoState,GallicchioMicheliPedrelli2018DeepESNDesign},
and dynamic linear root states.

For fixed targets, the augmentation also yields deterministic mode computation.
The ECM algorithm \citep{DempsterLairdRubin1977EM,MengRubin1993ECM} uses exact
inverse latent-scale moments in the E-step and two Gaussian conditional
maximization steps for the roots. Related asymmetric-Laplace quantile-regression EM
algorithms operate in a response working-likelihood setting
\citep{ZhaoLian2016EMBayesianQR}; the present ECM algorithm optimizes a
fixed loss-defined generalized-posterior mode. Posterior draws require the
Gibbs sampler described below.

The dynamic extension replaces each root coefficient vector by a linear
Gaussian state trajectory. The two trajectories have a jointly Gaussian stacked
prior, but the product residual makes their augmented generalized-posterior
contribution quartic. Conditioning on either complete trajectory restores a
linear Gaussian system for the other, to which dynamic-linear-model
forward-filtering backward-sampling (FFBS) applies
\citep{GoncalvesMigonBastos2020DQLM}. Alternating the two root-path draws gives
exact blocked-Gibbs updates for the specified fixed-joint generalized posterior.

Tolerance and full-distribution Bayesian interval summaries are downstream of
this fixed-target construction. The scan-calibrated empirical procedure first
chooses a retained count by a distribution-free scan statistic, selects the
shortest closed order-statistic window at that count, and then records the MTI
tilt of that selected window. A fixed-target MTI generalized posterior can
summarize local endpoint uncertainty after this choice has been made. The
same-sample shortest-window selection has its own scan-calibration layer.
The ordinary full-distribution Bayesian alternatives instead model the unknown
response distribution \(F\): the direct Dirichlet-process posterior gives
exact fixed-interval content probabilities, and a Gaussian
Dirichlet-process mixture provides a smooth posterior alternative. These
response-distribution models can be compared with MPI and MTI only after the
target, content, and evaluation criterion have been aligned.

These layers are kept separate throughout the article. MPI and MTI are
population fixed-content interval targets. The generalized posterior is a
loss-based distribution over roots or parameters for a specified target.
Response prediction belongs to a response-distribution model. A reported
endpoint summary is a decision rule applied to that generalized posterior or to
an optimizer.
Tolerance calibration is an external content--confidence requirement attached
to an empirical action. Full-distribution Bayesian alternatives instead put a
sampling model and prior on \(F\), and their uncertainty summaries have the
ordinary posterior interpretation under that model.

The contributions are fourfold. First, the residual-product loss is shown to
target the MPI member of the fixed-content interval class, and MTI is shown to
generalize MPI by adding a fixed retained-mean tilt that indexes the remaining
admissible contiguous windows; the same theory gives exact fractional empirical
balance identities. Second, a pseudo-asymmetric-Laplace (pseudo-AL) Gibbs
sampler is derived for fixed-rate static targets and extended to
ridge-regularized static regression, endpoint regression with fixed DESN
features, and root-blocked dynamic linear states under the stated fixed-target
conditions.
Third, a fixed-target MTI-ECM optimizer is derived using exact inverse
latent-scale moments and safeguarded objective descent. Fourth, tolerance and
ordinary full-distribution Bayesian interval actions are formulated as separate
inferential layers, with their available guarantees, diagnostics, and open
proof requirements stated explicitly.

The contribution is intentionally bounded. Available nonzero-tilt computation uses
proper Gaussian priors, a fixed learning rate, and fixed or precomputed dynamic
evolution covariances. Random shrinkage scales, random learning-rate tilt, adaptive or
component-scale dynamic tilt, data-driven tilt selection, variational
approximations, and regression-family tolerance calibration require additional
theory and validation. The generalized posterior concerns loss-defined
interval-root functionals and is not derived from an ordinary response
likelihood. Held-out loss, empirical coverage, width, and population-reference endpoint
recovery are therefore the primary evaluation quantities. Proper scoring rules
remain useful when their target matches the forecast object
\citep{GneitingRaftery2007,Gneiting2011,GneitingKatzfuss2014}; in particular,
the usual interval score targets an equal-tailed interval and is a
secondary comparison here \citep{BrehmerGneiting2021Intervals}. Learning-rate
calibration and sandwich adjustment address distinct uncertainty questions
\citep{SyringMartin2019GeneralPosteriorCalibration,Shaby2014OpenFacedSandwich}.

The paper proceeds as follows. Section~\ref{sec:interval-functionals} defines
the fixed-content interval class. Sections~\ref{sec:mpi-loss}
and~\ref{sec:mti-loss} identify the MPI target and the MTI placement family.
Section~\ref{sec:static-theory-statements} gives finite-sample balance
and fixed-dimensional iid uncertainty statements. Section~\ref{sec:posterior}
develops generalized-Bayes endpoint computation, fixed-target ECM, the MPI
regularized-horseshoe Nishimura--Suchard (RHS-NS) prior representation, and the
fixed-DESN-feature specialization. Section~\ref{sec:dynamic}
gives the dynamic endpoint-state sampler. Section~\ref{sec:tcsp} defines the
scan-calibrated minimum-width tolerance action and states the remaining proof
requirements.
Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} states the confirmatory validation design, and
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} concludes.

\section{Fixed-Content Interval Targets}
\label{sec:interval-functionals}

Let \(Y\) have a nondegenerate continuous distribution with finite mean
\(\mu\) and strictly increasing quantile function \(Q\) on \((0,1)\). Apart
from probability-zero subsets outside the support, every contiguous interval
of content \(c\) can be represented by the probability window
\[
I_c(u)=[Q(u),Q(u+c)],\qquad 0\le u\le1-c.
\]
Define its retained mean and geometric width by
\[
M_c(u)=\frac{1}{c}\int_u^{u+c}Q(v)\,dv,\qquad
W_c(u)=Q(u+c)-Q(u).
\]
Probability content fixes the length of the window on the probability scale,
but not its lower-tail index \(u\). We use ET for equal-tailed, MPI for
mean-preserving, and SH for shortest-contiguous intervals.
Table~\ref{tab:interval-functionals} summarizes these three principal
placement rules.

\section{The Mean-Preserving Interval Target}
\label{sec:mpi-loss}

The residual-product loss becomes a statistical procedure once its target is
identified. This section first defines the pointwise loss, then shows that its
regular unrestricted population solution is the mean-preserving member of the
fixed-content interval class.

Fix a desired interval content \(c\in(0,1)\) and ordered endpoints \(a<b\).
The pointwise MPI loss is
\begin{equation}
\ell_c^{\mathrm{MPI}}(a,b;y)=
\begin{cases}
(1-c)(y-a)(b-y), & a<y<b,\\
c(y-a)(y-b), & y\le a\ \text{or}\ y\ge b.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:mpi-loss-piecewise}
\end{equation}
Both branches are nonnegative and meet continuously at zero when \(y=a\) or
\(y=b\). An observation inside the interval is weighted by \(1-c\), whereas a
miss is weighted by \(c\). The two factors also encode distance from both
endpoints, so the criterion weights membership and endpoint displacement
together. Increasing \(c\) raises the relative cost of a miss; at a regular
population optimum, this weighting
produces the content equation established below. For data
\(y_1,\ldots,y_n\), MPI minimizes
\(\sum_{i=1}^n\ell_c^{\mathrm{MPI}}(a,b;y_i)\), or the corresponding regression
loss when the two endpoints depend on predictors.

The familiar check-loss notation gives an equivalent compact representation.
For a scalar argument \(r\), let
\[
\rho_c(r)=r\{c-\ind(r<0)\}
=
\begin{cases}
(1-c)|r|, & r<0,\\
cr, & r\ge0.
\end{cases}
\]
Now define the product residual
\[
e(a,b;y)=(y-a)(y-b).
\]
Because \(e(a,b;y)<0\) exactly when \(a<y<b\),
\begin{equation}
\ell_c^{\mathrm{MPI}}(a,b;y)=\rho_c\{e(a,b;y)\}.
\label{eq:mpi-product-check}
\end{equation}
This representation also makes clear that the loss is symmetric in its two
roots; the ordering \(a<b\) is imposed only to interpret them as lower and
upper endpoints.
The check loss therefore acts on the scalar product residual, not on
\(y-a\) or \(y-b\) separately. In particular, \(c\) is the desired interval
content; it is neither an assigned response-quantile level for either endpoint
nor the generalized-Bayes learning rate.

The midpoint--half-width parameterization makes the interval geometry
explicit. Write \(a=m-h\) and \(b=m+h\), where \(h>0\). Then
\[
e(a,b;Y)=(Y-m)^2-h^2,
\]
so \(e<0\) means that \(Y\) is within distance \(h\) of \(m\). The half-width
and midpoint are estimated jointly by minimizing the expected loss
\(R_c(a,b)=\E\{\ell_c^{\mathrm{MPI}}(a,b;Y)\}\). Its first-order conditions determine both
the interval content and its placement. The loss defines this population
estimand; the generalized-Bayes learning rate introduced in
Section~\ref{sec:posterior} separately controls how strongly the loss updates
the prior. The results below concern an unrestricted pointwise population
problem. Restricted regression classes generally recover only design-weighted
projections of these identities.

The endpoint scores have two population consequences: they set the interval
content and make the retained conditional distribution preserve the conditional
mean.

\begin{proposition}[Mean-preserving score characterization]
\label{prop:population-characterization}
Fix \(x\) and let \(a<b\). Suppose
\(\E(Y^2\mid X=x)<\infty\), the conditional distribution has no mass at \(a\)
or \(b\), differentiation may be interchanged with conditional expectation,
and \((a,b)\) is an interior stationary point over an unrestricted pointwise
endpoint class. Then the two population first-order conditions imply
\[
\Pr(a<Y<b\mid X=x)=c,
\]
and
\[
\E\{Y\ind(a<Y<b)\mid X=x\}
=c\,\E(Y\mid X=x).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\E(Y\mid a<Y<b,X=x)=\E(Y\mid X=x).
\]
\end{proposition}

The population content equation is due to
\citet{PouplinEtAl2024RQR}. The retained-mean equation is a further
consequence of the same first-order system: conditional on \(X=x\), interval
membership has zero covariance with \(Y\). In omitted-tail form, the same
identity balances first-moment mass around the conditional mean:
\[
\E[\{\mu_x-Y\}\ind(Y<a)\mid X=x]
=
\E[\{Y-\mu_x\}\ind(Y>b)\mid X=x],
\qquad \mu_x=\E(Y\mid X=x).
\]
Tail probabilities may differ; MPI balances their first moments around
\(\mu_x\). The retained core and its excluded complement consequently have the
same conditional mean, although the endpoint midpoint may differ from that
mean. The supplement further shows that the retained core is a mean-preserving
contraction of the full distribution in convex order. A finite first moment is
enough for the retained-mean identity once the endpoint scores are valid. The
stronger second-moment assumption ensures that the expected MPI loss
is finite and supports the profiled-risk analysis. This distinction matters
because the loss grows quadratically in an extreme response.

The next theorem converts the score identities into a quantile-window
functional. This is the object that the unrestricted population loss estimates.

\begin{theorem}[Quantile-window identification]
\label{thm:quantile-window}
In addition to the conditions of
Proposition~\ref{prop:population-characterization}, suppose that \(F_x\) is
continuous and strictly increasing on its support, and let \(Q_x=F_x^{-1}\).
Define
\[
M_{c,x}(u)=\frac{1}{c}\int_u^{u+c}Q_x(v)\,dv,
\qquad 0\le u\le1-c.
\]
Then \(M_{c,x}\) is strictly increasing and there is a unique index \(u_c(x)\)
such that \(M_{c,x}\{u_c(x)\}=\mu_x\). The nondegenerate unrestricted MPI
stationary interval is therefore
\[
a_c(x)=Q_x\{u_c(x)\},\qquad
b_c(x)=Q_x\{u_c(x)+c\}.
\]
If \(F_x\) is symmetric about \(\mu_x\), then
\(u_c(x)=(1-c)/2\), and the MPI and equal-tailed intervals coincide.
\end{theorem}

This theorem identifies the unique regular stationary window.
Theorem~\ref{thm:profiled-mean-tilt} below strengthens that statement to
the unique global expected-loss minimizer by profiling over the half-width.
The supplement proves these results and records the restricted score
equations. Under the connected-support and local positive-density conditions
used in Theorem~\ref{thm:profiled-mean-tilt}, the regular unrestricted
population intervals form a nested path. If the conditional quantile function
is continuous at \(p_{\mu,x}=F_x(\mu_x)\) and
\(Q_x(p_{\mu,x})=\mu_x\), the two endpoints converge to \(\mu_x\) as
\(c\downarrow0\). Without this local support condition, the zero-content
limit may straddle a support gap. At fixed positive content, the midpoint's
role remains interval placement, while the conditional mean is imposed through
retained-mean balance. If the
conditional law has endpoint atoms,
the corresponding directional conditions give
\[
\Pr(a<Y<b\mid X=x)\le c\le\Pr(a\le Y\le b\mid X=x),
\]
so exact open-interval coverage requires endpoint continuity. Coincident roots
reduce the loss to \(c(Y-a)^2\) and target the conditional mean rather than a
nontrivial interval. Exact retained-mean equality is not asserted at endpoint
atoms without a separate joint subgradient and mass-allocation analysis.

MPI therefore targets an interval directly and can accommodate asymmetric
conditional distributions without assigning endpoint quantile levels in
advance. The characterization is pointwise and unrestricted; it holds only
under the stated conditions. Linear and other restricted root classes instead
satisfy design-weighted score equations and need not attain conditional
coverage or mean preservation at every covariate value. Nor does the
finite-sample unbiasedness argument for fixed endpoints apply automatically to
fitted endpoints: the endpoints depend on the data, and the empirical
objective is nonsmooth when an observation coincides with a root. Finite-sample
or asymptotic guarantees require a separate empirical-process argument.

\section{Mean-Tilted Intervals}
\label{sec:mti-loss}

The MTI family is the fixed-tilt generalization of MPI. It preserves the MPI
content score and replaces the zero retained-mean-balance equation by a fixed
retained-mean tilt \(\delta\), measured in response units. To move through the
content-\(c\) family without changing the coverage score, define
\[
\ell_{c,\delta}^{\mathrm{MTI}}(m,h;y)
=
\rho_c\{(y-m)^2-h^2\}-2c\delta(m-y).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\ell_{c,\delta}^{\mathrm{MTI}}(a,b;y)
=
\rho_c\{(y-a)(y-b)\}-c\delta(a+b-2y).
\]
The second form shows that the loss remains invariant to exchanging the two
roots. MPI is exactly the \(\delta=0\) member of the MTI family.

This generalization changes placement but not content. The proposition records
that separation explicitly.

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-content mean tilt]
\label{prop:mean-tilt}
Under the regularity conditions of
Proposition~\ref{prop:population-characterization}, a nondegenerate interior
stationary point of the mean-tilted population risk satisfies
\[
\Pr(a_\delta<Y<b_\delta)=c,\qquad
\E(Y\mid a_\delta<Y<b_\delta)=\mu+\delta.
\]
If \(F\) is continuous and strictly increasing on its support, then for every
\(\delta\) strictly between \(M_c(0)-\mu\) and
\(M_c(1-c)-\mu\), its unique interior quantile-window index solves
\[
M_c(u_\delta)=\mu+\delta.
\]
\end{proposition}

The score equations identify stationary points. Global identification follows
by profiling over the unique content-\(c\) radius at each midpoint and
differentiating the profiled risk; the supplement gives the radius and
envelope-derivative argument.

\begin{theorem}[Global identification of the MTI target]
\label{thm:profiled-mean-tilt}
Suppose that the support closure is an interval (possibly with infinite
endpoints), \(F\) is absolutely continuous and strictly increasing between its
support endpoints, its density is positive and continuous on the support
interior, and \(\E(Y^2)<\infty\). Extend \(F\) by zero and one beyond finite
support endpoints. For every tilt satisfying
\[
M_c(0)-\mu<\delta<M_c(1-c)-\mu,
\]
the expected MTI loss has a unique global minimizer over finite ordered
roots. Its probability-window index \(u_\delta\in(0,1-c)\) is the unique
solution of
\[
M_c(u_\delta)=\mu+\delta.
\]
Nondegeneracy gives \(M_c(0)<\mu<M_c(1-c)\), so MPI is the interior
special case \(\delta=0\).
\end{theorem}

Finite second moments make both endpoint tilt values finite, even when a
support endpoint is infinite. Boundary tilts correspond to one-sided
probability-window limits. Infinite support endpoints lead to semi-infinite
limiting intervals, and finite endpoints require a support constraint to choose
a unique inactive-root representation. Outside the closed admissible tilt range,
the unrestricted risk is unbounded below along the corresponding escaping-root
direction. A proper Gaussian prior can nevertheless yield a proper
finite-sample generalized posterior.

The recovery tilts translate familiar placement rules into the MTI coordinate.
There are three distinct uses of the tilt notation. A fixed target tilt is
chosen before generalized-posterior computation; a population recovery tilt
identifies which \(\delta\) reproduces a familiar interval functional under a
known distribution; an empirical plug-in tilt estimates such a value through a
separate target-selection rule.

\begin{corollary}[Recovery tilts for familiar interval functionals]
\label{cor:recovery-tilts}
MPI is recovered by \(\delta_{\mathrm{MPI}}=0\). Define
\[
S_{\mathrm{SH}}
=
\argmin_{0\le u\le1-c}W_c(u),\qquad
\Delta_{\mathrm{SH}}
=
\{M_c(u)-\mu:u\in S_{\mathrm{SH}}\}.
\]
The population recovery tilt for the equal-tailed interval is
\[
\delta_{\mathrm{ET}}=M_c\{(1-c)/2\}-\mu.
\]
Each \(u\in S_{\mathrm{SH}}\) has recovery tilt
\(\delta_{\mathrm{SH}}(u)=M_c(u)-\mu\). Singular notation is used only when the
shortest-contiguous window is unique or one minimizer has been selected.
\end{corollary}

At regular interior points where the endpoint quantiles are differentiable
and have positive densities,
\[
\frac{du_\delta}{d\delta}=\frac{c}{W_c(u_\delta)},\qquad
\frac{dW_c(u_\delta)}{d\delta}
=\frac{c\,W_c'(u_\delta)}{W_c(u_\delta)}.
\]
Thus an interior width minimum along the tilt path satisfies the usual
equal-endpoint-density condition.

For initialization and external tilt selection, the same identities suggest a
simple first-order approximation. Let \(\gamma_1\) denote standardized
skewness and set
\[
q_c=\Phi^{-1}\{(1+c)/2\},\qquad K_c=q_c\phi(q_c)/c.
\]
A Cornish--Fisher expansion around a standardized Normal law
\citep{CornishFisher1937} gives the
shortest-oriented and equal-tailed standardized tilt approximations
\[
d_{\mathrm{SH}}^{\mathrm{CF}}=-\gamma_1K_c,\qquad
d_{\mathrm{ET}}^{\mathrm{CF}}=\frac{1}{3}d_{\mathrm{SH}}^{\mathrm{CF}},
\qquad d=\delta/\operatorname{SD}(Y).
\]
These formulas are not new interval targets; they are local skewness-based
approximations to the population recovery tilts. Right skewness gives a
negative shortest-oriented tilt, while left skewness gives a positive tilt.
They supply simple, interpretable starting values for tilt exploration, not
automatic shortest-interval estimators. Figure~\ref{fig:mean-tilt-cf-anchors}
places the two approximations on the exact population tilt path using true
population skewness rather than a sample estimate.

Table~\ref{tab:mean-tilt-cf-mini-study} shows why the approximation should
remain an approximation rather than a target definition. It is exact at symmetry and
close for the near-Normal Gamma laws. Its errors are larger for lognormal and
bounded Beta shapes and for the exponential law, whose shortest interval is a
support-boundary solution. The reflected Beta rows verify the expected sign
reversal but also show that skewness alone does not determine approximation
accuracy. Across these examples the ET approximation is more accurate than the
SH approximation, but that comparison is descriptive rather than a general
ordering.
The numerical summaries therefore report moment shape,
probability-window boundary behavior, and bootstrap sensitivity whenever a
sample plug-in approximation is constructed.

The recovery tilts above are population quantities. A universal tilt does not
recover the same interval functional across distributions, and conditional
recovery generally requires a covariate-dependent \(\delta(x)\). The tilt is
therefore fixed or selected by a separate validation rule; sampling
it as an ordinary parameter would mix distinct interval targets. The target is
equivariant under positive affine changes of response scale when \(\delta\)
and the fixed learning rate are transformed accordingly, but it is not
invariant to arbitrary monotone response transformations. Fixed nonzero tilt
is currently supported for fixed-rate ridge regression and MPI endpoint
regression with DESN features,
and for the MPI dynamic linear model (MPI-DLM) with fixed evolution covariance
or a precomputed discount template.
Other prior, learning-rate, and evolution-specification combinations require separate
propriety arguments and validation. Data-driven tilt selection likewise
requires an independent selection rule before empirical claims are made.

The population score equations do not by themselves establish propriety of a
finite-sample generalized posterior under an arbitrary root prior. With a
fixed learning rate, a finite design or state horizon, and proper Gaussian
root priors, the tilt is linear in the root parameters and the Gaussian tails
make the generalized posterior proper. This argument covers ridge regression
and Gaussian state models. Heavier-tailed shrinkage priors require a separate
propriety analysis before nonzero tilt is considered.

\section{Empirical Balance and Static Uncertainty}
\label{sec:static-theory-statements}

This section separates two finite-sample questions: what the fitted training
interval balances exactly and how fixed-dimensional static endpoint uncertainty
differs from generalized-posterior curvature.

\paragraph{Training-sample balance.}
Fitted endpoints depend on the observations, and the empirical MPI loss is
nonsmooth when an observation lies on a root. The corresponding finite-sample
score identity is fractional. For intercept-only MTI with fixed
\(\delta\), let
\((\widehat a,\widehat b)\), \(\widehat a<\widehat b\), be an interior
subgradient-stationary empirical solution. Then there exist allocations
\(\widehat q_i\in[0,1]\), equal to one for observations strictly inside the
fitted interval, zero for observations strictly outside it, and fractional
only at fitted roots, such that
\[
\sum_{i=1}^n \widehat q_i=nc,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^n \widehat q_i y_i
=nc(\overline y+\delta).
\]
Thus the training interval has exact fractional content and exact fractional
retained-mean balance in its empirical estimating equations. Population content
requires an additional probability argument, such as the iid intercept-only DKW
bound below.

If \(B_n\) observations lie on fitted roots, then
\[
P_n(\widehat a<Y<\widehat b)\le c\le
P_n(\widehat a\le Y\le\widehat b),
\qquad
\left|P_n(\widehat a<Y<\widehat b)-c\right|\le B_n/n.
\]
In the iid intercept-only case, this combines with the
Dvoretzky--Kiefer--Wolfowitz inequality to give a conservative
high-probability population-content bound; the supplement gives the explicit
display. Regression fits satisfy analogous design-weighted score equations;
when a global ordering is valid, their coverage component contains
\(\sum_i X_i\widehat w_i(c-\widehat q_i)=0\), with
\(\widehat w_i=\widehat U_i-\widehat L_i\). Within such regression fits, the
score controls a width-weighted projection in the chosen design span. Pointwise
conditional coverage requires additional model and richness conditions.

\paragraph{Static endpoint uncertainty.}
A separate static large-sample theory concerns iid random-design observations
\(Z_i=(X_i,Y_i)\), fixed coefficient dimension, fixed \(c\), fixed
\(\delta(X)\), and fixed \(\omega_{\mathrm R}\). It assumes a globally
labelable ordered chart with uniformly separated roots
\(a_0(X)=X^\top\beta_{L0}<b_0(X)=X^\top\beta_{U0}\), positive endpoint
densities, a unique population target, positive-definite curvature, and a
proper prior positive near the target. The result applies in this ordered-chart setting.
Crossing raw linear roots after pointwise sorting, selected DESN reservoirs,
deterministic fixed-design triangular arrays, dependent time series, growing
feature dimension, random \(\kappa\), and same-sample tilt selection fall
outside the theorem.

Under these static regularity conditions, write
\(\theta_0=(\beta_{L0}^\top,\beta_{U0}^\top)^\top\). Let \(H\) be the
expected-risk Hessian and \(J=\operatorname{Var}\{\psi_0(Z)\}\) the variance
of the endpoint score vector; the supplement records their block forms. These
matrices separate curvature of the loss-defined target from repeated-sampling
variation in the empirical score. The
empirical minimizer has asymptotic sandwich covariance
\[
\Sigma_F=H^{-1}JH^{-1},
\]
whereas the fixed-rate generalized posterior has local curvature covariance
\[
\Sigma_G=(\omega_{\mathrm R}H)^{-1}.
\]
Consequently a scalar learning rate reproduces the full joint endpoint
covariance only under the information-proportionality condition
\(J=\omega_{\mathrm R}^{-1}H\). When consistent estimators
\(\widehat H\) and \(\widehat J\) are available and \(H,J\) are positive
definite, an open-faced-sandwich transformation
\[
\theta^{\mathrm{adj}}
=\widehat\theta+
\widehat\Sigma_F^{1/2}\widehat\Sigma_G^{-1/2}
(\theta-\widehat\theta)
\]
calibrates static coefficient and differentiable endpoint-function summaries
to first order. The adjustment is applied after the generalized-posterior draw
is produced and targets static endpoint uncertainty; dynamic and DESN forecast
calibration require the separate diagnostics and validation protocols below.

\section{Generalized-Bayes Endpoint Computation}
\label{sec:posterior}

\subsection{Static Endpoint Models and Target Definition}

This section uses raw roots for computation and ordered endpoints for
reporting. The root labels \(1\) and \(2\) index coefficient blocks; lower
and upper endpoints are formed only after the roots are evaluated at a design
row.

Let \(I_{\mathrm{obs}}\) index the observed rows of
\(\mat X\in\R^{n\times p}\), with \(n_{\mathrm{obs}}=|I_{\mathrm{obs}}|\), and
define two raw-root predictors
\[
\vect\eta_k=\mat X\vect\beta_k,\qquad k=1,2.
\]
Pointwise reporting uses
\[
L_i=\min(\eta_{1i},\eta_{2i}),\quad
U_i=\max(\eta_{1i},\eta_{2i}),\quad
M_i=(L_i+U_i)/2,\quad W_i=U_i-L_i.
\]
These ordered summaries are generalized-posterior functionals of the unordered
root pair. The implemented generalized posterior remains the raw-root
specification in \((\vect\beta_1,\vect\beta_2)\). For interpretation, the same
draws can be summarized by the midpoint and signed half-gap coefficients
\[
\vect\beta_m=\frac{\vect\beta_1+\vect\beta_2}{2},\qquad
\vect\beta_s=\frac{\vect\beta_2-\vect\beta_1}{2}.
\]
At a design row \(\vect x\), write
\[
M(\vect x)=\vect x^\top\vect\beta_m,\qquad
S(\vect x)=\vect x^\top\vect\beta_s.
\]
Then
\[
L(\vect x)=M(\vect x)-|S(\vect x)|,\qquad
U(\vect x)=M(\vect x)+|S(\vect x)|,\qquad
W(\vect x)=2|S(\vect x)|.
\]
A complete exchange of the two raw root blocks leaves
\(\vect\beta_m\), \(L\), \(U\), and \(W\) unchanged and reverses the sign of
\(\vect\beta_s\). Thus \(\vect\beta_m\) describes the geometric midpoint of the
reported interval, while \(\vect\beta_s\) is a signed gap coordinate. This
midpoint is geometric:
Proposition~\ref{prop:population-characterization} concerns the retained
conditional mean, and MTI uses \(\delta\) to set a retained-mean offset.

When the two root priors are exchangeable, the raw labels \(k=1,2\) are
therefore computational labels. The sampler may apply a complete-root label swap
as an acceptance-one symmetry move; this exchanges all root-specific coefficient
and prior-state variables while preserving the ordered interval functionals.
Coefficient-level lower/upper summaries are reported only as a post-processing
chart on a specified reference design, and only when one complete root block is
separated below the other across that design. If the fitted linear roots cross
on the reference domain or the keep/swap assignment is ambiguous, the
lower/upper coefficient chart is suppressed. In that case, pointwise ordered endpoints,
widths, midpoints, and covariate-profile contrasts are the appropriate
summaries. The static asymptotic result above has the narrower ordered-chart
setting: it assumes one globally labelable, uniformly separated endpoint
ordering.

For each observed row, define
\[
e_i=(y_i-\eta_{1i})(y_i-\eta_{2i}),\qquad
\ell_{c,\delta_i}(\eta_{1i},\eta_{2i};y_i)
=\rho_c(e_i)-c\delta_i(\eta_{1i}+\eta_{2i}-2y_i).
\]
Write \(\theta=(\vect\beta_1^\top,\vect\beta_2^\top)^\top\) for the two-root
coefficient block. For prior densities \(\pi_1,\pi_2\), a fixed externally
specified \(\vect\delta\), and a fixed learning rate \(\omega_{\mathrm R}>0\),
the generalized posterior kernel is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\pi_{\vect\delta}
(\vect\beta_1,\vect\beta_2\mid\vect y,\mat X)
\propto{}&
\pi_1(\vect\beta_1)\pi_2(\vect\beta_2)\\
&\times\exp\left\{-\omega_{\mathrm R}
\sum_{i\in I_{\mathrm{obs}}}
\ell_{c,\delta_i}^{\mathrm{MTI}}(\eta_{1i},\eta_{2i};y_i)\right\}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:fixed-mt-target}
\end{equation}
Thus the prior is updated by the cumulative interval loss. At
\(\delta_i=0\), each contribution is the nonnegative MPI loss in
\eqref{eq:mpi-loss-piecewise}--\eqref{eq:mpi-product-check}.
Equation~\eqref{eq:fixed-mt-target} is a generalized posterior, not one derived
from a response likelihood. The tilt indexes the interval functional and is
fixed before posterior computation rather than sampled as a response
parameter.
Nonzero tilt uses a fixed learning rate and a proper Gaussian prior,
including a ridge prior,
in the present derivation.

The same loss can be paired with several endpoint specifications. Static regression
uses \(\eta_k=\mat X\vect\beta_k\); fixed-DESN-feature endpoint regression
replaces \(\mat X\) by fixed reservoir features; dynamic linear roots use
\(\eta_{kt}=\vect f_t^\top\vect\theta_{kt}\). Exact full conditionals in this
article refer to the specified generalized posterior under these endpoint specifications, not
to a response likelihood or to calibrated endpoint uncertainty. Fixed-rate MPI
and fixed-rate MTI admit Gaussian root updates under the stated Gaussian or
ridge specifications. MPI also supports the hierarchical loss-scale and the
RHS-NS prior representation described below. Nonzero MTI is kept to fixed-rate
Gaussian or ridge settings, fixed-DESN-feature endpoint regression, and fixed
or precomputed dynamic evolution specifications.

\subsection{Pseudo-AL Computation: Gibbs and ECM}
\label{sec:gibbs}

This subsection gives the computational bridge from the loss-defined target to
Gibbs and deterministic mode calculations. The bridge has three parts: a
normal--exponential identity for the product-residual loss factor, a
generalized-inverse-Gaussian update for the latent scales, and Gaussian updates
for one root conditional on the other. The same identity also gives the ECM
mode update after replacing inverse latent scales by their exact conditional
moments.

\paragraph{Latent-scale identity.}
For each observed row, let
\[
r_i=y_i^2,\qquad
m_i^{\mathrm R}=y_i(\eta_{1i}+\eta_{2i})-\eta_{1i}\eta_{2i}.
\]
Then \(r_i-m_i^{\mathrm R}=e_i\). With
\[
\sigma_{\mathrm R}=\omega_{\mathrm R}^{-1},\qquad
\xi_c=\frac{1-2c}{c(1-c)},\qquad
\phi_c=\frac{2}{c(1-c)},
\]
the single-observation factor in the generalized posterior kernel is
proportional to
\[
\int_0^\infty
\Normal\!\left(
r_i\mid m_i^{\mathrm R}+\xi_c v_i,\,
\phi_c\sigma_{\mathrm R}v_i
\right)
\Exp(v_i\mid \text{rate}=1/\sigma_{\mathrm R})\,dv_i .
\]
The integral is over the positive latent scale \(v_i\). It augments the
single-observation factor of the generalized posterior; it is not a sampling
density for the response. The response value enters through the
product-residual loss, while any sampling model for \(Y\) belongs to a
separate response-distribution layer.

\paragraph{Latent-scale update.}
Under the three-parameter convention
\[
\GIG(p,a,b):\quad
p(v)\propto v^{p-1}\exp\{-(av+b/v)/2\},
\]
the observed latent scales have the following full conditional distributions
under the augmented generalized posterior:
\[
v_i\mid\cdot\sim
\GIG\left(
\frac12,\,
\frac{\omega_{\mathrm R}}{2c(1-c)},\,
\frac{\omega_{\mathrm R}c(1-c)e_i^2}{2}
\right),
\qquad i\in I_{\mathrm{obs}}.
\]
The MPI hierarchical-scale convention instead sets
\(\sigma_{\mathrm R}=s_L/\kappa\) and follows the separate partially
collapsed order stated later in this section and in the supplement.

\paragraph{Gaussian root update.}
Conditional on \(\vect\beta_2\), the pseudo-observation equation is linear in
\(\vect\beta_1\):
\[
\mat A_1=\diag(\vect y_{\mathrm{obs}}-\vect\eta_{2,\mathrm{obs}})
\mat X_{\mathrm{obs}},\qquad
\vect z_1=\vect y_{\mathrm{obs}}\circ
(\vect y_{\mathrm{obs}}-\vect\eta_{2,\mathrm{obs}})
-\xi_c\vect v_{\mathrm{obs}}.
\]
With
\(\mat W_v=\diag\{(\phi_c\sigma_{\mathrm R}v_i)^{-1}:i\in I_{\mathrm{obs}}\}\)
and Gaussian prior
canonical parameters \((\mat Q_{01},\vect h_{01})\),
\[
\mat Q_1=\mat Q_{01}+\mat A_1^\top\mat W_v\mat A_1,\qquad
\vect h_1=\vect h_{01}+\mat A_1^\top\mat W_v\vect z_1
+\omega_{\mathrm R}c\mat X_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\top}\vect\delta_{\mathrm{obs}},
\]
so the Gaussian full conditional is
\[
\vect\beta_1\mid\cdot\sim
\Normal(\mat Q_1^{-1}\vect h_1,\mat Q_1^{-1}).
\]
The update for \(\vect\beta_2\) is identical after swapping root labels and
conditioning on the newly drawn first root. The tilt changes only the two
Gaussian information vectors: the latent-scale conditionals and Gaussian
precision matrices are unchanged.

\paragraph{ECM mode.}
For a deterministic fixed-target mode, the same augmentation gives an ECM
algorithm. At the current roots, for nonzero residual product \(e_i\),
\[
\tau_i=E(v_i^{-1}\mid\cdot)=\frac{1}{c(1-c)|e_i|}.
\]
Holding the second root fixed, let
\[
\mat A_1=\diag(\vect y_{\mathrm{obs}}-\vect\eta_{2,\mathrm{obs}})
\mat X_{\mathrm{obs}},\qquad
\vect c_1=\vect y_{\mathrm{obs}}\circ
(\vect y_{\mathrm{obs}}-\vect\eta_{2,\mathrm{obs}}).
\]
Replacing \(v_i^{-1}\) by \(\tau_i\) in the expected complete-data criterion
gives a Gaussian conditional maximization with precision
\[
\mat Q_1^{\mathrm{ECM}}
=\mat Q_{01}
+\frac{1}{\phi_c\sigma_{\mathrm R}}
\mat A_1^\top\diag(\vect\tau)\mat A_1
\]
and canonical vector
\[
\vect h_1^{\mathrm{ECM}}
=\vect h_{01}
+\frac{1}{\phi_c\sigma_{\mathrm R}}
\mat A_1^\top\{\diag(\vect\tau)\vect c_1-\xi_c\vect 1\}
+\omega_{\mathrm R}c\mat X_{\mathrm{obs}}^\top\vect\delta_{\mathrm{obs}}.
\]
The first conditional maximization sets
\(\vect\beta_1=(\mat Q_1^{\mathrm{ECM}})^{-1}\vect h_1^{\mathrm{ECM}}\);
the second uses the updated first root and the same outer E-step
\(\vect\tau\). This is ECM rather than a single EM maximization because the
augmented pseudo-residual is quartic jointly in the two root blocks but
quadratic in either block conditional on the other. The numerical ECM calculation
uses a prespecified residual-product safeguard at \(e_i=0\), exact observed
objective backtracking, and deterministic multi-start selection. Its reported
endpoint values are fixed-target mode summaries; tolerance certification comes from the
scan-calibrated decision rule.

\subsection{Regularization and the Static Gibbs Sweep}

The fixed-design calculation also supports regularized MPI. Let
\(\vect\psi_k\) denote a root-specific prior state and suppose that,
conditional on this state, root \(k\) has Gaussian canonical parameters
\(\{\mat Q_{0k}(\vect\psi_k),\vect h_{0k}(\vect\psi_k)\}\). The MPI
MCMC representation supports the regularized-horseshoe Nishimura--Suchard (RHS-NS)
prior representation
\citep{CarvalhoPolsonScott2010HS,PiironenVehtari2017RHS,
NishimuraSuchard2023SSS}. For this specification, the first
design column must be the intercept and is excluded from shrinkage.
For every remaining coefficient \(j\), the conditional prior precision is
\[
d_{kj}
=\frac{1}{\tau_k^2\lambda_{kj}^2}+\frac{1}{\zeta_k^2},
\]
with a separately specified Gaussian precision for the unshrunk intercept.
The local, global, auxiliary, and shoulder-scale quantities in
\(\vect\psi_k\) are updated after the corresponding coefficient draw. The two
roots carry distinct, identically specified prior states, and a root-label
swap moves \((\vect\beta_k,\vect\psi_k)\) as one complete block.

This conditionally Gaussian representation uses the external RHS-NS hierarchy
rather than rederiving its marginal prior. It is available only for MPI,
\(\vect\delta=\vect 0\). The Gaussian
moment-generating-function argument that establishes fixed-rate nonzero-tilt
propriety does not establish the same result after random-scale mixing.

\begin{algorithmblock}{Static fixed-rate MPI/MTI root-blocked Gibbs sampler}
\label{alg:fixed-design-gibbs}
\item \textbf{Specify the generalized posterior.} Fix
\(\vect y,\mat X,I_{\mathrm{obs}},c,\omega_{\mathrm R}\), the target tilt
\(\vect\delta_{\mathrm{obs}}\), proper root priors, and the label-swap rule.
Missing rows contribute no generalized-posterior factor, latent scale, or tilt
term.
\item \textbf{Initialize.} Choose finite
\(\vect\beta_1,\vect\beta_2\), positive \(v_i\) for \(i\in I_{\mathrm{obs}}\), and
valid root-specific prior states when the MPI RHS-NS representation is used.
\item \textbf{Sweep.} For each iteration:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=1.8em,itemsep=0.15em,topsep=0.15em]
\item compute \(e_i\) and draw every observed \(v_i\) from its GIG full
conditional;
\item draw \(\vect\beta_1\) from its Gaussian full conditional given
\(\vect\beta_2\), with tilt shift
\(\omega_{\mathrm R}c\mat X_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\top}
\vect\delta_{\mathrm{obs}}\);
\item update root 1's MPI RHS-NS prior state when that representation is used;
\item rebuild the system and draw \(\vect\beta_2\) conditionally on the new
\(\vect\beta_1\), using the same tilt shift;
\item update root 2's MPI RHS-NS prior state when that representation is used; and
\item when the two complete root blocks are exchangeable, apply the
acceptance-one label swap.
\end{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Report ordered functionals.} Store ordered endpoints, widths,
midpoints, losses, raw root-label diagnostics, and target information. Optional
labelability-based coefficient summaries are post-processing outputs and require a
global labelability condition on the specified reference design. The sampler leaves the
specified fixed-rate proper-Gaussian generalized posterior invariant; at
\(\vect\delta=\vect 0\) it
reduces to MPI. The MPI random-\(\kappa\) sweep and RHS-NS
applicability details are given in the supplement.
\end{algorithmblock}

At \(\vect\delta=\vect 0\), Algorithm~\ref{alg:fixed-design-gibbs} is the
fixed-rate MPI Gibbs sampler. The RHS-NS representation then updates the two
root-specific prior states separately. It can also be used with the MPI
random-\(\kappa\) generalized posterior and the partial-collapse order below.
Neither use establishes fixed- or random-rate propriety at nonzero tilt.

\subsection{Endpoint Regression with Fixed DESN Features}
\label{sec:desn}

Let \(\mat X_{\mathrm{DESN}}\) be the complete deterministic feature design
produced by a deep echo-state network, including any specified intercept column,
with fixed reservoir weights, scaling, washout, preprocessing,
tuning decisions, and random seeds
\citep{Jaeger2001EchoState,GallicchioMicheliPedrelli2018DeepESNDesign,
McDermottWikle2018DeepESNUQ}. Substituting
\(\mat X=\mat X_{\mathrm{DESN}}\) in
Algorithm~\ref{alg:fixed-design-gibbs} defines MPI endpoint regression with
fixed DESN features. It uses the same generalized posterior and Gibbs sampler.
A ridge specification supports the
fixed-rate nonzero-tilt Gaussian full conditionals available through the
static sampler;
the RHS-NS specification and matched evidence concern MPI only.

Future root functionals use a fixed future design
\(\mat X_{\mathrm{DESN,fut}}\) constructed under the same fixed feature
specification, without using held-out responses to refit its features. Ordered
endpoints, midpoints, and widths are functions of the coefficient draws. No
response-simulation distribution is implied, and reservoir or feature
selection occurs outside the generalized-posterior update. This computational
equivalence to a fixed static design does not, by itself, establish the iid
random-design asymptotic theory for serially dependent DESN rows or for
data-selected reservoir features.

\subsection{MPI-Only Hierarchical Loss Scale}

A separate MPI-only convention uses a positive reference loss scale
\(s_L\), sets \(\sigma_{\mathrm R}=s_L/\kappa\), writes
\(L_{n,c}(\vartheta)=
\sum_{i\in I_{\mathrm{obs}}}\rho_c(e_i)\), and declares
\[
\pi_{\mathrm R}(\vartheta,\kappa\mid\vect y)
\propto
\pi(\vartheta)\kappa^{n_{\mathrm{obs}}}\pi_\kappa(\kappa)
\exp\{-\kappa L_{n,c}(\vartheta)/s_L\},
\]
the \emph{pseudo-residual-normalized hierarchical-scale generalized posterior}.
The factor
\(\kappa^{n_{\mathrm{obs}}}\) arises from normalizing an asymmetric-Laplace
kernel in the abstract pseudo-residual \(e_i\); it does not normalize a
response density in \(y_i\). Here \(\vartheta\) collects both roots and, when
present, their proper root-specific prior or evolution states. If
\(\kappa\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(a_\kappa,b_\kappa)\) in shape--rate form, its
collapsed full conditional is
\[
\kappa\mid\vartheta,\vect y
\sim\mathrm{Gamma}\left(a_\kappa+n_{\mathrm{obs}},
b_\kappa+L_{n,c}(\vartheta)/s_L\right).
\]
The factor \(\kappa^{n_{\mathrm{obs}}}\) is part of this generalized posterior
rather than an irrelevant constant when \(\kappa\) is random. A pure-loss
sensitivity generalized posterior that omits this factor is kept separate.

The random \(\kappa\) is part of this hierarchical generalized posterior.
Coherence for a fixed loss scale does not establish that \(\kappa\) is
an ordinary jointly learnable response parameter or that its generalized
posterior calibrates endpoint uncertainty
\citep{LeeLiuNicholls2025LearningRate,WuMartin2023LearningRateComparison}.
We therefore use fixed-rate sensitivity analyses as the primary specification
and treat hierarchical-scale inference as an additional convention. When \(s_L\) is
computed from training responses, its calculation sample and variance
rule are fixed before MCMC; the resulting
generalized posterior is data dependent in an empirical-Bayes sense.

This hierarchical generalized posterior can be paired only with an otherwise
valid MPI fixed-design or fixed-joint root model. Its partially collapsed
Gibbs sampler draws \(\kappa\),
immediately refreshes every observed latent scale at the new value, and only
then updates roots 1 and 2; Supplement Algorithm~S1 gives the complete order.
For any proper MPI root/prior-state law \(\pi(\vartheta)\), joint
integrability follows because the loss is nonnegative,
\(\exp\{-\kappa L_{n,c}(\vartheta)/s_L\}\leq1\), and the Gamma prior
has a finite \(n_{\mathrm{obs}}\)th moment. This covers the specified MPI
RHS-NS representation while preserving its complete prior-state updates.
The mean-tilted loss may be negative, and a proper normalized joint generalized
posterior with random rate has not been established. The Gamma full conditional
above must not be copied to nonzero tilt.

\section{Dynamic Endpoint-State Computation}
\label{sec:dynamic}

The mean-tilted dynamic linear model (MTI-DLM) represents each raw root by a
linear Gaussian state trajectory,
\[
\eta_{kt}=\vect f_t^\top\vect\theta_{kt},\qquad
\vect\theta_{k0}\sim\Normal(\vect m_0,\mat C_0),\qquad
\vect\theta_{kt}=\mat G_t\vect\theta_{k,t-1}+\vect\epsilon_{kt},
\qquad \vect\epsilon_{kt}\sim\Normal(\vect 0,\mat W_t),
\qquad k=1,2.
\]
The two trajectories can be written as one stacked state with block-diagonal
evolution matrices. That representation does not yield one Gaussian FFBS
update. Jointly in both root states, the augmented generalized posterior
contains the square of
\[
(y_t-\vect f_t^\top\vect\theta_{1t})
(y_t-\vect f_t^\top\vect\theta_{2t}),
\]
and hence fourth-order cross-terms. Conditional on either complete root path,
the same expression is linear in the other path. Partitioning the stacked
state by root and drawing the paths sequentially therefore gives exact
full-conditional updates for the specified fixed-joint generalized posterior.
The resulting computational concern is Markov-chain mixing, not approximation
of either full conditional.

For \(t\in I_{\mathrm{obs}}\), conditional on the second root path and the MTI latent
scales, the first path has the pseudo-observation system
\[
z_{1t}=y_t(y_t-\eta_{2t})-\xi_c v_t,\qquad
H_{1t}=(y_t-\eta_{2t})\vect f_t^\top,\qquad
V_t=\phi_c\sigma_{\mathrm R}v_t.
\]
Here the state equation supplies the Gaussian prior on root trajectories, while
the pseudo-observation equation is a conditional representation of the
loss-based generalized-posterior factor.
The second root is analogous and conditions on the newly drawn first path.
Under fixed-rate MTI, the tilt adds the canonical linear site
\[
\vect g_t=\omega_{\mathrm R}c\delta_t\vect f_t
\]
to each root-state conditional. This information-form update never divides by
\(y_t-\eta_{\mathrm{other},t}\) and remains well defined when that quantity is
zero. Equivalently, if the zero-tilt
pseudo-observation update at time \(t\) gives mean
\(\vect m_t^{(0)}\) and covariance \(\mat C_t\), the tilted filter uses
\[
\vect m_t=\vect m_t^{(0)}+\mat C_t\vect g_t
\]
and the same \(\mat C_t\). This shifted mean is propagated before the next
update. At a missing response, the loss, \(v_t\), and \(\vect g_t\) are all
absent. In full-path canonical notation the same operation is
\(\vect m_{\mathrm{path}}^{(0)}+\mat Q_{\mathrm{path}}^{-1}\vect g\), which
provides a direct numerical check.

The evolution specification is part of the generalized posterior. Fixed
\(\mat W_t\) gives a Gaussian state prior. A precomputed discount template first
maps specified component-specific discounts to a complete
\(\mat W_{1:T}\) sequence through a reference covariance recursion and then
holds that sequence fixed throughout MCMC. Conditional on the precomputed template,
alternating root-specific FFBS samples from the exact root-path full
conditionals. A template constructed from training data is treated as an
empirical-Bayes specification.

\begin{algorithmblock}{Fixed-rate MTI-DLM root-blocked Gibbs sampler}
\label{alg:dynamic-gibbs}
\item \textbf{Specify the joint generalized posterior.} Fix
\((\vect y,I_{\mathrm{obs}})\), \(c,\omega_{\mathrm R}\), \(\delta_{1:T}\),
\(\vect f_{1:T}\), \(\mat G_{1:T}\), proper Gaussian initial laws, and either
fixed \(\mat W_{1:T}\) or one precomputed discount template.
\item \textbf{Initialize.} Choose finite root paths
\(\vect\theta_{1,1:T},\vect\theta_{2,1:T}\) and positive initial latent
scales at observed times. Missing responses contribute no generalized-posterior
factor, latent scale, or tilt term.
\item \textbf{Sweep.} For each iteration:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=1.8em,itemsep=0.15em,topsep=0.15em]
\item compute the current product residual and draw \(v_t\) at every
\(t\in I_{\mathrm{obs}}\) from the GIG conditional;
\item conditional on \(\vect\theta_{2,1:T}\), build root 1's scalar Gaussian
pseudo-observation system, add
\(\vect g_t=\omega_{\mathrm R}c\delta_t\vect f_t\) at observed times, and draw
\(\vect\theta_{1,1:T}\) by FFBS;
\item rebuild the system conditional on the new first path and draw the second
path by the analogous tilted FFBS update; and
\item when all root-state priors and evolution laws are exchangeable,
apply the acceptance-one complete-root label swap.
\end{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Report root functionals.} Retain ordered time-local endpoints,
widths, midpoints, selected state functionals, losses, and complete
target and evolution specification. These are not simulated responses. If a dynamic
lower/upper label is requested beyond pointwise ordered endpoints, the
post-processing decision is one complete path assignment per retained draw,
not a separate relabeling at each time. The sampler targets the exact
generalized posterior for fixed \(W_t\) and precomputed-template fixed-joint modes;
adaptive discounts and MPI shared
component scales are separate extensions described in the supplement.
\end{algorithmblock}

At \(\delta_{1:T}=0\), Algorithm~\ref{alg:dynamic-gibbs} reduces exactly to
the fixed-rate MPI-DLM sampler for fixed \(\mat W_t\) or a
precomputed template. A random-learning-rate tilted generalized posterior has not been
normalized and cannot be obtained by inserting the MPI Gamma full
conditional. Future-root propagation
uses a separate fixed forecast specification with fixed future
\(\vect f_t,\mat G_t,\mat W_t\); it neither changes the training Gibbs sweep
nor defines response simulation.

\subsection{MPI-Only Dynamic Variants}

For MPI, an exact component-specific alternative
partitions the state into blocks and sets
\[
\mat W_t(\vect q)=\operatorname{blockdiag}
\{q_1\mat Q_{1t},\ldots,q_J\mat Q_{Jt}\},
\qquad q_j\sim\operatorname{IG}(a_j,b_j),
\]
where the \(\mat Q_{jt}\) are fixed positive-definite templates and the
multipliers are shared across the two roots to preserve exchangeability. The
MPI component-scale sampler uses rootwise partial collapse
and centered/noncentered interweaving; the supplement gives the invariant
transition and update order
\citep{PapaspiliopoulosRobertsSkold2007,YuMeng2011ASIS}. This extension has
been validated for the MPI target. In the noncentered parameterization
the tilt depends on \(\sqrt{q_j}\) through the reconstructed state path, so a
tilted component-scale generalized posterior requires separate normalization,
propriety, and validation. Likewise, recomputing \(\mat W_t\) from each
changing conditional filter covariance is retained only as an experimental
working update until a common joint generalized posterior is supplied.

\section{Calibrated Minimum-Width Tolerance Intervals}
\label{sec:tcsp}

The preceding theory fixes population content and interval placement. A
tolerance interval adds a repeated-sampling requirement. For iid continuous
univariate observations, the target statement is
\[
\Pr_F^n\{C_F(\widehat T_n)\ge c\}\ge 1-\alpha,\qquad
C_F([L,U])=F(U)-F(L),
\]
where \(\widehat T_n\) is a data-dependent interval. Six quantities must be
kept distinct. The required population content is \(c\), and the tolerance
confidence is \(1-\alpha\). The scan calibration returns a retained count
\(k\), so the fitted content used by a fixed-target MTI approximation is
\(q=k/n\). The fixed MTI placement tilt is \(\delta\), and the
generalized-Bayes learning rate is \(\omega_{\mathrm R}\). When a separate
response-distribution model for \(F\) is used, \(1-\gamma\) denotes a posterior
content-probability threshold for eligible intervals. Neither \(\omega_{\mathrm R}\) nor
\(1-\gamma\) determines the tolerance confidence.
In this article, TCSP denotes the tolerance-calibrated shortest-path action:
first calibrate a retained count by the scan statistic, then report the
shortest closed empirical window at that count.

\paragraph{Scan-calibrated empirical action.}
Let \(Y_{(1)}\le\cdots\le Y_{(n)}\). For content \(c\), define the uniform scan
statistic
\[
M_n(c)=\max_{0\le s\le 1-c}\#\{i:U_i\in[s,s+c]\},
\qquad U_i\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\mathrm{Unif}(0,1).
\]
The calibrated retained count is
\[
k_{n,c,\alpha}
=\min\{k:\Pr(M_n(c)<k)\ge 1-\alpha\}.
\]
This count is fixed before any endpoint model is fit. The formal empirical
action is the formal closed order-statistic window
\[
\widehat T_n=
[Y_{(\widehat j)},Y_{(\widehat j+k-1)}],
\qquad
\widehat j=\min\argmin_{1\le j\le n-k+1}
\{Y_{(j+k-1)}-Y_{(j)}\},
\]
with \(k=k_{n,c,\alpha}\). This convention is closed and contains exactly
\(k\) sample observations under continuity. It differs from the half-open
\((Y_{(j)},Y_{(j+k)}]\) convention sometimes used for order-statistic
tolerance limits, so a result proved for one convention cannot be transferred
without reconciling the indices and endpoint rule.

\paragraph{MTI summaries after selection.}
The selected window also defines a fixed-target MTI approximation for endpoint
uncertainty. Its fitted content and content buffer are
\[
q_{n,c,\alpha}=k_{n,c,\alpha}/n,\qquad
t_{n,c,\alpha}=q_{n,c,\alpha}-c.
\]
The empirical shortest-path tilt is
\[
\widehat\delta_{\mathrm{SH}}(q)
=
\frac1k\sum_{\ell=\widehat j}^{\widehat j+k-1}Y_{(\ell)}
-\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nY_i.
\]
After \(q\) and \(\widehat\delta_{\mathrm{SH}}\) are fixed, an MTI endpoint
model with parameter block \(\theta\) uses the loss-based update
\[
\pi_{q,\delta,\omega_{\mathrm R}}(\theta\mid D_n)
\propto
\exp\{-\omega_{\mathrm R}L_{q,\delta,n}(\theta)\}\pi_0(\theta).
\]
This generalized posterior quantifies uncertainty about a fixed interval-root
functional after the target has been specified. The formal tolerance action
remains the empirical order-statistic window \(\widehat T_n\). Because
\(\widehat\delta_{\mathrm{SH}}\) is computed from the same sample, these
posterior summaries are conditional on the selected target and do not propagate
shortest-window selection uncertainty. An unconditional interpretation requires
a plug-in stability theorem or a separate target-selection sample.

\paragraph{Supplementary extensions.}
Response-distribution Bayesian shortest-interval calculations and sample-split spacing constructions
are useful extensions, but they answer different evidentiary questions from the
primary TCSP action. A response-distribution model gives posterior
content probabilities under a model for \(F\), whereas TCSP supplies a
distribution-free scan-calibrated empirical action. A split-spacing construction
uses independent pilot and calibration samples, whereas TCSP uses the full
sample to choose the minimum-width calibrated order-statistic window. These
extensions remain available as separate methodological and proof directions;
they are not part of the main validation comparison.

\paragraph{Numerical certification and proof guarantees.}
Numerical evaluation of \(k_{n,c,\alpha}\) is consequential because it sets the
reported interval. The calculation uses adaptive Monte Carlo
calibration of the uniform scan distribution. At each look, one-sided
Clopper--Pearson lower bounds are Bonferroni-adjusted over feasible retained
counts and adaptive looks, and the first retained count whose certified lower
probability reaches \(1-\alpha\) is selected. The full-range interval
uses its exact order-statistic probability. A more conservative
Massart--DKW empirical-CDF lower band remains available as a conservative
alternative
\citep{Massart1990DKW}. Exact scan recursion remains an open numerical task.
Accordingly, exact critical-count calculation and the finite-sample theorem for
this closed-window action are treated as open proof and certification tasks.

The available theory is deliberately narrower than the computational procedures.
Theorems~\ref{thm:quantile-window}--\ref{thm:profiled-mean-tilt} identify
the fixed-content MTI family and its admissible tilt coordinate. For tolerance
inference, the empirical closed order-statistic window is the formal action;
generalized-posterior summaries for fixed \((q,\delta)\) describe endpoint-root
uncertainty after a target has been specified and do not serve as the
content--confidence certificate. Split exact-spacing calibration has an exact
conditional iid univariate Beta law. The scan count is certified by the
adaptive Clopper--Pearson numerical procedure above, with numerical confidence
distinguished from tolerance confidence. Direct DP and Gaussian DPM
intervals are ordinary
response-distribution Bayesian alternatives, not MTI generalized posteriors.
Exact scan recursion, an action-matched finite-sample proof for the closed
window convention, selected-action asymptotics, posterior-to-action transfer,
and regression-family tolerance calibration remain separate certification
tasks.

\paragraph{Shortest-path continuation.}
The population shortest-window path gives useful continuation geometry for
moving across contents. This geometry helps initialize and check numerical
paths; it is not the scan certificate. Suppose that the density is
differentiable and strictly unimodal,
and that the shortest window
\(I_q=[L_q,U_q]=[Q(u_q),Q(u_q+q)]\) is unique and interior over a range of
\(q\). Equal endpoint densities then satisfy
\[
f(L_q)=f(U_q)=\lambda_q.
\]
Writing \(a_q=f'(L_q)>0\) and \(b_q=f'(U_q)<0\), formal differentiation of
the content and equal-density equations gives
\[
L_q'=\frac{b_q}{\lambda_q(a_q-b_q)}<0,
\qquad
U_q'=\frac{a_q}{\lambda_q(a_q-b_q)}>0.
\]
Thus the first-order shares of added probability assigned to the left and
right boundaries are
\[
p_L(q)=\frac{-b_q}{a_q-b_q},\qquad
p_R(q)=\frac{a_q}{a_q-b_q},\qquad
p_L(q)+p_R(q)=1.
\]
Only under local symmetry are the two shares equal to one half. The width
satisfies
\[
W_{\mathrm{SH}}'(q)=\frac{1}{\lambda_q},
\qquad
W_{\mathrm{SH}}''(q)
=-\frac{a_qb_q}{\lambda_q^3(a_q-b_q)}>0.
\]
Hence additional content becomes increasingly expensive as the endpoints move
into lower-density regions. If
\(m_q=q^{-1}\int_{u_q}^{u_q+q}Q(s)\,ds
=\mu+\delta_{\mathrm{SH}}(q)\), then
\[
\delta_{\mathrm{SH}}'(q)
=\frac{p_L(q)L_q+p_R(q)U_q-m_q}{q}.
\]
These derivative calculations motivate numerical continuation and diagnose
its behavior. Full regularity, boundary, and nonuniqueness arguments are
reserved for a formal shortest-path theorem.

The continuation calculation starts from a globally solved content, predicts each next
window by nested expansion of the previous window, applies local correction in
a neighborhood of the current solution, expands the neighborhood when the local optimum lands on its
boundary, and finally verifies the formal action by an exhaustive empirical scan. The exhaustive
scan, not the continuation approximation, defines the selected window. The continuation argument does not cover infeasible calibration
\((k>n)\), ties or atoms that violate the continuous-sample convention,
nonunique shortest windows, response-scale dependence of geometric width, and
unsupported attempts to attach the scan certificate to posterior summaries.

The construction therefore retains three distinct outputs: an empirical scan
tolerance action, a full-distribution Bayesian shortest-interval uncertainty
quantification action, and fixed-target MTI generalized-posterior summaries.
Finite-sample scan
validity requires an action-matched proof and certified critical count;
first-order tolerance or population-reference width statements additionally require
same-sample shortest-window asymptotics. Any transfer from posterior content
probabilities to endpoint coverage, or from fixed-target MTI summaries to
the selected shortest action, requires a separate action-equivalence or
direct-calibration result. Tolerance confidence comes from the scan calibration,
and \(\omega_{\mathrm R}\) remains the generalized-Bayes learning rate.

\section{Validation Design}
\label{sec:evaluation}

Validation is reserved for questions that require repeated sampling or
held-out comparison. Population calculations verify the fixed-content and
mean-tilt characterizations without Monte Carlo error. Reference calculations
compare generalized-posterior draws with quadrature, dense Gaussian full
conditionals, analytic scale updates, and restart equivalence. These
checks provide numerical evidence for the stated computational target on
reference problems. Empirical coverage, width efficiency, and comparator
ranking require repeated-sampling validation.

The main tolerance study is prespecified before inspecting comparative results,
following simulation-study reporting principles
\citep{MorrisWhiteCrowther2019Simulation}. It uses iid continuous samples from
eight centered and scaled simulation distributions. Gaussian and Student \(t_3\)
samples serve as symmetric reference distributions. The asymmetric simulation
distributions are exponential,
asymmetric Laplace with \(\tau=0.10\), a two-piece Normal law with scale ratio
\(1{:}12\), Beta\((1,8)\), Gamma\((0.5,1)\), and log-normal with log-scale
standard deviation \(1.25\). This panel is intentionally skewed toward
asymmetric laws because the theoretical advantage of a shortest calibrated
window is most relevant when equal-tail or full-range behavior is inefficient.
Multimodal cases are excluded because none of the reported methods is designed
to recover disconnected high-density regions.
The primary simulation design fixes tolerance confidence at \(0.95\) and uses 1000
paired replications per simulation distribution and feasible cell.
The feasible cells are \(n=50,c=0.90\), \(n=100,c\in\{0.90,0.95\}\), and
\(n\in\{500,1000\},c\in\{0.90,0.95,0.99\}\).

The small-sample restriction is structural. For a two-sided distribution-free
interval, even the full sample range reaches content \(c\) with confidence
\(0.95\) only when
\[
  1-n(1-c)c^{n-1}-c^n \ge 0.95 .
\]
The corresponding minimum sample sizes are \(46\), \(93\), and \(473\) for
\(c=0.90\), \(0.95\), and \(0.99\), respectively. Thus
\(n=50,c=0.95\) and \(n=100,c=0.99\) are excluded from the confirmatory
comparison because no two-sided distribution-free method can certify the
requested statement in those cells. The \(c=0.99\) rows begin at \(n=500\), the
nearest rounded sample size above the \(n=473\) threshold.

The comparison is deliberately narrow. TCSP is the formal scan-calibrated
shortest empirical action and is therefore the primary tolerance action. The
reported TCSP calculations use the adaptive Clopper--Pearson scan calibration described
above. Its optimality is class-specific: it minimizes width among closed
order-statistic windows satisfying the calibrated retained-count rule.
The MTI-ECM comparator links the validation study to the generalized-Bayes
interval construction. It uses a frozen adaptive calibration policy indexed by
sample size and requested content. Within each cell, the policy fixes a
direct Dirichlet-process content screen from an independent calibration run,
then profiles fixed-target MTI-ECM endpoint fits over fitted content and
retained-mean tilt and reports the shortest candidate passing that screen. This
empirical validation is separate from the distribution-free scan theorem that
supports TCSP.
Young--Mathew interpolation
\citep{YoungMathew2014NonparametricTolerance} and Wilks min--max intervals
\citep{Wilks1941ToleranceLimits,Wilks1942StatisticalPrediction} provide the
classical order-statistic comparators. The primary metric is the
content-attainment rate: the Monte Carlo estimate of
\(\Pr_F^n\{C_F(\widehat T_n)\ge c\}\), where
\(C_F([L,U])=F(U)-F(L)\). For each replication, this content check uses the
known simulation distribution rather than the fraction of sampled observations
inside the produced interval. If a method produced no interval in a replication,
that replication would be counted as nonattainment. Widths are reported as distribution-level empirical
2.5\%--97.5\% ranges, avoiding width averages across distributions with
different scales and tail behavior.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Probability content alone determines a class of interval placements.
MPI selects its mean-preserving member under the stated population
conditions. MTI generalizes MPI by making retained-mean balance a fixed
coordinate: \(\delta=0\) returns MPI, and each admissible interior value
specifies one contiguous content-\(c\) window. Equal-tailed and
shortest-contiguous intervals are distribution-specific members of this family
rather than universally named tilt values.

The generalized-Bayes computation follows directly from this target. The
normal--exponential representation augments the generalized posterior with one
latent scale per observed pseudo-residual. It yields Gaussian root full
conditionals for the loss-based generalized posterior; response simulation
requires a separate response model. The MPI RHS-NS representation regularizes the
coefficient blocks. A fixed DESN feature design invokes the same static sampler
as a fixed-design endpoint regression. The hierarchical inverse-loss scale
\(\kappa\) is a convention within a specified hierarchical generalized
posterior. Response variance and endpoint calibration are separate modeling and
validation quantities.

The exchangeable-root construction also determines how regression effects should
be read. Raw root coefficients are useful computational coordinates, while the
scientific summaries are the ordered endpoints, the geometric midpoint, the
width, and contrasts of these quantities across named covariate profiles.
Conventional lower- and upper-endpoint coefficient summaries require a
global labelability condition on a specified domain. Under crossing or ambiguous
labeling, the interval remains meaningful through its ordered pointwise
functionals; a permanent endpoint role for one root block is then unavailable.

The dynamic extension preserves the interval target while replacing static
coefficients by evolving root states. The product residual leaves a quartic
joint term under a Gaussian stacked prior, so exact computation alternates the
two Gaussian root-path conditionals. Fixed evolution
covariances, precomputed discount templates, and MPI shared component
scales define fixed-joint generalized posteriors. Nonzero-tilt component-scale
interweaving remains future work. Recomputing discount covariances from each
changing conditional filter should be treated as a working recursion until a
joint density proof is supplied.

The tolerance proposal adds a distinct frequentist layer. A scan statistic
chooses an empirical retained count, after which the shortest closed
order-statistic window at that count is reported. This makes TCSP the formal
tolerance action and gives a class-specific minimum-width property within the
calibrated empirical scan class. Generalized-Bayes computation begins only after
the content and tilt are fixed. Tolerance confidence remains attached to the
scan action, while posterior credibility describes the chosen fixed-target
generalized posterior. The article validation therefore treats TCSP as the
formal tolerance action, MTI-ECM as a directly calibrated generalized-Bayes
comparator, and Young--Mathew and Wilks as classical order-statistic
comparators. Generalized-posterior endpoint summaries become
tolerance-certified intervals only with an additional transfer or
direct-calibration result. A complete finite-sample theorem for the
closed-window convention still requires an action-matched scan proof and
certified critical-count calculation.

The limitations delimit several next steps. Population identification is
unrestricted and pointwise, whereas regression and state-space models impose
restricted predictor classes. Static covariance statements require
fixed-dimensional iid random design and a globally separated ordered chart;
crossing raw linear roots after pointwise sorting, serial dependence, selected
reservoirs, and large-\(T\) state asymptotics are outside the present analysis. Boundary
tilts can yield semi-infinite limits or inactive roots, and tilts outside the
admissible range have no finite unrestricted target. Broader nonzero-tilt
priors and evolution modes, external tilt selection, variational
approximations, and matched nonlinear endpoint-regression evidence require separate
analysis. Evaluation should therefore retain distinct summaries of
interval-root loss, empirical coverage, width, and endpoint recovery rather
than interpreting any of them as posterior-predictive response calibration.
