\author{Antonio De Leon \and Raquel Prado \and Bruno Sans\'o}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Ordinary relaxed quantile regression induces a mean-preserving fixed-content
interval under the population conditions stated in the main article. The
derivations below establish its score equations, quantile-window and
profiled-risk identification, and consequences for the retained and omitted
distributions. They also distinguish equal-tailed, shortest-contiguous, and
mean-tilted targets; record exact fractional empirical balance and the
fixed-dimensional static uncertainty geometry; derive the
pseudo-asymmetric-Laplace augmentation and Gaussian/GIG blocks; state the
fixed-rate mean-tilted information shifts for
fixed design and dynamic states; and give the ordinary-only learned-scale and
component-scale contracts. The fixed-rate nonzero-tilt information shifts are
implemented for ridge fixed-design and DESN readouts and for fixed-\(W_t\) or
frozen-template dynamic modes; broader validation, nonzero-tilt RHS-NS,
learned-rate tilt, and component-scale dynamic tilt remain deferred.
\end{abstract}

\section{Interpretive Scope}

The main article presents the statistical target and the core Gibbs sampler.
This supplement keeps the derivations and implementation contracts recoverable.
The RQR update is a loss-based generalized update
\citep{BissiriHolmesWalker2016GeneralBayes} and does not define an ordinary
response likelihood. Root-trajectory draws from a state evolution equation
remain draws of endpoint functions; they are not posterior predictive draws of
the observed response.

\section{Fixed-Content Interval Functionals}
\label{sec:supp-interval-functionals}

Suppress \(x\) and write
\[
I_c(u)=[Q(u),Q(u+c)],\qquad
M_c(u)=\frac{1}{c}\int_u^{u+c}Q(v)\,dv,\qquad
W_c(u)=Q(u+c)-Q(u).
\]
The equal-tailed interval fixes \(u_{\mathrm{ET}}=(1-c)/2\). Ordinary RQR
solves \(M_c(u_{\mathrm{RQR}})=\mu\). Define the possibly set-valued collection
of shortest contiguous windows by
\[
\mathcal U_{\mathrm{SH}}
=\argmin_{0\le u\le1-c}W_c(u).
\]
If a positive density exists at an interior \(u_{\mathrm{SH}}\in
\mathcal U_{\mathrm{SH}}\), then
\[
W_c'(u_{\mathrm{SH}})
=\frac{1}{f\{Q(u_{\mathrm{SH}}+c)\}}
-\frac{1}{f\{Q(u_{\mathrm{SH}})\}}=0,
\]
so the endpoint densities agree. For a continuous strictly unimodal density
and a unique regular shortest interval, this interval agrees with the
corresponding highest-density interval. Under multimodality, a highest-density
region can be disconnected; the present construction remains restricted to
one contiguous interval
\citep{BrehmerGneiting2021Intervals}.

Under a symmetric unimodal distribution, all three intervals coincide. Under
asymmetry, their widths need not coincide; only the shortest interval is
ordered against the other two by definition. The standard interval score is
aligned with the central equal-tailed target and is not a target-neutral score
for this comparison
\citep{GneitingRaftery2007,BrehmerGneiting2021Intervals,
FisslerEtAl2021SetValued}.

\section{Ordinary-RQR Score Derivations and Consequences}
\label{sec:supp-population-target}

Fix a covariate value \(x\), write \(a=L(x)<U(x)=b\), and define
\(I_{a,b}(Y)=\ind(a<Y<b)\). Assume
\(\E(Y^2\mid X=x)<\infty\), the conditional risk is differentiable at an
interior stationary point, differentiation and conditional expectation may be
interchanged, and the conditional distribution places no mass at \(a\) or
\(b\). Since
\[
\frac{\partial}{\partial a}(Y-a)(Y-b)=b-Y,
\qquad
\frac{\partial}{\partial b}(Y-a)(Y-b)=a-Y,
\]
the pointwise first-order conditions are
\begin{align}
\E\left[\{c-I_{a,b}(Y)\}(b-Y)\mid X=x\right]&=0,
\label{eq:pop-foc-a}\\
\E\left[\{c-I_{a,b}(Y)\}(a-Y)\mid X=x\right]&=0.
\label{eq:pop-foc-b}
\end{align}
Subtracting \eqref{eq:pop-foc-b} from \eqref{eq:pop-foc-a} and using
\(b-a>0\) gives
\[
\Pr\{a<Y<b\mid X=x\}=c.
\]
Substitution into either first-order condition then gives
\[
\E\{Y\ind(a<Y<b)\mid X=x\}=c\E(Y\mid X=x).
\]

The second equation is equivalently
\[
\E(Y\mid a<Y<b,X=x)=\E(Y\mid X=x)
\]
and
\(\operatorname{Cov}\{Y,I_{a,b}(Y)\mid X=x\}=0\). It does not imply
independence, symmetry, or equal-tailed endpoints. The coverage implication is
established by \citet{PouplinEtAl2024RQR}; the conditional first-moment
implication is a further consequence of their first-order system.

Let \(\mu_x=\E(Y\mid X=x)\). Since
\(\E(Y-\mu_x\mid X=x)=0\), the first-moment equation is also equivalent to
\begin{equation}
\int_{-\infty}^{a}(\mu_x-y)\,dF_x(y)
=
\int_b^\infty(y-\mu_x)\,dF_x(y).
\label{eq:rqr-tail-moment-balance}
\end{equation}
Thus ordinary RQR balances omitted tail probability multiplied by average
tail displacement from the conditional mean; it does not generally balance
the two omitted probabilities.

\begin{theorem}[Quantile-window identification]
\label{prop:supp-quantile-window}
Suppose, in addition, that \(F_x\) is continuous and strictly increasing on
its support, and let \(Q_x=F_x^{-1}\). Define
\[
M_{c,x}(u)=\frac{1}{c}\int_u^{u+c}Q_x(v)\,dv,
\qquad 0\le u\le1-c.
\]
Then \(M_{c,x}\) is strictly increasing, and there is a unique \(u_c(x)\)
satisfying \(M_{c,x}\{u_c(x)\}=\mu_x\). The corresponding interval is
\[
\left[Q_x\{u_c(x)\},Q_x\{u_c(x)+c\}\right].
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Modulo conditional probability-zero portions outside the support, every
canonical contiguous interval having content \(c\) has the displayed
quantile-window form. If \(v>u\), then
\[
M_{c,x}(v)-M_{c,x}(u)
=\frac{1}{c}\int_0^c
\{Q_x(v+s)-Q_x(u+s)\}\,ds>0.
\]
The average of the lowest conditional \(c\)-fraction is below \(\mu_x\), and
the average of the highest conditional \(c\)-fraction is above \(\mu_x\), for
a nondegenerate distribution with finite mean. Continuity and strict
monotonicity therefore give existence and uniqueness. The first-moment
equation identifies the stated endpoints.
\end{proof}

If \(F_x\) is symmetric about \(\mu_x\), then
\(Q_x(1-v)=2\mu_x-Q_x(v)\). The window beginning at
\((1-c)/2\) has retained mean \(\mu_x\), so uniqueness gives the equal-tailed
interval. Conversely, coincidence at one coverage level does not establish
global symmetry.

The distribution conditional on interval membership and its complementary
tail distribution both have mean \(\mu_x\). This is a first-moment statement,
not independence between \(Y\) and interval membership. It also clarifies why
the endpoint midpoint need not equal \(\mu_x\) under asymmetry.

\begin{proposition}[Mean-preserving contraction]
\label{prop:supp-convex-order}
Let \(Y_{\mathrm{in}}\) and \(Y_{\mathrm{out}}\) have the conditional
distributions of \(Y\) given \(a<Y<b\) and \(Y\notin(a,b)\), respectively,
at a regular ordinary-RQR target. For every convex function \(\varphi\) for
which the required expectations exist,
\[
\E\{\varphi(Y_{\mathrm{in}})\}
\le
\E\{\varphi(Y)\}
\le
\E\{\varphi(Y_{\mathrm{out}})\}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
Y_{\mathrm{in}}\preceq_{\mathrm{cx}}Y
\preceq_{\mathrm{cx}}Y_{\mathrm{out}}.
\]
If second moments exist, the same ordering holds for the corresponding
variances.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let \(s_\varphi\) be the affine secant through
\(\{a,\varphi(a)\}\) and \(\{b,\varphi(b)\}\). Convexity gives
\(\varphi(y)\le s_\varphi(y)\) on \([a,b]\) and
\(\varphi(y)\ge s_\varphi(y)\) outside that interval. Both conditional
distributions have mean \(\mu_x\), so
\[
\E\{\varphi(Y_{\mathrm{in}})\}
\le s_\varphi(\mu_x)
\le \E\{\varphi(Y_{\mathrm{out}})\}.
\]
The full conditional distribution is their mixture with weights \(c\) and
\(1-c\), which places its convex expectation between the two. Taking
\(\varphi(y)=(y-\mu_x)^2\) gives the variance statement.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Interval path, response scale, and tail sensitivity}

Retain the quantile-window characterization and assume, for this derivative
calculation, connected support and positive continuous density at the relevant
endpoint quantiles. Write \(u_c\) for the ordinary-RQR index, and set
\(L_c=Q(u_c)\) and \(U_c=Q(u_c+c)\). Differentiating
\[
\int_{u_c}^{u_c+c}\{Q(v)-\mu\}\,dv=0
\]
with respect to \(c\), at regular interior values, gives
\[
\frac{du_c}{dc}
=-\frac{U_c-\mu}{U_c-L_c}<0,
\qquad
\frac{d(u_c+c)}{dc}
=\frac{\mu-L_c}{U_c-L_c}>0.
\]
The ordinary-RQR population intervals are therefore nested as content
increases, with analogous one-sided statements at isolated nonsmooth support
transitions. If \(p_\mu=F(\mu)\) satisfies \(Q(p_\mu)=\mu\) and \(Q\) is
continuous at \(p_\mu\), then \(Q(u_c)\le\mu\le Q(u_c+c)\), so
\(p_\mu\in[u_c,u_c+c]\). Since this probability window has length \(c\),
\[
L_c\longrightarrow\mu,\qquad U_c\longrightarrow\mu
\quad\text{as }c\downarrow0.
\]
This zero-content anchor needs the local support condition above. It is false
under the quantile-window assumptions alone: for the symmetric distribution
with density \(1/2\) on \([-2,-1]\cup[1,2]\), \(\mu=0\) and the ordinary
window has \(u_c=(1-c)/2\), hence \(L_c=-1-c\) and \(U_c=1+c\), so the
zero-content limit straddles the support gap rather than contracting to
\(\mu\). The corrected zero-content anchor is a property of the population
interval path; the
midpoint at a fixed positive \(c\) is not generally the mean, and independent
fits at several content levels do not by themselves define joint posterior
draws of that path.

For \(Y^\star=r+sY\), \(s>0\), the ordinary endpoints transform as
\[
L_c^\star=r+sL_c,\qquad U_c^\star=r+sU_c.
\]
For the tilted family, \(\delta^\star=s\delta\) and
\[
\ell^\star_{c,\delta^\star}
(r+sa,r+sb;r+sy)
=s^2\ell_{c,\delta}(a,b;y).
\]
Thus the population target is positive-affine equivariant. A corresponding
generalized posterior uses the transformed prior and
\(\omega_{\mathrm R}^\star=\omega_{\mathrm R}/s^2\). Conversely, if the
original response is standardized as \(\widetilde Y=(Y-r)/s\), use
\(\widetilde\delta=\delta/s\) and
\(\widetilde\omega_{\mathrm R}=\omega_{\mathrm R}s^2\).
Nonlinear monotone transformations generally change the mean-preserving
functional rather than merely re-expressing it.

Finally, with fixed endpoints,
\(\rho_c\{(y-a)(y-b)\}=O(y^2)\) as \(|y|\to\infty\), and the endpoint scores
grow linearly in \(|y|\). RQR is distribution-free in the sense that it does
not posit a parametric response density, but it is not quantile-robust to
arbitrarily heavy tails. This is why the finite-second-moment assumption and
response-scale choice are scientifically consequential.

Endpoint atoms require directional conditions. Write
\(m=(a+b)/2\), \(h=(b-a)/2>0\), so
\(e=(Y-m)^2-h^2\). The left and right derivatives in \(h\) are
\[
D_-R_x(m,h)=2h\{\Pr(a<Y<b\mid x)-c\},
\]
and
\[
D_+R_x(m,h)=2h\{\Pr(a\le Y\le b\mid x)-c\}.
\]
At a local minimum, \(D_-R_x\le0\le D_+R_x\), and therefore
\[
\Pr(a<Y<b\mid X=x)\le c\le\Pr(a\le Y\le b\mid X=x).
\]
Without a separate joint subgradient and endpoint-mass allocation analysis,
these directional conditions do not imply the exact retained-mean equality
derived above under endpoint continuity.
If \(a=b=m\), then \(e=(Y-m)^2\) and the risk is
\(c\E\{(Y-m)^2\mid X=x\}\); the coincident-root target is the conditional
mean when the second moment exists.

These implications are conditional, pointwise, and assumption dependent. They
do not follow by treating estimated data-dependent endpoints as fixed. For
linear roots \(\eta_k(X)=X^\top\beta_k\), the corresponding population score
conditions are instead
\begin{align}
\E\left[X\{c-I_{\eta_1,\eta_2}(Y)\}(\eta_2-Y)\right]&=0,\\
\E\left[X\{c-I_{\eta_1,\eta_2}(Y)\}(\eta_1-Y)\right]&=0,
\end{align}
where \(I_{\eta_1,\eta_2}(Y)\) indicates that \(Y\) lies between the
unordered roots. These design-weighted projection equations need not imply
conditional coverage for every \(x\).

We do not use the finite-sample unbiasedness argument in
\citet{PouplinEtAl2024RQR}. Its Monte Carlo step treats fitted,
data-dependent endpoints as fixed, and its zero-gradient argument does not
cover nonsmooth optima at sample points coinciding with a root. A valid
finite-sample or asymptotic result requires a separate empirical-process
analysis.

\section{Mean-Tilted RQR}
\label{sec:supp-mean-tilt}

For a fixed mean tilt \(\delta\in\R\), define
\begin{equation}
\ell_{c,\delta}(m,h;y)
=\rho_c\{(y-m)^2-h^2\}-2c\delta(m-y),
\label{eq:mean-tilted-loss-midpoint}
\end{equation}
or, equivalently,
\begin{equation}
\ell_{c,\delta}(a,b;y)
=\rho_c\{(y-a)(y-b)\}-c\delta(a+b-2y).
\label{eq:mean-tilted-loss-roots}
\end{equation}
The second form is invariant to exchanging the raw root labels. The tilt has
response units so that both terms in the loss have squared-response units.
For restricted linear roots \(\eta_k(X)=X^\top\beta_k\) and an externally
fixed \(\delta(X)\), the population score equations are
\begin{align}
\E\!\left[
X\left\{\{c-I_{\eta_1,\eta_2}(Y)\}(\eta_2-Y)-c\delta(X)\right\}
\right]&=0,\\
\E\!\left[
X\left\{\{c-I_{\eta_1,\eta_2}(Y)\}(\eta_1-Y)-c\delta(X)\right\}
\right]&=0.
\end{align}
They are design-weighted projection equations; they do not imply pointwise
conditional content or retained-mean identities unless the predictor class is
sufficiently rich and the unrestricted conditions apply.

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-content mean-tilted target]
\label{prop:supp-mean-tilt}
Under the regularity conditions in
Section~\ref{sec:supp-population-target}, a nondegenerate interior
stationary point \((m_\delta,h_\delta)\) satisfies
\[
\Pr(|Y-m_\delta|<h_\delta)=c
\]
and
\[
\E(Y\mid |Y-m_\delta|<h_\delta)=\mu+\delta.
\]
If \(F\) is continuous and strictly increasing on its support and \(\delta\)
lies strictly between the endpoint values of the admissible range, the unique
interior lower-tail index is the solution of
\[
M_c(u_\delta)=\mu+\delta.
\]
Endpoint tilts are understood as boundary limits and satisfy the corresponding
one-sided stationarity conditions.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let \(I_{m,h}(Y)=\ind(|Y-m|<h)\). Away from endpoint atoms,
\begin{align}
\frac{\partial}{\partial h}\E\{\ell_{c,\delta}(m,h;Y)\}
&=2h\{\Pr(I_{m,h}=1)-c\},\\
\frac{\partial}{\partial m}\E\{\ell_{c,\delta}(m,h;Y)\}
&=2\E[(m-Y)\{c-I_{m,h}(Y)\}]-2c\delta.
\end{align}
The first equation gives content \(c\). Substitution into the second gives
\(\E\{YI_{m,h}(Y)\}=c(\mu+\delta)\), which is the retained-mean equation.
Proposition~\ref{prop:supp-quantile-window} and strict monotonicity of \(M_c\)
give the quantile-window statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Profile radius and envelope derivative]
\label{lem:supp-profile-radius}
Suppose that the support closure of \(F\) is an interval
\([\ell,r]\), where either endpoint may be infinite, \(F\) is continuous and
strictly increasing and absolutely continuous between its support endpoints,
its density is positive and continuous on the support interior, and
\(\E(Y^2)<\infty\). Extend \(F\) by zero and one beyond finite support
endpoints. For every \(m\in\R\), there is a unique \(h_c(m)>0\) satisfying
\[
\Pr\{|Y-m|<h_c(m)\}=c.
\]
The map \(m\mapsto h_c(m)\) is continuous and is differentiable whenever its
two roots are interior to the support. Define
\[
\overline R_{c,\delta}(m)
=
\E\{\ell_{c,\delta}(m,h_c(m);Y)\}.
\]
Also let \(u_c(m)=F\{m-h_c(m)\}\). Then
\begin{equation}
\overline R_{c,\delta}'(m)
=2c\left[M_c\{u_c(m)\}-\mu-\delta\right],
\label{eq:supp-profiled-risk-derivative}
\end{equation}
where both roots are interior, with the corresponding one-sided derivatives
when a root crosses a finite support endpoint.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For fixed \(m\), the map
\[
H_m(h)=F(m+h)-F(m-h),\qquad h\ge0,
\]
is continuous, begins at zero, and converges to one. Interval support and
strict increase imply that \(H_m\) passes through every value in \((0,1)\)
strictly: any plateau can occur only before the expanding interval reaches
the support or after it contains the full support. Thus \(H_m(h)=c\) has one
solution. Continuity of this inverse in \(m\) follows by the same strict
crossing argument. When both roots are interior, the implicit-function
theorem applies because
\[
\frac{\partial H_m(h)}{\partial h}
=f(m+h)+f(m-h)>0.
\]

For fixed \(m\), write \(Z_m=(Y-m)^2\) and \(q=h^2\). The subgradient interval
of \(q\mapsto\E\{\rho_c(Z_m-q)\}\) is
\[
\left[\Pr(Z_m<q)-c,\ \Pr(Z_m\le q)-c\right].
\]
Continuity makes \(q=h_c(m)^2\) its unique minimizer. On a compact
neighborhood of \(m\), the partial midpoint score is dominated by a constant
multiple of \(1+|Y|\), which is integrable. Differentiation under the
expectation is therefore valid away from the probability-zero endpoints.
The standard two-sided profiling inequalities, evaluated once at
\(h_c(m)\) and once at \(h_c(m+s)\), together with continuity of \(h_c\),
give the envelope derivative
\begin{align*}
\overline R_{c,\delta}'(m)
&=
2\E\!\left[(m-Y)
\{c-\ind(m-h_c(m)<Y<m+h_c(m))\}\right]-2c\delta\\
&=
2c\left[M_c\{u_c(m)\}-\mu-\delta\right].
\end{align*}
The same inequalities give one-sided derivatives at a finite-support
transition.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Global profiled-risk identification]
\label{prop:supp-profiled-risk}
Under the conditions of Lemma~\ref{lem:supp-profile-radius}, set
\(u_c(m)=F\{m-h_c(m)\}\), so \(u_c(m)\in[0,1-c]\). For
\[
\delta_-:=M_c(0)-\mu<\delta<
\delta_+:=M_c(1-c)-\mu,
\]
the expected loss has a unique global minimizer over finite ordered roots. Its
index \(u_\delta\in(0,1-c)\) satisfies
\[
M_c(u_\delta)=\mu+\delta.
\]
Nondegeneracy gives \(\delta_-<0<\delta_+\), so ordinary RQR is an interior
member. The two raw root-label permutations represent the same ordered
interval.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write \(a_c(m)=m-h_c(m)\) and \(b_c(m)=m+h_c(m)\). Continuity gives
\[
F\{b_c(m)\}-F\{a_c(m)\}=c,
\]
so \(F\{b_c(m)\}=u_c(m)+c\). When \(0<u_c(m)<1-c\),
\[
a_c(m)=Q\{u_c(m)\},\qquad
b_c(m)=Q\{u_c(m)+c\},
\]
and therefore
\[
m=m_c\{u_c(m)\}
:=\frac{Q\{u_c(m)\}+Q\{u_c(m)+c\}}{2}.
\]
The map \(m_c(u)\) is strictly increasing. If a root extends beyond a finite
support endpoint, \(u_c(m)\) is clamped at \(0\) or \(1-c\); hence \(u_c\) is
globally nondecreasing.

Lemma~\ref{lem:supp-profile-radius} gives
\eqref{eq:supp-profiled-risk-derivative}. Strict monotonicity of \(Q\) also
gives, for \(v>u\),
\[
M_c(v)-M_c(u)
=\frac{1}{c}\int_0^c\{Q(v+s)-Q(u+s)\}\,ds>0.
\]
For an interior admissible tilt, the profiled derivative is therefore negative
before the unique solution of \(M_c(u)=\mu+\delta\) and positive afterward.
The profiled risk has a unique global midpoint minimizer, and the preceding
fixed-\(m\) argument gives its unique positive half-width. This proves joint
global identification of the ordered roots.
\end{proof}

The admissible population tilt range is
\[
M_c(0)-\mu\le\delta\le M_c(1-c)-\mu,
\]
and both endpoint values are finite under the assumed second moment. Interior
tilts correspond one-to-one with finite-root quantile windows. At
\(\delta_- = M_c(0)-\mu\), the canonical lower boundary member is
\([Q(0),Q(c)]\). If \(Q(0)=-\infty\), it is a semi-infinite limiting interval
and is not attained by finite roots, although the corresponding
population-risk infimum is finite. If \(Q(0)\) is finite, every pair
\((a,Q(c))\) with
\(a\le Q(0)\) has the same population loss, so the inactive lower root is not
identified without a support constraint. The upper boundary has the symmetric
qualification. For
\(\delta<\delta_-\) or \(\delta>\delta_+\), the unrestricted population risk
is unbounded below along the corresponding escaping-root direction and has no
finite global target.

Ordinary and equal-tailed recovery use
\[
\delta_{\mathrm{RQR}}=0,\qquad
\delta_{\mathrm{ET}}=M_c\{(1-c)/2\}-\mu,
\]
whereas shortest-contiguous recovery is generally set-valued. Define
\[
\Delta_{\mathrm{SH}}
=
\{M_c(u)-\mu:u\in\mathcal U_{\mathrm{SH}}\}.
\]
If \(\mathcal U_{\mathrm{SH}}\) is a singleton, its element and tilt are denoted
by \(u_{\mathrm{SH}}\) and \(\delta_{\mathrm{SH}}\). These identities establish
population representation, not automatic finite-sample estimation of a
recovery tilt.

Implicit differentiation yields
\[
\frac{du_\delta}{d\delta}
=\frac{c}{W_c(u_\delta)}
\]
and
\[
\frac{dW_c(u_\delta)}{d\delta}
=\frac{c\,W_c'(u_\delta)}{W_c(u_\delta)}.
\]
Hence profiling population width along the interior admissible tilt path
identifies any interior shortest-contiguous member. Boundary members use the
canonical one-sided closure described above, and the statement does not extend
the target to disconnected highest-density regions.

\subsection{First-order Cornish--Fisher tilt anchors}
\label{sec:supp-cf-tilt}

The recovery identities also give a low-cost initialization diagnostic. Let
\(Z=(Y-\mu)/\sigma\) have small standardized skewness \(\gamma_1\), and let
\(z_p=\Phi^{-1}(p)\). The formal first-order Cornish--Fisher expansion
\[
Q_Z(p)
 \approx
z_p+\frac{\gamma_1}{6}(z_p^2-1)
\]
approximates the quantile function of \(Z\) around the standard Normal law.
For content \(c\), define
\[
q_c=\Phi^{-1}\{(1+c)/2\},\qquad
K_c=q_c\phi(q_c)/c.
\]
Integrating the expansion over the equal-tailed probability window gives
\[
d_{\mathrm{ET}}^{\mathrm{CF}}
\approx
-\frac{\gamma_1}{3}K_c,
\qquad d=\delta/\sigma.
\]
Applying the same expansion to the shortest-window first-order condition
\(W_c'(u)=0\) and then evaluating the retained-window mean gives
\[
d_{\mathrm{SH}}^{\mathrm{CF}}
\approx
-\gamma_1K_c.
\]
Thus the shortest-oriented first-order tilt is three times the equal-tailed
one on the standardized scale. The sign convention is useful for checking
implementations: right skewness gives negative recovery tilts, while left
skewness gives positive recovery tilts.

These displays are used as first-order anchors, not as a universal remainder
theorem. An \(O(\epsilon^2)\) statement is defensible only for an explicitly
indexed near-Normal family \(F_\epsilon\) whose quantile function and
derivative admit a uniform expansion
\[
Q_\epsilon(p)
=z_p+\epsilon\kappa_3(z_p^2-1)/6+r_\epsilon(p),
\qquad
\sup_p\{|r_\epsilon(p)|+|\partial_pr_\epsilon(p)|\}=O(\epsilon^2),
\]
on a neighborhood of the central window, with
\(\gamma_1(F_\epsilon)=\epsilon\kappa_3+O(\epsilon^2)\). Small skewness by
itself does not control higher cumulants or the quantile remainder.

For a known population law these formulas use the true \(\gamma_1\) and
\(\sigma\). A finite-sample plug-in version may replace them by the adjusted
Fisher--Pearson skewness \(\widehat\gamma_1\) and training standard deviation
\(\widehat\sigma\):
\[
\widehat d_{\mathrm{SH}}^{\mathrm{CF}}
=
-\widehat\gamma_1K_c,\qquad
\widehat d_{\mathrm{ET}}^{\mathrm{CF}}
=
\widehat d_{\mathrm{SH}}^{\mathrm{CF}}/3,\qquad
\widehat\delta=\widehat\sigma\,\widehat d.
\]
The plug-in quantities are fixed candidate tilts for initialization or
external validation screening. They are not posterior draws, do not sample a
tilt in the Gibbs chain, and do not justify reusing the ordinary learned-scale
update at nonzero tilt. Strong skewness, finite-support boundary solutions,
and heavy-tailed cases can move the first-order shortest approximation outside
the admissible finite-root tilt range. The deterministic population table in
the main text is therefore an approximation diagnostic rather than an
empirical validation or held-out selection rule.

The tilt indexes the estimand and is initially treated like a fixed quantile
level. A scalar tilt generally defines only a restricted global target in a
regression model; pointwise equal-tailed or shortest recovery generally
requires \(\delta(x)\). Sampling \(\delta\) as an ordinary unknown would mix
different interval functionals. Data-driven tilt selection must therefore be
an external decision or validation layer with independent test data. Under
positive affine transformation \(Y^\star=r+sY\), the corresponding target uses
\(\delta^\star=s\delta\), as recorded in
Section~\ref{sec:supp-population-target}.

These population equations do not establish finite-sample propriety under
arbitrary root priors. For a fixed learning rate and a finite-dimensional
Gaussian root prior, the ordinary RQR loss is nonnegative and the tilt is
linear in the roots. The resulting kernel is integrable because a Gaussian
density has finite exponential moments in every linear direction. This covers
the initial ridge fixed-design model and finite-horizon Gaussian state model.
Nonzero tilt under heavier-tailed shrinkage priors requires a separate
coercivity or propriety result.

\section{Static Finite-Sample Balance and Uncertainty Geometry}
\label{sec:supp-static-theory}

\subsection{Fractional empirical balance}

For intercept-only roots and fixed \(\delta\), define
\[
R_n(a,b)
=\sum_{i=1}^n
\left[
\rho_c\{(y_i-a)(y_i-b)\}
-c\delta(a+b-2y_i)
\right],
\qquad a<b.
\]
Let \((\widehat a,\widehat b)\) be an interior subgradient-stationary point.
For each observation, choose
\[
s_i\in\partial\rho_c(\widehat e_i),
\qquad
\widehat e_i=(y_i-\widehat a)(y_i-\widehat b),
\]
where \(s_i=c-1\) if \(\widehat e_i<0\), \(s_i=c\) if
\(\widehat e_i>0\), and \(s_i\in[c-1,c]\) if \(\widehat e_i=0\). Set
\(\widehat q_i=c-s_i\). Then
\(\widehat q_i=1\) strictly inside the fitted interval,
\(\widehat q_i=0\) strictly outside, and
\(\widehat q_i\in[0,1]\) only at fitted roots.

\begin{theorem}[Exact fractional empirical balance]
\label{thm:supp-fractional-balance}
At any interior subgradient-stationary intercept-only MT-RQR solution,
there are allocations \(\widehat q_i\in[0,1]\) with the membership convention
above such that
\[
\sum_{i=1}^n\widehat q_i=nc,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^n\widehat q_i y_i
=nc(\overline y+\delta).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The endpoint subgradient equations are
\[
\sum_{i=1}^n s_i(\widehat b-y_i)-nc\delta=0,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^n s_i(\widehat a-y_i)-nc\delta=0.
\]
Subtracting them and using \(\widehat b-\widehat a>0\) gives
\(\sum_i s_i=0\), hence \(\sum_i\widehat q_i=nc\). Substituting this identity
back into either endpoint equation gives
\[
\sum_{i=1}^n(\widehat q_i-c)y_i=nc\delta,
\]
which is the retained-mean balance.
\end{proof}

If \(B_n=\#\{i:\widehat e_i=0\}\), then the theorem immediately implies
\[
P_n(\widehat a<Y<\widehat b)\le c\le
P_n(\widehat a\le Y\le\widehat b),
\qquad
\left|P_n(\widehat a<Y<\widehat b)-c\right|\le B_n/n.
\]
For iid intercept-only data, Massart's sharp DKW inequality
\citep{Massart1990DKW} gives, with probability at least \(1-\alpha\),
\[
\left|\Pr_F(\widehat a<Y<\widehat b)-c\right|
\le
\frac{B_n}{n}+
\sqrt{\frac{2\log(2/\alpha)}{n}}.
\]
The bound is conservative and high probability. It is not exact nominal
future coverage and it does not extend to restricted regression without a
separate argument.

For linear roots in a globally ordered chart, the same subgradient selection
gives
\begin{align}
\sum_i X_i\left[(c-\widehat q_i)(\widehat b_i-y_i)-c\delta_i\right]&=0,\\
\sum_i X_i\left[(c-\widehat q_i)(\widehat a_i-y_i)-c\delta_i\right]&=0.
\end{align}
Subtracting yields the width-weighted calibration equation
\[
\sum_i X_i\widehat w_i(c-\widehat q_i)=0,
\qquad \widehat w_i=\widehat b_i-\widehat a_i.
\]
This is the finite-sample analogue of the restricted population score. It is
not pointwise conditional coverage unless the predictor class is correctly
specified and sufficiently rich.

\subsection{Fixed-dimensional iid uncertainty geometry}

The static asymptotic statements used for uncertainty calibration are
fixed-dimensional iid random-design statements. Let \(Z=(X,Y)\) be iid with
bounded \(X\), fixed \(c\), fixed externally declared \(\delta(X)\), and fixed
learning rate \(\omega_{\mathrm R}\). Work on an ordered chart with
\[
a_\theta(X)=X^\top\beta_L,\qquad
b_\theta(X)=X^\top\beta_U,\qquad
\inf_X\{b_{\theta_0}(X)-a_{\theta_0}(X)\}>0
\]
in a neighborhood of the unique population minimizer \(\theta_0\). Assume
finite second response moment, positive continuous conditional endpoint
densities, positive-definite curvature, global risk separation, and a prior
that is positive and continuous near \(\theta_0\). These assumptions exclude
crossing raw linear roots after pointwise sorting, deterministic fixed-design
triangular arrays, serial dependence, selected DESN reservoirs, growing
dimension, learned \(\kappa\), and same-sample tilt screening.

Let \(w_0(X)=b_{\theta_0}(X)-a_{\theta_0}(X)\) and
\[
p_0(X)=\Pr\{a_{\theta_0}(X)<Y<b_{\theta_0}(X)\mid X\}.
\]
Writing the endpoint score vector as \(\psi_0(Z)\), define
\[
J=\operatorname{Var}\{\psi_0(Z)\}.
\]
The expected-risk Hessian is
\[
H=
\begin{pmatrix}
\E\{XX^\top w_0(X)f_{Y\mid X}(a_{\theta_0}\mid X)\}
&
\E\{XX^\top[c-p_0(X)]\}\\
\E\{XX^\top[c-p_0(X)]\}
&
\E\{XX^\top w_0(X)f_{Y\mid X}(b_{\theta_0}\mid X)\}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Under the standard nonsmooth M-estimation conditions for the product-check
loss, the empirical minimizer satisfies
\[
\sqrt n(\widehat\theta-\theta_0)
\Rightarrow
\Normal(0,H^{-1}JH^{-1}).
\]
Under the corresponding generalized-posterior concentration and local
asymptotic-quadratic conditions \citep{Miller2021GeneralizedPosteriors},
the fixed-rate generalized posterior satisfies the local Bernstein--von
Mises approximation
\[
\sqrt n(\theta-\widehat\theta)\mid\vect y
\approx
\Normal\{0,(\omega_{\mathrm R}H)^{-1}\}.
\]
Thus the generalized posterior is centered at the same first-order target
but has curvature covariance, not generally the frequentist sandwich
covariance.

The full joint covariance is matched by a scalar learning rate if and only if
\[
J=\omega_{\mathrm R}^{-1}H.
\]
Otherwise different endpoint, midpoint, width, or coefficient contrasts may
prefer different scalar rates. If \(H\) and \(J\) are positive definite and
consistent estimators \(\widehat H,\widehat J\) are available, the
open-faced-sandwich post-processing transformation
\citep{Shaby2014OpenFacedSandwich}
\[
\theta^{\mathrm{adj}}
=\widehat\theta+
\widehat\Sigma_F^{1/2}\widehat\Sigma_G^{-1/2}
(\theta-\widehat\theta),
\quad
\widehat\Sigma_F=\widehat H^{-1}\widehat J\widehat H^{-1},\quad
\widehat\Sigma_G=(\omega_{\mathrm R}\widehat H)^{-1},
\]
has the desired first-order covariance for static coefficient and
differentiable endpoint-function summaries. This is a calibration of
generalized-posterior draws. It is not equivalent to declaring an ordinary
response likelihood, and it is not a dynamic-state or DESN forecasting
theorem.

\section{RQR-W and Width Regularization}
\label{sec:supp-rqr-w}

For comparison, \citet{PouplinEtAl2024RQR} use the RQR-W population objective
\[
\E\left[\rho_q\{(Y-a)(Y-b)\}\right]
+\frac{\lambda_W}{2}(b-a)^2,
\qquad \lambda_W\ge0.
\]
At a nondegenerate interior stationary point, subtracting the two endpoint
score equations gives
\[
\Pr(a<Y<b)=q-2\lambda_W.
\]
Thus \(q=c+2\lambda_W\) restores content \(c\), subject to
\(0\le\lambda_W<(1-c)/2\). The midpoint equation then gives
\[
\E(Y\mid a<Y<b)
=\mu+\frac{2\lambda_W}{c}(\mu-m),
\qquad m=\frac{a+b}{2}.
\]
Except in special symmetric cases, RQR-W therefore changes the
mean-preserving placement equation. It is a coverage-corrected compromise
between product-check risk and squared width, not a tie-breaker among
unidentified ordinary-RQR minimizers.

More generally, if a differentiable width penalty \(P(w)\), \(w=b-a\), is
added to a check loss at level \(q\), the coverage equation is
\[
\Pr(a<Y<b)=q-\frac{2P'(w)}{w}.
\]
A correction independent of the distribution and realized width is possible
only when \(P'(w)/w\) is constant, which yields a quadratic penalty up to an
additive constant. This explains both the form of RQR-W and why its path does
not generally coincide with the shortest-contiguous path.

\section{Pseudo-AL Kernel}

For \(e_i=(y_i-\eta_{1i})(y_i-\eta_{2i})\) and
\(\rho_c(e)=e\{c-\ind(e<0)\}\), a fixed-rate generalized posterior is
proportional to
\(\exp\{-\omega_{\mathrm R}\sum_{i\in\mathcal O}\rho_c(e_i)\}\) times the
prior. The following normal--exponential identity adapts the standard
asymmetric-Laplace quantile-regression augmentation
\citep{KozumiKobayashi2011Gibbs}. Setting
\[
r_i=y_i^2,\qquad
m_i^{\mathrm R}=y_i(\eta_{1i}+\eta_{2i})-\eta_{1i}\eta_{2i}
\]
gives \(r_i-m_i^{\mathrm R}=e_i\). With
\[
\sigma_{\mathrm R}=\omega_{\mathrm R}^{-1},\qquad
\xi_c=\frac{1-2c}{c(1-c)},\qquad
\phi_c=\frac{2}{c(1-c)},
\]
the normalized asymmetric-Laplace kernel in the pseudo-residual is
\begin{align}
&\frac{c(1-c)}{\sigma_{\mathrm R}}
\exp\{-\rho_c(e_i)/\sigma_{\mathrm R}\}\notag\\
&\quad=\int_0^\infty
\Normal\!\left(e_i\mid\xi_cv_i,\phi_c\sigma_{\mathrm R}v_i\right)
\Exp\!\left(v_i\mid\mathrm{rate}=1/\sigma_{\mathrm R}\right)\,dv_i.
\label{eq:pseudo-al-mixture}
\end{align}
Equation \eqref{eq:pseudo-al-mixture} is an identity for an exponentiated loss
kernel. Its left-hand side is evaluated at a residual product containing
\(y_i\); it is not a sampling density for \(y_i\). Missing sites are excluded
from \(\mathcal O\) and have no latent scale.

The latent-scale update uses the \(\GIG(\nu,a,b)\) convention proportional to
\(v^{\nu-1}\exp\{-(av+b/v)/2\}\):
\[
v_i\mid\cdot\sim
\GIG\left\{
\frac12,\
\frac{1}{2\sigma_{\mathrm R}c(1-c)},\
\frac{c(1-c)e_i^2}{2\sigma_{\mathrm R}}
\right\}.
\]
At \(e_i=0\), this reduces to
\(v_i\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(1/2,\mathrm{rate}=a/2)\). For \(e_i\ne0\), the native
implementation uses the reciprocal identity
\[
v_i^{-1}\sim\mathrm{IG}\left(\sqrt{a/b_i},a\right),
\]
where the inverse-Gaussian parameters are mean and shape.

\section{Static Root Regression}
\label{sec:supp-static-regression}

\subsection{Gaussian coefficient blocks}

Let
\(\mat W_v=\diag\{(\phi_c\sigma_{\mathrm R}v_i)^{-1}:i\in\mathcal O\}\).
Conditional on the second root, define
\[
\mat A_1=\diag(\vect y_{\mathcal O}-\vect\eta_{2,\mathcal O})
\mat X_{\mathcal O},\qquad
\vect z_1=\vect y_{\mathcal O}\circ
(\vect y_{\mathcal O}-\vect\eta_{2,\mathcal O})
-\xi_c\vect v_{\mathcal O}.
\]
For Gaussian prior canonical parameters \((\mat Q_{01},\vect h_{01})\),
\[
\mat Q_1=\mat Q_{01}+\mat A_1^\top\mat W_v\mat A_1,\qquad
\vect h_1=\vect h_{01}+\mat A_1^\top\mat W_v\vect z_1.
\]
The conditional draw is
\[
\vect\beta_1\mid\cdot\sim
\Normal(\mat Q_1^{-1}\vect h_1,\mat Q_1^{-1}).
\]
The second root uses the same update after exchanging labels. A proper ridge
prior enters through the same Gaussian canonical form. The current ordinary
conditional-Gaussian shrinkage support is an external regularized-horseshoe
adapter using the Nishimura--Suchard augmentation (RHS-NS), with separate
states for the two roots; this supplement does not assert a standalone native
RHS-NS hierarchy or nonzero-tilt propriety for that random-scale adapter.

\subsection{Fixed-rate mean-tilt consequence}

Let \(\vect\delta_{\mathcal O}\) be fixed and define
\[
\mathcal L_{n,c,\vect\delta}
=\sum_{i\in\mathcal O}\left[
\rho_c(e_i)-c\delta_i(\eta_{1i}+\eta_{2i}-2y_i)
\right].
\]
For a fixed learning rate \(\omega_{\mathrm R}\), the conditional precision
\(\mat Q_1\) above is unchanged, while its canonical vector becomes
\begin{equation}
\vect h_1^{\mathrm{MT}}
=\vect h_1+
\omega_{\mathrm R}c\mat X_{\mathcal O}^\top\vect\delta_{\mathcal O}.
\label{eq:mean-tilt-canonical-shift}
\end{equation}
Root 2 receives the same shift. The latent GIG update is unchanged because the
tilt contains no \(v_i\). Setting \(\vect\delta=\vect 0\) recovers ordinary RQR
exactly.

Equation~\eqref{eq:mean-tilt-canonical-shift} is the algebraic implementation
contract used by the fixed-rate ridge sampler. Validation must verify exact
zero-tilt equivalence, unchanged
precisions and latent-scale conditionals, root-exchange invariance, affine
equivariance with a correspondingly transformed learning rate, and
intercept-only agreement with direct numerical quadrature.

For finite \(\mathcal O\), fixed \(\omega_{\mathrm R}\), and proper Gaussian
root priors, the nonzero-tilt kernel is proper. Indeed, the ordinary loss
factor is at most one and the remaining tilt factor is the exponential of a
finite linear functional of \((\vect\beta_1,\vect\beta_2)\); a Gaussian prior
has a finite moment-generating function in every finite direction. This
argument covers ridge priors. It does not establish the corresponding result
for the external horseshoe-family adapter.

\subsection{Ordinary-only RHS-NS adapter contract}
\label{sec:supp-rhs-ns}

The ordinary fixed-design MCMC implementation also exposes a
conditional-Gaussian RHS-NS adapter
\citep{CarvalhoPolsonScott2010HS,PiironenVehtari2017RHS,
NishimuraSuchard2023SSS}. For root \(k\), let
\[
\vect\psi_k
=\{\vect\lambda_k^2,\vect\nu_k,\tau_k^2,\xi_k,\zeta_k^2\}
\]
denote its current local, auxiliary, global, and shoulder-scale state. The
current adapter requires the first design column to be a declared intercept
and excludes its coefficient from shrinkage. For each remaining coefficient,
\(j=2,\ldots,p\), the adapter supplies the diagonal conditional prior precision
\begin{equation}
d_{kj}
=\frac{1}{\tau_k^2\lambda_{kj}^2}+\frac{1}{\zeta_k^2}.
\label{eq:supp-rhs-ns-precision}
\end{equation}
The first coefficient instead receives its separately declared fixed Gaussian
precision. Substituting the resulting
\(\mat Q_{0k}(\vect\psi_k)\) into the Gaussian block above gives the
coefficient conditional; the adapter then updates \(\vect\psi_k\) conditional
on the newly drawn \(\vect\beta_k\). Root 1 and root 2 have separate,
identically specified states. Any exchangeable label swap must therefore move
\((\vect\beta_k,\vect\psi_k)\) together.

Equation~\eqref{eq:supp-rhs-ns-precision} is the verified conditional adapter
contract, not a new standalone derivation of the marginal RHS-NS prior. The
implementation delegates prior-state construction and updating to the pinned,
archive-attested reference runtime and accepts the \texttt{rhs\_ns} mode, not
its distinct generic \texttt{rhs} mode. It is MCMC-only and restricted here to
ordinary RQR, \(\vect\delta=\vect 0\). The proper-Gaussian argument for the
nonzero-tilt kernel does not extend automatically through its random-scale
mixture, so neither MT-RQR-RHS-NS propriety nor implementation is claimed.

\subsection{Frozen-feature DESN specialization}
\label{sec:supp-desn}

Let \(\mat X_{\mathrm{DESN}}\) denote the complete deterministic DESN feature
design, including any declared intercept column
\citep{Jaeger2001EchoState,GallicchioMicheliPedrelli2018DeepESNDesign,
McDermottWikle2018DeepESNUQ}. The reservoir seed, depth, units, lag window,
spectral radius, leak rate, input scaling, washout, preprocessing, tuning
decisions, and feature reducers are design variables, not posterior unknowns.
With \(\mat X=\mat X_{\mathrm{DESN}}\), the RQR-DESN readout is exactly the
static target and coefficient block above. It introduces neither a new loss
nor a separate Gibbs sampler. A proper ridge prior supports the implemented
fixed-rate nonzero-tilt block; the external RHS-NS adapter and matched
evidence remain ordinary-RQR only.

A future design \(\mat X_{\mathrm{DESN,fut}}\) must be generated by the same
frozen reservoir contract, without using held-out responses to refit the
features. Posterior summaries are ordered endpoint functions, widths, and
midpoints formed from readout draws. Neither the training nor future feature
matrix defines a response-simulation distribution. Nonzero mean tilt for a
proper Gaussian ridge readout is implemented through the static fixed-rate
scan; reservoir tuning and any tilt selection belong to an external, recorded
validation layer. Nonzero-tilt RHS-NS and VB readouts remain disabled.

\section{Learned Inverse-Loss Scale}

Let \(s_L>0\) be a declared reference loss scale and set
\(\sigma_{\mathrm R}=s_L/\kappa\). With
\(\mathcal L_{n,c}(\vartheta)=
\sum_{i\in\mathcal O}\rho_c(e_i)\), the normalized target is
\begin{equation}
\pi(\vartheta,\kappa\mid\vect y)
\propto
\pi(\vartheta)\pi_\kappa(\kappa)
\kappa^{n_{\mathrm{obs}}}
\exp\{-\kappa\mathcal L_{n,c}(\vartheta)/s_L\},
\label{eq:normalized-scale-target}
\end{equation}
where \(\vartheta\) denotes both roots and, when present, their proper
root-specific prior or evolution states. For
\(\kappa\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(a_\kappa,b_\kappa)\) in shape--rate form,
\begin{equation}
\kappa\mid\vartheta,\vect y
\sim\mathrm{Gamma}\left(a_\kappa+n_{\mathrm{obs}},
b_\kappa+\mathcal L_{n,c}(\vartheta)/s_L\right).
\label{eq:kappa-collapsed}
\end{equation}

For any proper ordinary root and prior-state law \(\pi(\vartheta)\), the joint
target in \eqref{eq:normalized-scale-target} is integrable. Indeed,
\(\mathcal L_{n,c}(\vartheta)\geq0\), so its exponential factor is at most one,
and the remaining upper bound integrates to the finite
\(n_{\mathrm{obs}}\)th moment of the Gamma prior. This argument includes the
declared ordinary RHS-NS adapter when its complete prior-state law and update
are retained. It does not cover nonzero mean tilt, whose tilted loss need not
be nonnegative.

We call \eqref{eq:normalized-scale-target} the
\emph{pseudo-residual-normalized learned-scale target}. The factor
\(\kappa^{n_{\mathrm{obs}}}\) normalizes an asymmetric-Laplace kernel in the abstract
pseudo-residual \(e_i\), not a density in \(y_i\). It is an analyst-declared
joint generalized target rather than a consequence that \(\kappa\) is an
ordinary response parameter. Coherence of a fixed-rate loss update does not by
itself establish same-data learning or calibration of this scale
\citep{LeeLiuNicholls2025LearningRate,WuMartin2023LearningRateComparison}.
Fixed-rate sensitivity grids are therefore primary. If \(s_L\) is estimated
from the training responses, the calculation convention is recorded and the
value is frozen before MCMC; this is a data-dependent empirical-Bayes
specification.

For backward compatibility, the current software stores this quantity in
fields named \texttt{lambda}. Those fields represent the manuscript's
\(\kappa\); they are unrelated to the RQR-W penalty \(\lambda_W\).

This learned-scale target applies to ordinary RQR only. The mean-tilted loss
can be negative, and parameter-independent additive constants at fixed
\(\omega_{\mathrm R}\) become consequential when the learning rate is random.
The Gamma conditionals below must not be reused for mean-tilted RQR without a
separately normalized, proper joint target. The initial mean-tilted
implementation is therefore restricted to fixed learning rates.

If one conditions additionally on \(\vect v\), the normalized augmented
conditional is
\begin{align}
\kappa\mid\vartheta,\vect v,\vect y
\sim\mathrm{Gamma}\Bigg(&a_\kappa+\frac{3n_{\mathrm{obs}}}{2},\notag\\
&b_\kappa+\frac{1}{s_L}\sum_{i\in\mathcal O}
\left[v_i+\frac{(e_i-\xi_cv_i)^2}{2\phi_cv_i}\right]\Bigg).
\label{eq:kappa-augmented}
\end{align}

The diagnostic pure-loss target omits \(\kappa^{n_{\mathrm{obs}}}\). Its collapsed shape is
\(a_\kappa\), and a compatible augmented representation has shape
\(a_\kappa+n_{\mathrm{obs}}/2\). These alternatives are different targets, not two
implementations of the same posterior.

\begin{algorithmblock}{Ordinary-RQR learned-\(\kappa\) partially collapsed scan}
\label{alg:supp-learned-kappa}
\item \textbf{Declared target and applicability.} Target
\eqref{eq:normalized-scale-target}, restricted to ordinary RQR
\((\delta=0)\). It applies to a proper Gaussian/ridge fixed design, the
ordinary RHS-NS adapter defined above, or a declared fixed-joint dynamic mode
whose root and prior-state updates are otherwise valid.
\item \textbf{Inputs and fixed quantities.} Freeze
\(c,s_L,a_\kappa,b_\kappa,\mathcal O\), the root priors, all design or
evolution quantities, iteration controls, and the label-swap rule.
\item \textbf{Initialization.} Choose finite raw roots, \(\kappa>0\), positive
placeholder latent scales at observed sites, and valid ordinary-only prior or
evolution states.
\item \textbf{One complete sweep.} Repeat the following mandatory
partial-collapse order for every warm-up and sampling iteration:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=1.8em,itemsep=0.15em,topsep=0.15em]
\item draw \(\kappa\) from \eqref{eq:kappa-collapsed} using the current roots;
\item immediately refresh every \(v_i\), \(i\in\mathcal O\), from its GIG
conditional using the new \(\kappa\);
\item update root 1 conditional on root 2 and the refreshed scales;
\item update root 2 conditional on the new root 1 and refreshed scales;
\item update every declared ordinary-only prior or evolution state; and
\item if exchangeability permits, swap the two complete raw-root blocks and
their associated states together.
\end{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Stored quantities and label handling.} Retain ordered
endpoints, widths, midpoints, losses, \(\kappa\), and complete target,
missingness, source, and swap metadata. Omit the swap unless the complete
root-specific blocks are exchangeable.
\item \textbf{Rate status.} This is the ordinary-only learned inverse-loss
scale convention. Its \(\kappa\) is neither a response variance nor an
automatic coverage-calibration parameter.
\item \textbf{Exact, implementation, and validation status.} The displayed
order is an exact partially collapsed scan for the declared target in its
validated fixed-joint modes. It is implemented for ordinary RQR and must not
be generalized to nonzero tilt by copying the Gamma update.
\end{algorithmblock}
Store draws only according to the declared retention policy. The collapsed
\(\kappa\) draw must be followed immediately by the latent-scale refresh;
skipping that refresh would define a different, invalid hybrid scan.

\section{Dynamic Linear Root-State Computation}
\label{sec:supp-dynamic}

For root \(k=1,2\), consider the DLM representation
\citep{GoncalvesMigonBastos2020DQLM}
\[
\eta_{kt}=f_t^\top\theta_{kt},\qquad
\theta_{k0}\sim\Normal(m_0,C_0),\qquad
\theta_{kt}=G_t\theta_{k,t-1}+\epsilon_{kt},\qquad
\epsilon_{kt}\sim\Normal(0,W_{kt}).
\]
Equivalently, define
\[
\alpha_t=
\begin{pmatrix}\theta_{1t}\\\theta_{2t}\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\mathcal G_t=
\begin{pmatrix}G_t&0\\0&G_t\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\mathcal W_t=
\begin{pmatrix}W_{1t}&0\\0&W_{2t}\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then \(\alpha_t=\mathcal G_t\alpha_{t-1}+\varepsilon_t\) is one stacked
Gaussian state prior. It does not induce one conditionally Gaussian
measurement update. Writing
\(\eta_{1t}=f_t^\top\theta_{1t}\) and
\(\eta_{2t}=f_t^\top\theta_{2t}\), the augmented kernel contains
\[
\left\{(y_t-\eta_{1t})(y_t-\eta_{2t})-\xi_cv_t\right\}^2.
\]
Expansion produces \(\eta_{1t}^2\eta_{2t}^2\), so its log kernel is quartic rather than
quadratic in the stacked state. A conventional joint FFBS draw is therefore
unavailable. Holding \(\eta_{2t}\) fixed makes the expression affine in
\(\eta_{1t}\), and holding \(\eta_{1t}\) fixed does the same for
\(\eta_{2t}\). Alternating the two
root-specific FFBS draws is thus an exact blocked Gibbs scan for the stated
target, not an approximation obtained by fitting the roots separately. A
general non-Gaussian joint path sampler could target the same generalized
posterior, but would give up the closed-form Gaussian FFBS blocks.

\centering

The two root paths have a stacked Gaussian prior, but the augmented

The resulting sequential FFBS steps are exact blocked-Gibbs updates for the
declared fixed-joint model. A fixed nonzero mean tilt adds observed-time

Conditional on \(\theta_{2,1:T}\), the observed latent scales, and the declared
loss scale, root 1 has
\begin{align}
z_{1t}&=H_{1t}\theta_{1t}+\varepsilon_{1t},\\
z_{1t}&=y_t(y_t-\eta_{2t})-\xi_cv_t,\\
H_{1t}&=(y_t-\eta_{2t})f_t^\top,\\
\varepsilon_{1t}&\sim\Normal(0,V_t),\qquad
V_t=\phi_c\sigma_{\mathrm R}v_t.
\end{align}
Root 2 is obtained by exchanging labels. For fixed rate,
\(\sigma_{\mathrm R}=\omega_{\mathrm R}^{-1}\); the ordinary-only learned
target instead uses \(s_L/\kappa\).

For either conditional Gaussian system, write \(m_{t-1},C_{t-1}\) for filtered
moments. The forward recursion is
\begin{align}
a_t&=G_tm_{t-1}, & P_t&=G_tC_{t-1}G_t^\top,\\
R_t&=P_t+W_t, & q_t&=H_tR_tH_t^\top+V_t,\\
A_t&=R_tH_t^\top/q_t, & m_t&=a_t+A_t(z_t-H_ta_t),\\
C_t&=R_t-A_tq_tA_t^\top.
\end{align}
For fixed-rate MT-RQR-DLM, define the observed-time canonical site
\[
g_t=\omega_{\mathrm R}c\delta_tf_t.
\]
This canonical update never divides by
\(y_t-\eta_{\mathrm{other},t}\) and remains well defined when that quantity is
zero.
Treat the displayed measurement-update mean as \(m_t^{(0)}\), then apply
\begin{equation}
m_t=m_t^{(0)}+C_tg_t
\label{eq:supp-dlm-tilt-filter-shift}
\end{equation}
before propagating to time \(t+1\); \(C_t\) is unchanged. At a missing
response, the measurement update, latent scale, and tilt site are all omitted.
Equivalently, if the ordinary conditional path has precision
\(\mathcal Q_k\) and mean \(m_k^{(0)}\), the tilted conditional mean is
\begin{equation}
m_k^{(0)}+\mathcal Q_k^{-1}g,
\label{eq:supp-dlm-tilt-path-shift}
\end{equation}
with \(g\) formed by stacking the observed \(g_t\) values and zeros elsewhere.
Equations~\eqref{eq:supp-dlm-tilt-filter-shift} and
\eqref{eq:supp-dlm-tilt-path-shift} are numerically equivalent reference
contracts for fixed \(\mat W_t\) or a frozen evolution template. They are
implemented for fixed-rate fixed-\(\mat W_t\) and frozen-template dynamic
modes. Learned-rate, adaptive-discount, and shared component-scale tilted
extensions remain disabled.

Starting from \(\theta_T\sim\Normal(m_T,C_T)\), the backward draw for
\(t=T-1,\ldots,1\) uses
\begin{align}
B_t&=C_tG_{t+1}^\top R_{t+1}^{-1},\\
m_t^\star&=m_t+B_t(\theta_{t+1}-a_{t+1}),\\
C_t^\star&=C_t-B_tR_{t+1}B_t^\top,\\
\theta_t\mid\theta_{t+1},\cdot&\sim\Normal(m_t^\star,C_t^\star).
\end{align}

For fixed evolution covariances, the implementation integrates
\(\theta_{k0}\) out of the root-path update. Thus
\[
a_{k1}=G_1m_0,\qquad
R_{k1}=G_1C_0G_1^\top+W_1,
\]
and backward sampling terminates at \(t=1\). The component-scale extension
samples \(\theta_{k0}\) separately because its first innovation is required by
the conjugate scale update.

Missing responses skip the measurement update. The implementation uses
Cholesky factorization and does not silently turn an indefinite covariance into
a positive one by taking absolute singular values. Under the default fail-fast
policy, a failed factorization or negative-eigenvalue projection stops the fit;
an exact positive-semidefinite draw with zero eigenvalues remains admissible.
The audit policy uses a declared jitter ladder and records every covariance
alteration. When a backward prior
covariance is repaired, the repaired matrix is used consistently in the gain,
smoothing covariance, and path-draw covariance. Fixed-target fits with nonzero
repair counts are not eligible for promotion-grade exact-target analyses.
Symmetry and eigenvalue materiality are evaluated relative to the actual
matrix scale, including for matrices with entries below one. Jitter is likewise
matrix-relative except for an exactly zero matrix, for which use of the
declared ladder is recorded as an absolute fallback.

\section{Component-Specific Discount Factors}

Let component discounts be \(\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_J\), with
\(0<\gamma_j\le1\), and let their state dimensions sum to \(p\). Define the
\(p\times p\) matrix \(D\) to have value
\((1-\gamma_j)/\gamma_j\) throughout the within-component block \(j\) and zero
between components. This reproduces the public \texttt{df}/\texttt{dim.df}
construction in exdqlm 1.1.0. The covariance recursion is
\[
P_t=G_tC_{t-1}G_t^\top,\qquad
W_t=D\circ P_t,\qquad
R_t=P_t+W_t,
\]
where \(\circ\) denotes an elementwise product. Thus a within-component
covariance block is inflated by \(1/\gamma_j\), whereas cross-component
covariances are not inflated.

The standalone API distinguishes three uses:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.2em]
\item \texttt{fixed\_W}: \(W_{1:T}\) is supplied and fixed. This gives exact
FFBS for the declared Gaussian state prior.
\item \texttt{discount\_template}: a reference design and reference observation
variance generate \(W_{1:T}\) once through the recursion above. The sequence is
then frozen before MCMC. This also gives exact FFBS for the resulting declared
prior, conditional on the frozen template. If the reference quantities are
estimated from the training responses, the resulting prior specification is
empirical Bayes and must be recorded as such.
\item \texttt{adaptive\_discount}: \(W_t\) is recomputed inside each conditional
filter. In RQR, \(H_t\) and \(V_t\) depend on the other root, \(v_t\), and
\(\kappa\), so the implied evolution covariance also depends on those blocks.
The current alternating kernel omits terms that would generally arise from
such dependence in a fixed joint density. This mode is therefore experimental
and is not described as exact Gibbs for the stated generalized posterior.
\end{enumerate}
The same discounts are used for both roots unless a future model deliberately
breaks their prior exchangeability.

\subsection{Incompatibility of adaptive conditional discounts}

The experimental designation can be made precise. Consider \(T=2\), \(p=1\),
\(f_t=G_t=1\), and a nonzero discount inflation
\(d_\gamma=(1-\gamma)/\gamma\).
For the root-1 conditional filter, the time-1 covariance and time-2 innovation
variance include
\[
C_{11}=\frac{R_{11}V_1}
{(y_1-\theta_{21})^2R_{11}+V_1},
\qquad
w_1(\theta_{21})=d_\gamma C_{11}.
\]
The conditional log density therefore contains
\[
-\frac12\log w_1(\theta_{21})
-\frac{(\theta_{12}-\theta_{11})^2}{2w_1(\theta_{21})},
\]
whose mixed derivative is generically
\[
\frac{\partial^2}{\partial\theta_{21}\partial\theta_{12}}
\log\pi(\vect\theta_1\mid\vect\theta_2,\cdot)
=
\frac{(\theta_{12}-\theta_{11})w_1'(\theta_{21})}
{w_1(\theta_{21})^2}\ne0.
\]
In the advertised root-2 conditional, the adaptive variance depends on
\(\theta_{11}\), not \(\theta_{12}\); its time-1 pseudo-observation does not
involve \(\theta_{12}\), and its time-2 pseudo-observation does not involve
\(\theta_{21}\). Hence the corresponding mixed derivative is zero. A positive
twice-differentiable joint density would require equality of these derivatives.
Except in degenerate cases such as \(d_\gamma=0\), the two conditional adaptive FFBS
kernels are therefore incompatible with a common smooth joint density while
retaining their advertised Gaussian forms.

\subsection{Exact ordinary-RQR shared component scales}

Partition the state into \(J\) blocks of dimensions \(d_j\), let \(S_j\)
select block \(j\), and fix positive-definite templates \(Q_{jt}\). Define
\[
W_t(\vect q)=\operatorname{blockdiag}
\{q_1Q_{1t},\ldots,q_JQ_{Jt}\},
\qquad
q_j\sim\operatorname{IG}(a_j,b_j),
\]
where the inverse-Gamma density is proportional to
\(q_j^{-a_j-1}\exp(-b_j/q_j)\). The same \(q_j\) is shared by the two roots.
For
\[
d_{kjt}=S_j\{\theta_{kt}-G_t\theta_{k,t-1}\},
\]
the full conditional is
\[
q_j\mid\cdot\sim\operatorname{IG}\left(
a_j+Td_j,
b_j+\frac12\sum_{k=1}^2\sum_{t=1}^T
d_{kjt}^\top Q_{jt}^{-1}d_{kjt}
\right).
\]
The \(Td_j\) shape increment includes two roots, each contributing
\(Td_j/2\).

Because the fixed-\(W\) FFBS implementation integrates out the time-zero
state, the component-scale sampler restores it after each root-path draw. Its
conditional moments are
\begin{align}
C_{k0}^\star
&=\{C_0^{-1}+G_1^\top W_1(\vect q)^{-1}G_1\}^{-1},\\
m_{k0}^\star
&=C_{k0}^\star\{C_0^{-1}m_0+
G_1^\top W_1(\vect q)^{-1}\theta_{k1}\},
\end{align}
and
\[
\theta_{k0}\mid\theta_{k1},\vect q
\sim\Normal(m_{k0}^\star,C_{k0}^\star).
\]

The implemented transition first reduces scale--trajectory dependence by
integrating one root path. Conditional on root 2 and the current latent scales,
the root-1 pseudo-observation equation has
\[
z_{1t}=y_t(y_t-\eta_{2t})-\xi_cv_t,\qquad
\vect h_{1t}=(y_t-\eta_{2t})\vect f_t,
\]
with observation variance \(V_t\); missing observations are omitted from the
filter update. Let \(\ell_{1,\mathrm K}(\vect q)\) denote the resulting
Gaussian Kalman-filter log marginal after integrating
\((\vect\theta_{1,1:T},\vect\theta_{10})\) under the initial law
\(\Normal(m_0,C_0)\). For the conditioned root, define
\[
E_{2j}=\frac12\sum_{t=1}^T
d_{2jt}^\top Q_{jt}^{-1}d_{2jt}.
\]
On the log scale \(x_j=\log q_j\), the partially collapsed scale kernel is,
up to a constant,
\begin{equation}
\log\pi(\vect x\mid
\vect\theta_{2,0:T},\vect v,\ldots)
=
\ell_{1,\mathrm K}(\exp\vect x)
-\sum_{j=1}^J\left[
\left(a_j+\frac{Td_j}{2}\right)x_j+
(b_j+E_{2j})e^{-x_j}
\right].
\label{eq:supp-component-scale-partial-collapse}
\end{equation}
Only the conditioned root contributes the \(Td_j/2\) innovation
normalization and \(E_{2j}\) term; the integrated root contributes through
\(\ell_{1,\mathrm K}\). Coordinate slice transitions leave
\eqref{eq:supp-component-scale-partial-collapse} invariant. Conditional on
the accepted scales, FFBS then redraws root 1 and its time-zero state. The
sampler next exchanges the root indices: it constructs
\((z_{2t},\vect h_{2t})\) conditional on the refreshed root 1, replaces
\((\ell_{1,\mathrm K},E_{2j})\) in
\eqref{eq:supp-component-scale-partial-collapse} by
\((\ell_{2,\mathrm K},E_{1j})\), updates \(\vect q\), and redraws root 2 and
its time-zero state. Each marginal scale transition followed by its
conditional path redraw is invariant for the same augmented generalized
posterior; hence their root-symmetric composition is invariant. Each finite
slice sweep is a Markov transition rather than an independent conditional
draw.

The centered inverse-Gamma update can mix slowly when a component is nearly
static because its scale is strongly coupled to the realized state
innovations. The implemented confirmatory kernel therefore uses
ancillarity--sufficiency interweaving
\citep{PapaspiliopoulosRobertsSkold2007,YuMeng2011ASIS}. Define
\[
u_{kjt}=d_{kjt}/\sqrt{q_j}.
\]
Conditional on \(\theta_{k0}\), the complete path is reconstructed
recursively from
\[
\theta_{kt}
=G_t\theta_{k,t-1}
+\sum_{j=1}^J S_j^\top\sqrt{q_j}\,u_{kjt}.
\]
The two-root change of variables contributes
\(\prod_j q_j^{Td_j}\), which cancels the
\(\prod_j q_j^{-Td_j}\) scale factor from the two Gaussian innovation
densities. On the log scale \(x_j=\log q_j\), the remaining prior and
Jacobian contribution is
\[
-\sum_{j=1}^J\{a_jx_j+b_j\exp(-x_j)\}.
\]
Holding \(\vect u_1,\vect u_2,\theta_{10},\theta_{20}\), and the RQR latent
scales fixed, the noncentered log conditional is therefore, up to a constant,
\[
\log\pi(\vect x\mid\cdot)
=-\sum_{j=1}^J\{a_jx_j+b_j\exp(-x_j)\}
-\frac12\sum_{t:y_t\ {\rm observed}}
\frac{\{e_t(\vect x)-\xi_cv_t\}^2}{V_t},
\qquad
V_t=\phi_c\sigma_{\mathrm R}v_t,
\]
where \(e_t(\vect x)\) is evaluated after reconstructing both root paths.
This expression is a transformed augmented loss kernel, not a response
likelihood.

For the implemented ordinary learned-rate mode,
\(\sigma_{\mathrm R}=s_L/\kappa\). For fixed-rate ordinary or mean-tilted RQR,
\(\sigma_{\mathrm R}=\omega_{\mathrm R}^{-1}\). In the latter nonzero-tilt
case, the noncentered log conditional would additionally contain
\begin{equation}
\omega_{\mathrm R}c
\sum_{t\in\mathcal O}\delta_t
\{\eta_{1t}(\vect x)+\eta_{2t}(\vect x)-2y_t\}.
\label{eq:supp-tilted-component-scale-asis}
\end{equation}
Although the centered inverse-Gamma conditional given the reconstructed paths
is unchanged, the ASIS slice target is therefore not the ordinary expression:
the tilt depends on \(\vect q\) through the reconstructed states. The current
component-scale implementation is declared only for \(\delta=0\), evaluates
the ordinary target, and rejects nonzero tilt. Its tilted propriety and
interweaving invariance, including
\eqref{eq:supp-tilted-component-scale-asis}, require separate validation.

Each \(x_j\) is updated by a univariate stepping-out slice step with the other
log scales fixed. The finite stepping-out budget uses the standard randomized
left--right allocation, and the shrinkage step retains only points above the
sampled slice height. One interweaving cycle first draws \(\vect q\) from the
centered inverse-Gamma conditional, computes the standardized innovations,
applies two noncentered coordinate sweeps, and reconstructs both paths. The
centered Gibbs update and the noncentered reparameterized update each leave the
same joint generalized posterior invariant, so their composition is also
invariant. The time-zero states are held fixed during the noncentered step.
Conditional shape and rate values saved for diagnostics are recomputed from
the final retained paths.

One ordinary-RQR sweep that leaves the declared augmented target invariant
updates collapsed \(\kappa\) when it is learned,
refreshes \(\vect v\), applies the root-1 partially collapsed scale/path
transition in \eqref{eq:supp-component-scale-partial-collapse}, applies its
root-2 counterpart conditional on the refreshed root 1, applies the centered
component-scale and noncentered updates in the interweaving transition, and
optionally swaps the two complete root labels. Static \(q_j=0\) components are
separate degenerate models and are not part of the inverse-Gamma support.

\section{Reproducibility and Evidence Scope}

The repository separates mathematical target status, numerical execution, and
reproducibility eligibility. Reference checks cover the Gaussian filtering and
smoothing algebra, latent- and component-scale conditionals, missing
observations, root labels, and exact checkpoint continuation. A versioned run
manifest records the data-generating mechanism, forecast design, seeds,
numerical policy, package versions, and source commits. Heavy paths and fitted
objects remain in ignored local output directories.

These computational checks establish agreement with declared targets on their
specific fixtures. They do not establish frequentist calibration, comparative
forecasting performance, or a response-predictive distribution. Empirical
evaluation therefore reports held-out RQR loss, empirical coverage error,
width at comparable coverage, and oracle endpoint recovery when the population
roots are available. The equal-tailed interval score is retained as a
secondary, target-specific comparison. Simulation estimands, data-generating
factors, Monte Carlo precision, failure handling, and reporting rules are
prespecified \citep{MorrisWhiteCrowther2019Simulation}.

The implemented validation program is deepest for ordinary RQR
(\(\delta=0\)). Fixed-rate nonzero mean tilt currently has implementation
checks for the Gaussian information shift, zero-tilt equivalence, and the
declared ridge/static and fixed-joint dynamic gates. It still requires
independent small-target numerical references, recovery at prespecified
population tilts, and externally evaluated tilt selection before comparative
scientific evidence is claimed.
Detailed implementation status, runtime attestations, gate results, and
withheld-evidence decisions are maintained in the repository audit notes
rather than in the statistical supplement.
