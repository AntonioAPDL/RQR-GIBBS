\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This supplement records the mean-preserving RQR population target, its
comparison with equal-tailed and shortest-contiguous intervals, a fixed
mean-tilted extension, the pseudo-AL augmentation, the partially collapsed
ordinary-RQR learned-scale sampler, fixed-design Gaussian blocks, dynamic FFBS
equations, component-discount contracts, and reproducibility gates for the
standalone RQR-GIBBS article.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope}

The main article presents the statistical target and the core Gibbs sampler.
This supplement keeps the derivations and implementation contracts recoverable.
The RQR update is loss based and does not define an ordinary response
likelihood. Root-trajectory draws from a state evolution equation remain draws
of endpoint functions; they are not posterior predictive draws of the observed
response.

\section{Population Target and Restricted Score Equations}
\label{sec:supp-population-target}

Fix a covariate value \(x\), write \(a=L(x)<U(x)=b\), and define
\(I_{a,b}(Y)=\ind(a<Y<b)\). Assume
\(\E(Y^2\mid X=x)<\infty\), the conditional risk is differentiable at an
interior stationary point, differentiation and conditional expectation may be
interchanged, and the conditional distribution places no mass at \(a\) or
\(b\). Since
\[
\frac{\partial}{\partial a}(Y-a)(Y-b)=b-Y,
\qquad
\frac{\partial}{\partial b}(Y-a)(Y-b)=a-Y,
\]
the pointwise first-order conditions are
\begin{align}
\E\left[\{c-I_{a,b}(Y)\}(b-Y)\mid X=x\right]&=0,
\label{eq:pop-foc-a}\\
\E\left[\{c-I_{a,b}(Y)\}(a-Y)\mid X=x\right]&=0.
\label{eq:pop-foc-b}
\end{align}
Subtracting \eqref{eq:pop-foc-b} from \eqref{eq:pop-foc-a} and using
\(b-a>0\) gives
\[
\Pr\{a<Y<b\mid X=x\}=c.
\]
Substitution into either first-order condition then gives
\[
\E\{Y\ind(a<Y<b)\mid X=x\}=c\E(Y\mid X=x).
\]

The second equation is equivalently
\[
\E(Y\mid a<Y<b,X=x)=\E(Y\mid X=x)
\]
and
\(\operatorname{Cov}\{Y,I_{a,b}(Y)\mid X=x\}=0\). It does not imply
independence, symmetry, or equal-tailed endpoints. The coverage implication is
established by \citet{PouplinEtAl2024RQR}; the conditional first-moment
implication is a further consequence of their first-order system.

Let \(\mu_x=\E(Y\mid X=x)\). Since
\(\E(Y-\mu_x\mid X=x)=0\), the first-moment equation is also equivalent to
\[
\int_{-\infty}^{a}(\mu_x-y)\,dF_x(y)
=
\int_b^\infty(y-\mu_x)\,dF_x(y).
\label{eq:rqr-tail-moment-balance}
\]
Thus ordinary RQR balances omitted tail probability multiplied by average
tail displacement from the conditional mean; it does not generally balance
the two omitted probabilities.

\begin{proposition}[Quantile-window identification]
\label{prop:supp-quantile-window}
Suppose, in addition, that \(F_x\) is continuous and strictly increasing, and
let \(Q_x=F_x^{-1}\). Define
\[
M_{c,x}(u)=\frac{1}{c}\int_u^{u+c}Q_x(v)\,dv,
\qquad 0\le u\le1-c.
\]
Then \(M_{c,x}\) is strictly increasing, and there is a unique \(u_c(x)\)
satisfying \(M_{c,x}\{u_c(x)\}=\mu_x\). The corresponding interval is
\[
\left[Q_x\{u_c(x)\},Q_x\{u_c(x)+c\}\right].
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every contiguous interval having conditional content \(c\) has the displayed
quantile-window form. On the interior of its domain,
\[
M_{c,x}'(u)
=\frac{Q_x(u+c)-Q_x(u)}{c}>0.
\]
The average of the lowest conditional \(c\)-fraction is below \(\mu_x\), and
the average of the highest conditional \(c\)-fraction is above \(\mu_x\), for
a nondegenerate distribution with finite mean. Continuity and strict
monotonicity therefore give existence and uniqueness. The first-moment
equation identifies the stated endpoints.
\end{proof}

If \(F_x\) is symmetric about \(\mu_x\), then
\(Q_x(1-v)=2\mu_x-Q_x(v)\). The window beginning at
\((1-c)/2\) has retained mean \(\mu_x\), so uniqueness gives the equal-tailed
interval. Conversely, coincidence at one coverage level does not establish
global symmetry.

The distribution conditional on interval membership and its complementary
tail distribution both have mean \(\mu_x\). This is a first-moment statement,
not independence between \(Y\) and interval membership. It also clarifies why
the endpoint midpoint need not equal \(\mu_x\) under asymmetry.

\begin{proposition}[Mean-preserving contraction]
\label{prop:supp-convex-order}
Let \(Y_{\mathrm{in}}\) and \(Y_{\mathrm{out}}\) have the conditional
distributions of \(Y\) given \(a<Y<b\) and \(Y\notin(a,b)\), respectively,
at a regular ordinary-RQR target. For every convex function \(\varphi\) for
which the required expectations exist,
\[
\E\{\varphi(Y_{\mathrm{in}})\}
\le
\E\{\varphi(Y)\}
\le
\E\{\varphi(Y_{\mathrm{out}})\}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
Y_{\mathrm{in}}\preceq_{\mathrm{cx}}Y
\preceq_{\mathrm{cx}}Y_{\mathrm{out}}.
\]
If second moments exist, the same ordering holds for the corresponding
variances.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let \(s_\varphi\) be the affine secant through
\(\{a,\varphi(a)\}\) and \(\{b,\varphi(b)\}\). Convexity gives
\(\varphi(y)\le s_\varphi(y)\) on \([a,b]\) and
\(\varphi(y)\ge s_\varphi(y)\) outside that interval. Both conditional
distributions have mean \(\mu_x\), so
\[
\E\{\varphi(Y_{\mathrm{in}})\}
\le s_\varphi(\mu_x)
\le \E\{\varphi(Y_{\mathrm{out}})\}.
\]
The full conditional distribution is their mixture with weights \(c\) and
\(1-c\), which places its convex expectation between the two. Taking
\(\varphi(y)=(y-\mu_x)^2\) gives the variance statement.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Interval path, response scale, and tail sensitivity}

Retain the assumptions of
Proposition~\ref{prop:supp-quantile-window}, write \(u_c\) for the ordinary-RQR
index, and set \(L_c=Q(u_c)\) and \(U_c=Q(u_c+c)\). Differentiating
\[
\int_{u_c}^{u_c+c}\{Q(v)-\mu\}\,dv=0
\]
with respect to \(c\) gives
\[
\frac{du_c}{dc}
=-\frac{U_c-\mu}{U_c-L_c}<0,
\qquad
\frac{d(u_c+c)}{dc}
=\frac{\mu-L_c}{U_c-L_c}>0.
\]
The ordinary-RQR population intervals are therefore nested as content
increases. Moreover, \(Q(u_c)<\mu<Q(u_c+c)\), so
\(F(\mu)\in(u_c,u_c+c)\). It follows directly that
\[
L_c\longrightarrow\mu,\qquad U_c\longrightarrow\mu
\quad\text{as }c\downarrow0.
\]
This zero-content anchor is a property of the population interval path; the
midpoint at a fixed positive \(c\) is not generally the mean, and independent
fits at several content levels do not by themselves define joint posterior
draws of that path.

For \(Y^\star=r+sY\), \(s>0\), the ordinary endpoints transform as
\[
L_c^\star=r+sL_c,\qquad U_c^\star=r+sU_c.
\]
For the tilted family, \(\delta^\star=s\delta\) and
\[
\ell^\star_{c,\delta^\star}
(r+sa,r+sb;r+sy)
=s^2\ell_{c,\delta}(a,b;y).
\]
Thus the population target is positive-affine equivariant. A corresponding
generalized posterior uses the transformed prior and
\(\omega_{\mathrm R}^\star=\omega_{\mathrm R}/s^2\). Conversely, if the
original response is standardized as \(\widetilde Y=(Y-r)/s\), use
\(\widetilde\delta=\delta/s\) and
\(\widetilde\omega_{\mathrm R}=\omega_{\mathrm R}s^2\).
Nonlinear monotone transformations generally change the mean-preserving
functional rather than merely re-expressing it.

Finally, with fixed endpoints,
\(\rho_c\{(y-a)(y-b)\}=O(y^2)\) as \(|y|\to\infty\), and the endpoint scores
grow linearly in \(|y|\). RQR is distribution-free in the sense that it does
not posit a parametric response density, but it is not quantile-robust to
arbitrarily heavy tails. This is why the finite-second-moment assumption and
response-scale choice are scientifically consequential.

Endpoint atoms require directional conditions. Write
\(m=(a+b)/2\), \(h=(b-a)/2>0\), so
\(e=(Y-m)^2-h^2\). The left and right derivatives in \(h\) are
\[
D_-R_x(m,h)=2h\{\Pr(a<Y<b\mid x)-c\},
\]
and
\[
D_+R_x(m,h)=2h\{\Pr(a\le Y\le b\mid x)-c\}.
\]
At a local minimum, \(D_-R_x\le0\le D_+R_x\), and therefore
\[
\Pr(a<Y<b\mid X=x)\le c\le\Pr(a\le Y\le b\mid X=x).
\]
If \(a=b=m\), then \(e=(Y-m)^2\) and the risk is
\(c\E\{(Y-m)^2\mid X=x\}\); the coincident-root target is the conditional
mean when the second moment exists.

These implications are conditional, pointwise, and assumption dependent. They
do not follow by treating estimated data-dependent endpoints as fixed. For
linear roots \(\eta_k(X)=X^\top\beta_k\), the corresponding population score
conditions are instead
\begin{align}
\E\left[X\{c-I_{\eta_1,\eta_2}(Y)\}(\eta_2-Y)\right]&=0,\\
\E\left[X\{c-I_{\eta_1,\eta_2}(Y)\}(\eta_1-Y)\right]&=0,
\end{align}
where \(I_{\eta_1,\eta_2}(Y)\) indicates that \(Y\) lies between the
unordered roots. These design-weighted projection equations need not imply
conditional coverage for every \(x\).

We do not use the finite-sample unbiasedness argument in
\citet{PouplinEtAl2024RQR}. Its Monte Carlo step treats fitted,
data-dependent endpoints as fixed, and its zero-gradient argument does not
cover nonsmooth optima at sample points coinciding with a root. A valid
finite-sample or asymptotic result requires a separate empirical-process
analysis.

\section{Fixed-Coverage Interval Functionals}
\label{sec:supp-interval-functionals}

Suppress \(x\) and write
\[
I_c(u)=[Q(u),Q(u+c)],\qquad
M_c(u)=\frac{1}{c}\int_u^{u+c}Q(v)\,dv,\qquad
W_c(u)=Q(u+c)-Q(u).
\]
The equal-tailed interval fixes \(u_{\mathrm{ET}}=(1-c)/2\). Ordinary RQR
solves \(M_c(u_{\mathrm{RQR}})=\mu\). The shortest contiguous interval solves
\[
u_{\mathrm{SH}}\in\argmin_{0\le u\le1-c}W_c(u).
\]
If a positive density exists at an interior shortest solution, then
\[
W_c'(u_{\mathrm{SH}})
=\frac{1}{f\{Q(u_{\mathrm{SH}}+c)\}}
-\frac{1}{f\{Q(u_{\mathrm{SH}})\}}=0,
\]
so the endpoint densities agree. For a continuous unimodal density, this
interval agrees with the corresponding highest-density interval. Under
multimodality, a highest-density region can be disconnected; the present
construction remains restricted to one contiguous interval
\citep{BrehmerGneiting2021Intervals}.

Under a symmetric unimodal distribution, all three intervals coincide. Under
asymmetry, they generally differ. The shortest interval is no wider than the
other two by definition, but there is no universal width ordering between
ordinary RQR and the equal-tailed interval. The standard interval score is
aligned with the central equal-tailed target and is not a target-neutral score
for this comparison \citep{GneitingRaftery2007,BrehmerGneiting2021Intervals}.

\section{Mean-Tilted RQR}
\label{sec:supp-mean-tilt}

For a fixed mean tilt \(\delta\in\R\), define
\[
\ell_{c,\delta}(m,h;y)
=\rho_c\{(y-m)^2-h^2\}-2c\delta(m-y),
\label{eq:mean-tilted-loss-midpoint}
\]
or, equivalently,
\[
\ell_{c,\delta}(a,b;y)
=\rho_c\{(y-a)(y-b)\}-c\delta(a+b-2y).
\label{eq:mean-tilted-loss-roots}
\]
The second form is invariant to exchanging the raw root labels. The tilt has
response units so that both terms in the loss have squared-response units.

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-coverage mean-tilted target]
\label{prop:supp-mean-tilt}
Under the regularity conditions in
Section~\ref{sec:supp-population-target}, a nondegenerate interior
stationary point \((m_\delta,h_\delta)\) satisfies
\[
\Pr(|Y-m_\delta|<h_\delta)=c
\]
and
\[
\E(Y\mid |Y-m_\delta|<h_\delta)=\mu+\delta.
\]
If \(F\) is continuous and strictly increasing and \(\delta\) lies strictly
between the endpoint values of the admissible range, the unique interior
lower-tail index is the solution of
\[
M_c(u_\delta)=\mu+\delta.
\]
Endpoint tilts are understood as boundary limits and satisfy the corresponding
one-sided stationarity conditions.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let \(I_{m,h}(Y)=\ind(|Y-m|<h)\). Away from endpoint atoms,
\begin{align}
\frac{\partial}{\partial h}\E\{\ell_{c,\delta}(m,h;Y)\}
&=2h\{\Pr(I_{m,h}=1)-c\},\\
\frac{\partial}{\partial m}\E\{\ell_{c,\delta}(m,h;Y)\}
&=2\E[(m-Y)\{c-I_{m,h}(Y)\}]-2c\delta.
\end{align}
The first equation gives content \(c\). Substitution into the second gives
\(\E\{YI_{m,h}(Y)\}=c(\mu+\delta)\), which is the retained-mean equation.
Proposition~\ref{prop:supp-quantile-window} and strict monotonicity of \(M_c\)
give the quantile-window statement.
\end{proof}

The admissible population tilt range is
\[
M_c(0)-\mu\le\delta\le M_c(1-c)-\mu,
\]
with the appropriate extended-real interpretation when a tail average is
infinite. Interior tilts correspond one-to-one with interior quantile windows;
the full closed interval family is obtained by adjoining the feasible boundary
limits. The special oracle tilts are
\[
\delta_{\mathrm{RQR}}=0,\qquad
\delta_{\mathrm{ET}}=M_c\{(1-c)/2\}-\mu,
\]
and
\[
\delta_{\mathrm{SH}}=M_c(u_{\mathrm{SH}})-\mu.
\]
These equalities establish population representation, not automatic
finite-sample estimation of either recovery tilt.

Implicit differentiation yields
\[
\frac{du_\delta}{d\delta}
=\frac{c}{W_c(u_\delta)}
\]
and
\[
\frac{dW_c(u_\delta)}{d\delta}
=\frac{c\,W_c'(u_\delta)}{W_c(u_\delta)}.
\]
Hence profiling population width along the admissible tilt path recovers the
shortest contiguous interval. Boundary solutions require one-sided
conditions, and this statement does not extend the target to disconnected
highest-density regions.

The tilt indexes the estimand and is initially treated like a fixed quantile
level. A scalar tilt generally defines only a restricted global target in a
regression model; pointwise equal-tailed or shortest recovery generally
requires \(\delta(x)\). Sampling \(\delta\) as an ordinary unknown would mix
different interval functionals. Data-driven tilt selection must therefore be
an external decision or validation layer with independent test data. Under
positive affine transformation \(Y^\star=r+sY\), the corresponding target uses
\(\delta^\star=s\delta\), as recorded in
Section~\ref{sec:supp-population-target}.

These population equations do not establish finite-sample propriety under
arbitrary root priors. For a fixed learning rate and a finite-dimensional
Gaussian root prior, the ordinary RQR loss is nonnegative and the tilt is
linear in the roots. The resulting kernel is integrable because a Gaussian
density has finite exponential moments in every linear direction. This covers
the initial ridge fixed-design model and finite-horizon Gaussian state model.
Nonzero tilt under heavier-tailed shrinkage priors requires a separate
coercivity or propriety result.

\section{RQR-W and Width Regularization}
\label{sec:supp-rqr-w}

For comparison, the RQR-W population objective in the original RQR article is
\[
\E\left[\rho_q\{(Y-a)(Y-b)\}\right]
+\frac{\lambda}{2}(b-a)^2,
\qquad \lambda\ge0.
\]
At a nondegenerate interior stationary point, subtracting the two endpoint
score equations gives
\[
\Pr(a<Y<b)=q-2\lambda.
\]
Thus \(q=c+2\lambda\) restores content \(c\), subject to
\(0\le\lambda<(1-c)/2\). The midpoint equation then gives
\[
\E(Y\mid a<Y<b)
=\mu+\frac{2\lambda}{c}(\mu-m),
\qquad m=\frac{a+b}{2}.
\]
Except in special symmetric cases, RQR-W therefore changes the
mean-preserving placement equation. It is a coverage-corrected compromise
between product-check risk and squared width, not a tie-breaker among
unidentified ordinary-RQR minimizers.

More generally, if a differentiable width penalty \(P(w)\), \(w=b-a\), is
added to a check loss at level \(q\), the coverage equation is
\[
\Pr(a<Y<b)=q-\frac{2P'(w)}{w}.
\]
A correction independent of the distribution and realized width is possible
only when \(P'(w)/w\) is constant, which yields a quadratic penalty up to an
additive constant. This explains both the form of RQR-W and why its path does
not generally coincide with the shortest-contiguous path.

\section{Pseudo-AL Kernel}

For \(e_t=(y_t-\eta_{1t})(y_t-\eta_{2t})\) and
\(\rho_c(e)=e\{c-\ind(e<0)\}\), a fixed-rate generalized posterior is
proportional to
\(\exp\{-\omega_{\mathrm R}\sum_t\rho_c(e_t)\}\) times the prior. Setting
\[
r_t=y_t^2,\qquad
m_t^{\mathrm R}=y_t(\eta_{1t}+\eta_{2t})-\eta_{1t}\eta_{2t}
\]
gives \(r_t-m_t^{\mathrm R}=e_t\). With
\[
\sigma_{\mathrm R}=\omega_{\mathrm R}^{-1},\qquad
\xi_c=\frac{1-2c}{c(1-c)},\qquad
\phi_c=\frac{2}{c(1-c)},
\]
the normalized asymmetric-Laplace kernel in the pseudo-residual is
\begin{align}
&\frac{c(1-c)}{\sigma_{\mathrm R}}
\exp\{-\rho_c(e_t)/\sigma_{\mathrm R}\}\notag\\
&\quad=\int_0^\infty
\Normal\!\left(e_t\mid\xi_cv_t,\phi_c\sigma_{\mathrm R}v_t\right)
\Exp\!\left(v_t\mid\mathrm{rate}=1/\sigma_{\mathrm R}\right)\,dv_t.
\label{eq:pseudo-al-mixture}
\end{align}
Equation \eqref{eq:pseudo-al-mixture} is an identity for an exponentiated loss
kernel. Its left-hand side is evaluated at a residual product containing
\(y_t\); it is not a sampling density for \(y_t\).

The latent-scale update uses the \(\GIG(\nu,a,b)\) convention proportional to
\(v^{\nu-1}\exp\{-(av+b/v)/2\}\):
\[
v_t\mid\cdot\sim
\GIG\left\{
\frac12,\
\frac{1}{2\sigma_{\mathrm R}c(1-c)},\
\frac{c(1-c)e_t^2}{2\sigma_{\mathrm R}}
\right\}.
\]
At \(e_t=0\), this reduces to
\(v_t\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(1/2,\mathrm{rate}=a/2)\). For \(e_t\ne0\), the native
implementation uses the reciprocal identity
\[
v_t^{-1}\sim\mathrm{IG}\left(\sqrt{a/b_t},a\right),
\]
where the inverse-Gaussian parameters are mean and shape.

\section{Fixed-Design Gaussian Blocks}

Let \(W_v=\diag\{(\phi_c\sigma_{\mathrm R}v_t)^{-1}\}\). Conditional on the
second root, define
\[
\mat A_1=\diag(\vect y-\vect\eta_2)\mat X,\qquad
\vect z_1=\vect y\circ(\vect y-\vect\eta_2)-\xi_c\vect v.
\]
For Gaussian prior canonical parameters \((\mat Q_{01},\vect h_{01})\),
\[
\mat Q_1=\mat Q_{01}+\mat A_1^\top W_v\mat A_1,\qquad
\vect h_1=\vect h_{01}+\mat A_1^\top W_v\vect z_1.
\]
The conditional draw is
\[
\vect\beta_1\mid\cdot\sim
\Normal(\mat Q_1^{-1}\vect h_1,\mat Q_1^{-1}).
\]
The second root uses the same update after exchanging labels. Ridge and
\(\RHS\) priors enter through the same Gaussian canonical form, with separate
shrinkage states for each root. The current native dynamic implementation uses
Gaussian state priors; fixed-design RHS-family support remains an explicit
adapter to the pinned exdqlm implementation until it is promoted natively.

\subsection{Fixed-rate mean-tilt consequence}

Let \(\vect\delta=(\delta_1,\ldots,\delta_n)^\top\) be fixed and define
\[
\mathcal L_{n,c,\vect\delta}
=\sum_{i=1}^n\left[
\rho_c(e_i)-c\delta_i(\eta_{1i}+\eta_{2i}-2y_i)
\right].
\]
For a fixed learning rate \(\omega_{\mathrm R}\), the conditional precision
\(\mat Q_1\) above is unchanged, while its canonical vector becomes
\[
\vect h_1^{\mathrm{MT}}
=\vect h_1+\omega_{\mathrm R}c\mat X^\top\vect\delta.
\label{eq:mean-tilt-canonical-shift}
\]
Root 2 receives the same shift. The latent GIG update is unchanged because the
tilt contains no \(v_i\). Setting \(\vect\delta=\vect 0\) recovers ordinary RQR
exactly.

Equation~\eqref{eq:mean-tilt-canonical-shift} is an algebraic implementation
contract, not evidence that mean tilt is present in the current package. A
valid implementation must verify exact zero-tilt equivalence, unchanged
precisions and latent-scale conditionals, root-exchange invariance, affine
equivariance with a correspondingly transformed learning rate, and
intercept-only agreement with direct numerical quadrature.

\section{Learned Inverse-Loss Scale}

Let \(s_L>0\) be a declared reference loss scale and set
\(\sigma_{\mathrm R}=s_L/\lambda\). The normalized target is
\[
\pi(\vartheta,\lambda\mid\vect y)
\propto
\pi(\vartheta)\pi_\lambda(\lambda)
\lambda^n\exp\{-\lambda\mathcal L_{n,c}(\vartheta)/s_L\},
\label{eq:normalized-scale-target}
\]
where \(\vartheta\) denotes both roots. For
\(\lambda\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(a_\lambda,b_\lambda)\) in shape--rate form,
\[
\lambda\mid\vartheta,\vect y
\sim\mathrm{Gamma}\left(a_\lambda+n,
b_\lambda+\mathcal L_{n,c}(\vartheta)/s_L\right).
\label{eq:lambda-collapsed}
\]

We call \eqref{eq:normalized-scale-target} the
\emph{pseudo-residual-normalized learned-scale target}. The factor
\(\lambda^n\) normalizes an asymmetric-Laplace kernel in the abstract
pseudo-residual \(e_t\), not a density in \(y_t\). It is an analyst-declared
joint generalized target rather than a consequence that \(\lambda\) is an
ordinary response parameter. Coherence of a fixed-rate loss update does not by
itself establish same-data learning or calibration of this scale
\citep{LeeLiuNicholls2025LearningRate,WuMartin2023LearningRateComparison}.
Fixed-rate sensitivity grids are therefore primary. If \(s_L\) is estimated
from the training responses, the calculation convention is recorded and the
value is frozen before MCMC; this is a data-dependent empirical-Bayes
specification.

This learned-scale target applies to ordinary RQR only. The mean-tilted loss
can be negative, and parameter-independent additive constants at fixed
\(\omega_{\mathrm R}\) become consequential when the learning rate is random.
The Gamma conditionals below must not be reused for mean-tilted RQR without a
separately normalized, proper joint target. The initial mean-tilted
implementation is therefore restricted to fixed learning rates.

If one conditions additionally on \(\vect v\), the normalized augmented
conditional is
\begin{align}
\lambda\mid\vartheta,\vect v,\vect y
\sim\mathrm{Gamma}\Bigg(&a_\lambda+\frac{3n}{2},\notag\\
&b_\lambda+\frac{1}{s_L}\sum_{t=1}^n
\left[v_t+\frac{(e_t-\xi_cv_t)^2}{2\phi_cv_t}\right]\Bigg).
\label{eq:lambda-augmented}
\end{align}

The diagnostic pure-loss target omits \(\lambda^n\). Its collapsed shape is
\(a_\lambda\), and a compatible augmented representation has shape
\(a_\lambda+n/2\). These alternatives are different targets, not two
implementations of the same posterior.

The implemented partially collapsed scan uses the following order:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.2em]
\item draw \(\lambda\) from \eqref{eq:lambda-collapsed} using the current roots;
\item refresh every \(v_t\) from its GIG conditional using the new \(\lambda\);
\item update root 1 conditional on root 2 and the refreshed scales;
\item update root 2 conditional on the new root 1 and the refreshed scales.
\end{enumerate}
The second step cannot be skipped after the collapsed draw.

\section{RQR-DESN Contract}

RQR-DESN conditions on a deterministic DESN feature matrix. The reservoir seed,
depth, units, lag window, spectral radius, leak rate, input scaling, washout,
and feature reducers are design variables, not posterior unknowns. Posterior
summaries are ordered endpoint functions, widths, and midpoints.

\section{RQR-DLM Contract and FFBS}

For root \(k=1,2\), let
\[
\eta_{kt}=F_t^\top\theta_{kt},\qquad
\theta_{k0}\sim\Normal(m_0,C_0),\qquad
\theta_{kt}=G_t\theta_{k,t-1}+\epsilon_{kt},\qquad
\epsilon_{kt}\sim\Normal(0,W_{kt}).
\]
Equivalently, define
\[
\alpha_t=
\begin{pmatrix}\theta_{1t}\\\theta_{2t}\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\mathcal G_t=
\begin{pmatrix}G_t&0\\0&G_t\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\mathcal W_t=
\begin{pmatrix}W_{1t}&0\\0&W_{2t}\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then \(\alpha_t=\mathcal G_t\alpha_{t-1}+\varepsilon_t\) is one stacked
Gaussian state prior. It does not induce one conditionally Gaussian
measurement update. Writing \(a_t=F_t^\top\theta_{1t}\) and
\(b_t=F_t^\top\theta_{2t}\), the augmented kernel contains
\[
\left\{(y_t-a_t)(y_t-b_t)-\xi_cv_t\right\}^2.
\]
Expansion produces \(a_t^2b_t^2\), so its log kernel is quartic rather than
quadratic in the stacked state. A conventional joint FFBS draw is therefore
unavailable. Holding \(b_t\) fixed makes the expression affine in \(a_t\), and
holding \(a_t\) fixed does the same for \(b_t\). Alternating the two
root-specific FFBS draws is thus an exact blocked Gibbs scan for the stated
target, not an approximation obtained by fitting the roots separately. A
general non-Gaussian joint path sampler could target the same generalized
posterior, but would give up the closed-form Gaussian FFBS blocks.

The two root paths have a stacked Gaussian prior, but the augmented

Conditional on \(\theta_{2,1:T}\), \(v_{1:T}\), and \(\lambda\), root 1 has
\begin{align}
z_{1t}&=H_{1t}\theta_{1t}+\varepsilon_{1t},\\
z_{1t}&=y_t(y_t-\eta_{2t})-\xi_cv_t,\\
H_{1t}&=(y_t-\eta_{2t})F_t^\top,\\
\varepsilon_{1t}&\sim\Normal(0,V_t),\qquad
V_t=\phi_cs_Lv_t/\lambda.
\end{align}
Root 2 is obtained by exchanging labels.

For either conditional Gaussian system, write \(m_{t-1},C_{t-1}\) for filtered
moments. The forward recursion is
\begin{align}
a_t&=G_tm_{t-1}, & P_t&=G_tC_{t-1}G_t^\top,\\
R_t&=P_t+W_t, & q_t&=H_tR_tH_t^\top+V_t,\\
A_t&=R_tH_t^\top/q_t, & m_t&=a_t+A_t(z_t-H_ta_t),\\
C_t&=R_t-A_tq_tA_t^\top.
\end{align}
Starting from \(\theta_T\sim\Normal(m_T,C_T)\), the backward draw for
\(t=T-1,\ldots,1\) uses
\begin{align}
B_t&=C_tG_{t+1}^\top R_{t+1}^{-1},\\
h_t&=m_t+B_t(\theta_{t+1}-a_{t+1}),\\
H_t^{\mathrm B}&=C_t-B_tR_{t+1}B_t^\top,\\
\theta_t\mid\theta_{t+1},\cdot&\sim\Normal(h_t,H_t^{\mathrm B}).
\end{align}

For fixed evolution covariances, the implementation integrates
\(\theta_{k0}\) out of the root-path update. Thus
\[
a_{k1}=G_1m_0,\qquad
R_{k1}=G_1C_0G_1^\top+W_1,
\]
and backward sampling terminates at \(t=1\). The component-scale extension
samples \(\theta_{k0}\) separately because its first innovation is required by
the conjugate scale update.

Missing responses skip the measurement update. The implementation uses
Cholesky factorization and does not silently turn an indefinite covariance into
a positive one by taking absolute singular values. Under the default fail-fast
policy, a failed factorization or negative-eigenvalue projection stops the fit;
an exact positive-semidefinite draw with zero eigenvalues remains admissible.
The audit policy uses a declared jitter ladder and records every covariance
alteration. When a backward prior
covariance is repaired, the repaired matrix is used consistently in the gain,
smoothing covariance, and path-draw covariance. Fixed-target fits with nonzero
repair counts are not eligible for promotion-grade exact-target analyses.
Symmetry and eigenvalue materiality are evaluated relative to the actual
matrix scale, including for matrices with entries below one. Jitter is likewise
matrix-relative except for an exactly zero matrix, for which use of the
declared ladder is recorded as an absolute fallback.

\section{Component-Specific Discount Factors}

Let component discounts be \(\delta_1,\ldots,\delta_J\), with
\(0<\delta_j\le1\), and let their state dimensions sum to \(p\). Define the
\(p\times p\) matrix \(D\) to have value
\((1-\delta_j)/\delta_j\) throughout the within-component block \(j\) and zero
between components. This reproduces the public \texttt{df}/\texttt{dim.df}
construction in exdqlm 1.1.0. The covariance recursion is
\[
P_t=G_tC_{t-1}G_t^\top,\qquad
W_t=D\circ P_t,\qquad
R_t=P_t+W_t,
\]
where \(\circ\) denotes an elementwise product. Thus a within-component
covariance block is inflated by \(1/\delta_j\), whereas cross-component
covariances are not inflated.

The standalone API distinguishes three uses:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.2em]
\item \texttt{fixed\_W}: \(W_{1:T}\) is supplied and fixed. This gives exact
FFBS for the declared Gaussian state prior.
\item \texttt{discount\_template}: a reference design and reference observation
variance generate \(W_{1:T}\) once through the recursion above. The sequence is
then frozen before MCMC. This also gives exact FFBS for the resulting declared
prior, conditional on the frozen template. If the reference quantities are
estimated from the training responses, the resulting prior specification is
empirical Bayes and must be recorded as such.
\item \texttt{adaptive\_discount}: \(W_t\) is recomputed inside each conditional
filter. In RQR, \(H_t\) and \(V_t\) depend on the other root, \(v_t\), and
\(\lambda\), so the implied evolution covariance also depends on those blocks.
The current alternating kernel omits terms that would generally arise from
such dependence in a fixed joint density. This mode is therefore experimental
and is not described as exact Gibbs for the stated generalized posterior.
\end{enumerate}
The same discounts are used for both roots unless a future model deliberately
breaks their prior exchangeability.

\subsection{Incompatibility of adaptive conditional discounts}

The experimental designation can be made precise. Consider \(T=2\), \(p=1\),
\(F_t=G_t=1\), and a nonzero discount inflation \(d=(1-\delta)/\delta\).
For the root-1 conditional filter, the time-1 covariance and time-2 innovation
variance include
\[
C_{11}=\frac{R_{11}V_1}
{(y_1-\theta_{21})^2R_{11}+V_1},
\qquad
w_1(\theta_{21})=dC_{11}.
\]
The conditional log density therefore contains
\[
-\frac12\log w_1(\theta_{21})
-\frac{(\theta_{12}-\theta_{11})^2}{2w_1(\theta_{21})},
\]
whose mixed derivative is generically
\[
\frac{\partial^2}{\partial\theta_{21}\partial\theta_{12}}
\log\pi(\vect\theta_1\mid\vect\theta_2,\cdot)
=
\frac{(\theta_{12}-\theta_{11})w_1'(\theta_{21})}
{w_1(\theta_{21})^2}\ne0.
\]
In the advertised root-2 conditional, the adaptive variance depends on
\(\theta_{11}\), not \(\theta_{12}\); its time-1 pseudo-observation does not
involve \(\theta_{12}\), and its time-2 pseudo-observation does not involve
\(\theta_{21}\). Hence the corresponding mixed derivative is zero. A positive
twice-differentiable joint density would require equality of these derivatives.
Except in degenerate cases such as \(d=0\), the two conditional adaptive FFBS
kernels are therefore incompatible with a common smooth joint density while
retaining their advertised Gaussian forms.

\subsection{Exact shared component scales}

Partition the state into \(J\) blocks of dimensions \(d_j\), let \(S_j\)
select block \(j\), and fix positive-definite templates \(Q_{jt}\). Define
\[
W_t(\vect q)=\operatorname{blockdiag}
\{q_1Q_{1t},\ldots,q_JQ_{Jt}\},
\qquad
q_j\sim\operatorname{IG}(a_j,b_j),
\]
where the inverse-Gamma density is proportional to
\(q_j^{-a_j-1}\exp(-b_j/q_j)\). The same \(q_j\) is shared by the two roots.
For
\[
d_{kjt}=S_j\{\theta_{kt}-G_t\theta_{k,t-1}\},
\]
the full conditional is
\[
q_j\mid\cdot\sim\operatorname{IG}\left(
a_j+Td_j,
b_j+\frac12\sum_{k=1}^2\sum_{t=1}^T
d_{kjt}^\top Q_{jt}^{-1}d_{kjt}
\right).
\]
The \(Td_j\) shape increment includes two roots, each contributing
\(Td_j/2\).

Because the fixed-\(W\) FFBS implementation integrates out the time-zero
state, the component-scale sampler restores it after each root-path draw. Its
conditional moments are
\begin{align}
C_{k0}^\star
&=\{C_0^{-1}+G_1^\top W_1(\vect q)^{-1}G_1\}^{-1},\\
m_{k0}^\star
&=C_{k0}^\star\{C_0^{-1}m_0+
G_1^\top W_1(\vect q)^{-1}\theta_{k1}\},
\end{align}
and
\[
\theta_{k0}\mid\theta_{k1},\vect q
\sim\Normal(m_{k0}^\star,C_{k0}^\star).
\]
One exact sweep updates collapsed \(\lambda\), refreshes \(\vect v\), samples
each root path and its time-zero state, updates all \(q_j\), and optionally
swaps the two complete root labels. Static \(q_j=0\) components are separate
degenerate models and are not part of the inverse-Gamma support.

\section{Validation Gates}

Before a production simulation, the package must pass pure-R/C++ filtering and
smoothing parity against an independent dense Gaussian reference,
multiblock discount construction, missing-observation, extreme-scale GIG,
learned-scale, component-scale conditional, checkpoint-continuation,
root-label, and small end-to-end tests. Promotion-grade exact-target fits must
have zero numerical repairs. A run manifest must then freeze data-generating
mechanisms, sample sizes, train/test windows, forecast horizons, method
configurations, seeds, package versions, and repository commits. Fit objects
record data, complete model/target/evolution, and checkpoint digests; RNG state;
compiler and BLAS/LAPACK provenance; required external-repository states; and a
versioned schema. Promotion requires clean detected checkouts matching every
exact commit declared in the run manifest. If an external package contributes
executed code, promotion additionally requires an isolated-runtime
attestation. The verifier reconstructs the Git and extracted-archive
mode--blob--path manifests, compares the complete expected and built
source-package file sets, rehashes post-command receipts and build and
installation logs, requires one successful full-package installation, and
checks both the pre-marker and final installed-runtime digests. A
source-package lineage marker is required inside the executing runtime; direct
source-tree execution is ineligible. This is a controlled local-build
integrity contract, not a claim of bit-for-bit reproducible compilation across
platforms or a cryptographic signature. The primary \texttt{rqrgibbs}
namespace is subject to the same isolated-runtime rule. Exact
continuation stops on schema, checkpoint, data, model, target, or evolution
mismatch and, by default, on package, R, numerical-library, dependency, or
source-commit mismatch. An explicit portability override is persisted and
removes reproducibility and promotion eligibility. Repair counts, numerical
exactness, reproducibility, and promotion status are recorded per generation.
Validation reconstructs parent-checkpoint links, the mismatch ledger, and every
cumulative recursion; requested and resolved FFBS backends are compared
separately. Heavy
state draws and fitted objects remain in ignored local output directories.

The first bounded normalized learned-scale fixture uses four production
partially collapsed chains, four independently coded fully augmented chains,
and event-boundary-aware two-dimensional root quadrature with the Gamma
loss-scale conditional integrated analytically. The maintained
\texttt{posterior} implementation supplies rank-normalized \(\widehat R\) and
bulk and tail effective sample sizes. The predeclared convergence, Monte Carlo
error, quadrature-accuracy, and zero-repair gates all pass. This is a
target-implementation check on a
deterministic intercept-only fixture, not a calibration result.

The current bounded and confirmatory RQR-DLM protocols concern ordinary RQR,
or \(\delta=0\) in the notation of
Section~\ref{sec:supp-mean-tilt}. Mean-tilted validation is a separate staged
program: deterministic oracle identities, exact zero-tilt equivalence,
intercept-only quadrature, fixed-design recovery at prespecified tilts, and
only afterward an independently evaluated tilt-selection rule. No dynamic
mean-tilt run is authorized by the algebra in this supplement.

Held-out RQR loss, empirical coverage error, and width at comparable coverage
are primary. Oracle endpoint recovery is primary when the population RQR roots
are available. The standard interval score is secondary because its canonical
target is an equal-tailed interval, whereas RQR does not preassign endpoint
quantile probabilities.

Preliminary RQR-DESN results are withheld from the circulated article package
until the matched standalone study, including the planned RQR-DLM comparison,
is complete. This avoids presenting calibration-screened legacy output as
confirmatory evidence for the current article target.
