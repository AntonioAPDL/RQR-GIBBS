\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The supplement gives the derivations and computational specifications behind the
mean-preserving interval (MPI), its mean-tilted interval (MTI) generalization,
generalized-Bayes, and tolerance-action constructions in the main article. It
establishes the MPI score equations, quantile-window identification, MTI
profiled-risk identification, retained-distribution consequences, exact
fractional empirical balance, and fixed-dimensional static uncertainty
geometry. It then derives the
pseudo-asymmetric-Laplace augmentation, Gaussian and
generalized-inverse-Gaussian full conditionals, fixed-target ECM updates, and
root-blocked dynamic-state computation. Separate sections describe the MPI-only
hierarchical inverse-loss scale, regularized-horseshoe Nishimura--Suchard (RHS-NS)
prior representation, endpoint regression with fixed DESN features, precomputed discount
templates, and shared
component-scale restrictions. The
Bayesian response-distribution layer is developed separately from the MTI
generalized posterior: the direct Dirichlet-process posterior gives exact
fixed-interval Beta content probabilities, while Gaussian
Dirichlet-process-mixture (DPM) summaries provide a smooth posterior
alternative. Finite-sample scan certification, selected-action asymptotics,
posterior endpoint-coverage transfer, and regression-family tolerance theory
remain open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Guide to the Supplement}
\label{sec:supp-guide}

The main article carries the statistical narrative. The supplement provides a
technical reference for definitions, proofs, computational identities, and supporting results. The
MTI generalized posterior combines a prior with a loss-based update
\citep{BissiriHolmesWalker2016GeneralBayes}. Root-trajectory draws therefore
induce generalized-posterior distributions for endpoint functions. Future
response prediction belongs to a separately specified response-distribution
model.

Notation follows the main article. Finite-dimensional generalized posteriors
are written as densities or kernels, such as \(\pi(\theta\mid D)\), rather than
with measure-style posterior arguments. Differentials are reserved for actual
integrals, for example over a latent scale. The observed-index set is
\(I_{\mathrm{obs}}\); subvectors and submatrices restricted to that set use the
shorter ``obs'' subscript.

\section*{Part II. Population Theory and Proofs}

The population-theory part supports the main article's fixed-content, MPI, MTI,
finite-sample balance, and static uncertainty claims. It works before any
algorithmic approximation is introduced and records the assumptions under which
the interval target, score equations, and covariance statements are valid.

\section{Fixed-Content Interval Targets}
\label{sec:supp-interval-functionals}

The population interval class is most transparent on the probability scale.
Suppress \(x\) and write
\[
I_c(u)=[Q(u),Q(u+c)],\qquad
M_c(u)=\frac{1}{c}\int_u^{u+c}Q(v)\,dv,\qquad
W_c(u)=Q(u+c)-Q(u).
\]
The equal-tailed interval fixes \(u_{\mathrm{ET}}=(1-c)/2\). MPI
solves \(M_c(u_{\mathrm{MPI}})=\mu\). Define the possibly set-valued collection
of shortest contiguous windows by
\[
S_{\mathrm{SH}}
=\argmin_{0\le u\le1-c}W_c(u).
\]
If a positive density exists at an interior \(u_{\mathrm{SH}}\in
S_{\mathrm{SH}}\), then
\[
W_c'(u_{\mathrm{SH}})
=\frac{1}{f\{Q(u_{\mathrm{SH}}+c)\}}
-\frac{1}{f\{Q(u_{\mathrm{SH}})\}}=0,
\]
so the endpoint densities agree. For a continuous strictly unimodal density
and a unique regular shortest interval, this interval agrees with the
corresponding highest-density interval. Under multimodality, a highest-density
region can be disconnected; the present construction remains restricted to
one contiguous interval
\citep{BrehmerGneiting2021Intervals}.

Under a symmetric unimodal distribution, all three intervals coincide. Under
asymmetry, their widths need not coincide; only the shortest interval is
ordered against the other two by definition. The standard interval score is
aligned with the central equal-tailed target and is not a target-neutral score
for this comparison
\citep{GneitingRaftery2007,BrehmerGneiting2021Intervals,
FisslerEtAl2021SetValued}.

\section{Mean-Preserving Interval Scores}
\label{sec:supp-population-target}

The derivation starts from the endpoint scores because those scores determine
both content and placement. Fix a covariate value \(x\), a desired content
\(c\in(0,1)\), and endpoints
\(a=L(x)<U(x)=b\). The pointwise loss is
\begin{equation}
\ell_c(a,b;y)
=
\begin{cases}
(1-c)(y-a)(b-y), & a<y<b,\\
c(y-a)(y-b), & y\le a\ \text{or}\ y\ge b.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:supp-mpi-loss-piecewise}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, with
\(e(a,b;y)=(y-a)(y-b)\) and
\(\rho_c(r)=r\{c-\ind(r<0)\}\),
\begin{equation}
\ell_c(a,b;y)=\rho_c\{e(a,b;y)\}.
\label{eq:supp-mpi-product-check}
\end{equation}
The check function is applied to the product residual. Its index \(c\) denotes
the desired interval content rather than a prescribed response-quantile level
for either endpoint or a generalized-Bayes learning rate. The loss is symmetric
in its two roots; ordering is used only for the endpoint interpretation.

Define \(I_{a,b}(Y)=\ind(a<Y<b)\). Away from the two roots, the derivative of
the check loss with respect to its scalar argument is
\(c-I_{a,b}(Y)\). Assume
\(\E(Y^2\mid X=x)<\infty\), the conditional risk is differentiable at an
interior stationary point, differentiation and conditional expectation may be
interchanged, and the conditional distribution places no mass at \(a\) or
\(b\). Since
\[
\frac{\partial}{\partial a}(Y-a)(Y-b)=b-Y,
\qquad
\frac{\partial}{\partial b}(Y-a)(Y-b)=a-Y,
\]
the pointwise first-order conditions are
\begin{align}
\E\left[\{c-I_{a,b}(Y)\}(b-Y)\mid X=x\right]&=0,
\label{eq:pop-foc-a}\\
\E\left[\{c-I_{a,b}(Y)\}(a-Y)\mid X=x\right]&=0.
\label{eq:pop-foc-b}
\end{align}
Subtracting \eqref{eq:pop-foc-b} from \eqref{eq:pop-foc-a} and using
\(b-a>0\) gives
\[
\Pr\{a<Y<b\mid X=x\}=c.
\]
Substitution into either first-order condition then gives
\[
\E\{Y\ind(a<Y<b)\mid X=x\}=c\E(Y\mid X=x).
\]

The second equation is equivalently
\[
\E(Y\mid a<Y<b,X=x)=\E(Y\mid X=x)
\]
and
\(\operatorname{Cov}\{Y,I_{a,b}(Y)\mid X=x\}=0\). This covariance statement is
compatible with dependence, asymmetry, and unequal-tailed endpoints. The
coverage implication is established by \citet{PouplinEtAl2024RQR}; the
conditional first-moment implication is a further consequence of their
first-order system.

Let \(\mu_x=\E(Y\mid X=x)\). Since
\(\E(Y-\mu_x\mid X=x)=0\), the first-moment equation is also equivalent to
\begin{equation}
\E[\{\mu_x-Y\}\ind(Y<a)\mid X=x]
=
\E[\{Y-\mu_x\}\ind(Y>b)\mid X=x].
\label{eq:rqr-tail-moment-balance}
\end{equation}
Thus MPI balances omitted tail probability multiplied by average
tail displacement from the conditional mean. The two omitted tail probabilities
may differ.

\begin{theorem}[Quantile-window identification]
\label{thm:supp-quantile-window}
Suppose, in addition, that \(F_x\) is continuous and strictly increasing on
its support, and let \(Q_x=F_x^{-1}\). Define
\[
M_{c,x}(u)=\frac{1}{c}\int_u^{u+c}Q_x(v)\,dv,
\qquad 0\le u\le1-c.
\]
Then \(M_{c,x}\) is strictly increasing, and there is a unique \(u_c(x)\)
satisfying \(M_{c,x}\{u_c(x)\}=\mu_x\). The corresponding interval is
\[
\left[Q_x\{u_c(x)\},Q_x\{u_c(x)+c\}\right].
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Modulo conditional probability-zero portions outside the support, every
contiguous interval having content \(c\) has the displayed
quantile-window form. If \(v>u\), then
\[
M_{c,x}(v)-M_{c,x}(u)
=\frac{1}{c}\int_0^c
\{Q_x(v+s)-Q_x(u+s)\}\,ds>0.
\]
The average of the lowest conditional \(c\)-fraction is below \(\mu_x\), and
the average of the highest conditional \(c\)-fraction is above \(\mu_x\), for
a nondegenerate distribution with finite mean. Continuity and strict
monotonicity therefore give existence and uniqueness. The first-moment
equation identifies the stated endpoints.
\end{proof}

If \(F_x\) is symmetric about \(\mu_x\), then
\(Q_x(1-v)=2\mu_x-Q_x(v)\). The window beginning at
\((1-c)/2\) has retained mean \(\mu_x\), so uniqueness gives the equal-tailed
interval. Conversely, coincidence at one coverage level does not establish
global symmetry.

The distribution conditional on interval membership and its complementary
tail distribution both have mean \(\mu_x\). This is a first-moment statement,
not independence between \(Y\) and interval membership. It also clarifies why
the endpoint midpoint need not equal \(\mu_x\) under asymmetry.

\begin{proposition}[Mean-preserving contraction]
\label{prop:supp-convex-order}
Let \(Y_{\mathrm{in}}\) and \(Y_{\mathrm{out}}\) have the conditional
distributions of \(Y\) given \(a<Y<b\) and \(Y\notin(a,b)\), respectively,
at a regular MPI target. For every convex function \(\varphi\) for
which the required expectations exist,
\[
\E\{\varphi(Y_{\mathrm{in}})\}
\le
\E\{\varphi(Y)\}
\le
\E\{\varphi(Y_{\mathrm{out}})\}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
Y_{\mathrm{in}}\preceq_{\mathrm{cx}}Y
\preceq_{\mathrm{cx}}Y_{\mathrm{out}}.
\]
If second moments exist, the same ordering holds for the corresponding
variances.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let \(s_\varphi\) be the affine secant through
\(\{a,\varphi(a)\}\) and \(\{b,\varphi(b)\}\). Convexity gives
\(\varphi(y)\le s_\varphi(y)\) on \([a,b]\) and
\(\varphi(y)\ge s_\varphi(y)\) outside that interval. Both conditional
distributions have mean \(\mu_x\), so
\[
\E\{\varphi(Y_{\mathrm{in}})\}
\le s_\varphi(\mu_x)
\le \E\{\varphi(Y_{\mathrm{out}})\}.
\]
The full conditional distribution is their mixture with weights \(c\) and
\(1-c\), which places its convex expectation between the two. Taking
\(\varphi(y)=(y-\mu_x)^2\) gives the variance statement.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Interval Paths and Tail Sensitivity}

Retain the quantile-window characterization and assume, for this derivative
calculation, connected support and positive continuous density at the relevant
endpoint quantiles. Write \(u_c\) for the MPI index, and set
\(L_c=Q(u_c)\) and \(U_c=Q(u_c+c)\). Differentiating
\[
\int_{u_c}^{u_c+c}\{Q(v)-\mu\}\,dv=0
\]
with respect to \(c\), at regular interior values, gives
\[
\frac{du_c}{dc}
=-\frac{U_c-\mu}{U_c-L_c}<0,
\qquad
\frac{d(u_c+c)}{dc}
=\frac{\mu-L_c}{U_c-L_c}>0.
\]
The MPI population intervals are therefore nested as content
increases, with analogous one-sided statements at isolated nonsmooth support
transitions. If \(p_\mu=F(\mu)\) satisfies \(Q(p_\mu)=\mu\) and \(Q\) is
continuous at \(p_\mu\), then \(Q(u_c)\le\mu\le Q(u_c+c)\), so
\(p_\mu\in[u_c,u_c+c]\). Since this probability window has length \(c\),
\[
L_c\longrightarrow\mu,\qquad U_c\longrightarrow\mu
\quad\text{as }c\downarrow0.
\]
This zero-content limit needs the local support condition above. It is false
under the quantile-window assumptions alone: for the symmetric distribution
with density \(1/2\) on \([-2,-1]\cup[1,2]\), \(\mu=0\) and the MPI
window has \(u_c=(1-c)/2\), hence \(L_c=-1-c\) and \(U_c=1+c\), so the
zero-content limit straddles the support gap rather than contracting to
\(\mu\). The corrected zero-content limit is a property of the population
interval path; the
midpoint at a fixed positive \(c\) is not generally the mean, and independent
fits at several content levels do not by themselves define joint
generalized-posterior draws of that path.

For \(Y^\star=r+sY\), \(s>0\), the MPI endpoints transform as
\[
L_c^\star=r+sL_c,\qquad U_c^\star=r+sU_c.
\]
For the tilted family, \(\delta^\star=s\delta\) and
\[
\ell^\star_{c,\delta^\star}
(r+sa,r+sb;r+sy)
=s^2\ell_{c,\delta}(a,b;y).
\]
Thus the population target is positive-affine equivariant. A corresponding
generalized posterior uses the transformed prior and
\(\omega_{\mathrm R}^\star=\omega_{\mathrm R}/s^2\). Conversely, if the
original response is standardized as \(\widetilde Y=(Y-r)/s\), use
\(\widetilde\delta=\delta/s\) and
\(\widetilde\omega_{\mathrm R}=\omega_{\mathrm R}s^2\).
Nonlinear monotone transformations generally change the mean-preserving
functional rather than merely re-expressing it.

Finally, with fixed endpoints,
\(\rho_c\{(y-a)(y-b)\}=O(y^2)\) as \(|y|\to\infty\), and the endpoint scores
grow linearly in \(|y|\). MPI is distribution-free in the sense that it does
not posit a parametric response density. Its retained-mean target still depends
on second moments, so the finite-second-moment assumption and response-scale
choice are scientifically consequential.

Endpoint atoms require directional conditions. Write
\(m=(a+b)/2\), \(h=(b-a)/2>0\), so
\(e=(Y-m)^2-h^2\). The left and right derivatives in \(h\) are
\[
D_-R_x(m,h)=2h\{\Pr(a<Y<b\mid x)-c\},
\]
and
\[
D_+R_x(m,h)=2h\{\Pr(a\le Y\le b\mid x)-c\}.
\]
At a local minimum, \(D_-R_x\le0\le D_+R_x\), and therefore
\[
\Pr(a<Y<b\mid X=x)\le c\le\Pr(a\le Y\le b\mid X=x).
\]
Without a separate joint subgradient and endpoint-mass allocation analysis,
these directional conditions do not imply the exact retained-mean equality
derived above under endpoint continuity.
If \(a=b=m\), then \(e=(Y-m)^2\) and the risk is
\(c\E\{(Y-m)^2\mid X=x\}\); the coincident-root target is the conditional
mean when the second moment exists.

These implications are conditional, pointwise, and assumption dependent. They
do not follow by treating estimated data-dependent endpoints as fixed. For
linear roots \(\eta_k(X)=X^\top\beta_k\), the corresponding population score
conditions are instead
\begin{align}
\E\left[X\{c-I_{\eta_1,\eta_2}(Y)\}(\eta_2-Y)\right]&=0,\\
\E\left[X\{c-I_{\eta_1,\eta_2}(Y)\}(\eta_1-Y)\right]&=0,
\end{align}
where \(I_{\eta_1,\eta_2}(Y)\) indicates that \(Y\) lies between the
unordered roots. These design-weighted projection equations need not imply
conditional coverage for every \(x\).

The finite-sample unbiasedness calculation in
\citet{PouplinEtAl2024RQR} treats fitted, data-dependent endpoints as fixed,
and its zero-gradient argument does not cover nonsmooth optima when an
observation coincides with a fitted root. We therefore do not use that
calculation as a finite-sample guarantee; such a result requires a separate
empirical-process analysis.

\section{Mean-Tilted Interval Loss and Target}
\label{sec:supp-mean-tilt}

The MTI loss is the fixed-tilt generalization of MPI. It keeps the MPI content
score and shifts only the retained-mean equation; setting \(\delta=0\) recovers
the MPI loss. For a fixed mean tilt \(\delta\in\R\), define
\begin{equation}
\ell_{c,\delta}^{\mathrm{MTI}}(m,h;y)
=\rho_c\{(y-m)^2-h^2\}-2c\delta(m-y),
\label{eq:mean-tilted-loss-midpoint}
\end{equation}
or, equivalently,
\begin{equation}
\ell_{c,\delta}^{\mathrm{MTI}}(a,b;y)
=\rho_c\{(y-a)(y-b)\}-c\delta(a+b-2y).
\label{eq:mean-tilted-loss-roots}
\end{equation}
The second form is invariant to exchanging the raw root labels. The tilt has
response units so that both terms in the loss have squared-response units, and
nonzero \(\delta\) changes the target placement rather than the content score.
For restricted linear roots \(\eta_k(X)=X^\top\beta_k\) and an externally
fixed \(\delta(X)\), the population score equations are
\begin{align}
\E\!\left[
X\left\{\{c-I_{\eta_1,\eta_2}(Y)\}(\eta_2-Y)-c\delta(X)\right\}
\right]&=0,\\
\E\!\left[
X\left\{\{c-I_{\eta_1,\eta_2}(Y)\}(\eta_1-Y)-c\delta(X)\right\}
\right]&=0.
\end{align}
They are design-weighted projection equations. Pointwise conditional content
and retained-mean identities require a sufficiently rich predictor class and
the unrestricted conditions below.

\subsection{Root-Labeling Convention}

The MTI losses \eqref{eq:mean-tilted-loss-roots}, including their MPI
\(\delta=0\) special case, are invariant to exchanging the two raw roots. With
exchangeable root priors, the unordered pair is identified but the labels
``root 1'' and ``root 2'' are not. The static sampler therefore
permits a complete-root swap that exchanges the coefficient vector and any
root-specific prior state as one block. Lower and upper endpoint functionals are
obtained by pointwise sorting after the raw roots are evaluated.
Coefficient-level lower/upper summaries require a separate
post-processing chart: one binary keep/swap assignment is made for each
retained complete root block using distances on a specified reference design, and
lower/upper coefficient summaries are suppressed if the two fitted linear roots
cross or the assignment is ambiguous on that design. The dynamic sampler
uses the same rule for ordinate paths: a retained draw may be kept or swapped
as one complete path, rather than by relabeling roots separately at different
times.

\subsection{Interval-Functional Coefficient Interpretation}
\label{sec:supp-coefficient-interpretation}

The static sampler continues to target the raw-root draws
\((\vect\beta_1,\vect\beta_2)\). This subsection defines deterministic
reporting coordinates derived from those same draws. For static roots, define
\[
\vect\beta_m=\frac{\vect\beta_1+\vect\beta_2}{2},\qquad
\vect\beta_s=\frac{\vect\beta_2-\vect\beta_1}{2}.
\]
At a design row \(\vect x\),
\[
M(\vect x)=\vect x^\top\vect\beta_m,\qquad
S(\vect x)=\vect x^\top\vect\beta_s,
\]
and the ordered interval functionals are
\[
L(\vect x)=M(\vect x)-|S(\vect x)|,\qquad
U(\vect x)=M(\vect x)+|S(\vect x)|,\qquad
W(\vect x)=2|S(\vect x)|.
\]
A complete root swap leaves \(\vect\beta_m\), \(L\), \(U\), and \(W\) invariant
and changes the sign of \(\vect\beta_s\). Thus \(\vect\beta_m\) is the
coefficient vector for the geometric midpoint. The signed vector
\(\vect\beta_s\) is best read as an orientation coordinate for the gap between
the two raw roots. A conventional half-width coefficient interpretation requires
a domain where the sign of \(S(\vect x)\) is fixed.

If one complete root is separated below the other on a specified domain
\(\mathcal X_A\), the oriented coefficients may be written
\(\vect\beta_L\) and \(\vect\beta_U\), with
\[
\vect\beta_W=\vect\beta_U-\vect\beta_L.
\]
On \(\mathcal X_A\), this gives the usual lower-endpoint, upper-endpoint, and
width coefficient language. The domain qualification is essential: an affine
gap that is positive on an observed or scientific reference design need not
remain positive under arbitrary extrapolation.

When the raw roots cross, endpoint effects are local or profile specific. Away
from \(S(\vect x)=0\), a continuous predictor \(x_j\) has local effects
\[
\frac{\partial L(\vect x)}{\partial x_j}
=\beta_{m,j}-\operatorname{sgn}\{S(\vect x)\}\beta_{s,j},\qquad
\frac{\partial U(\vect x)}{\partial x_j}
=\beta_{m,j}+\operatorname{sgn}\{S(\vect x)\}\beta_{s,j},
\]
and
\[
\frac{\partial W(\vect x)}{\partial x_j}
=2\operatorname{sgn}\{S(\vect x)\}\beta_{s,j}.
\]
At \(S(\vect x)=0\), the ordered endpoint and width functions generally have a
kink, so finite contrasts are usually clearer than derivatives. For two
scientifically meaningful rows \(\vect x_0\) and \(\vect x_1\), compute within
each retained draw
\[
\Delta M=(\vect x_1-\vect x_0)^\top\vect\beta_m,
\]
\[
\Delta W
=2\left\{
|\vect x_1^\top\vect\beta_s|-|\vect x_0^\top\vect\beta_s|
\right\},
\]
and
\[
\Delta L
=\Delta M-\left\{
|\vect x_1^\top\vect\beta_s|-|\vect x_0^\top\vect\beta_s|
\right\},\qquad
\Delta U
=\Delta M+\left\{
|\vect x_1^\top\vect\beta_s|-|\vect x_0^\top\vect\beta_s|
\right\}.
\]
These contrasts are invariant to the raw root labels and remain valid when the
two profiles lie on different sides of a crossing. They also match the
reporting convention: posterior summaries of \(L\), \(U\), \(M\),
and \(W\) are formed after draw-wise endpoint ordering, while lower/upper
coefficient summaries are reported only after the specified-domain separation
check succeeds.

The midpoint in these formulas is geometric. The unrestricted MPI score
conditions make the retained conditional mean equal to the full conditional
mean, and MTI changes that retained-mean balance by the fixed tilt
\(\delta\). These are statements about the mean of the observations inside the
selected interval, whereas \(M(\vect x)=\{L(\vect x)+U(\vect x)\}/2\) is the
center of the two endpoints. Under asymmetry, tail imbalance, or a restricted
feature class, the two quantities can differ.

For endpoint regression with fixed DESN features, the same algebra holds with
\(\vect x\) replaced by the fixed reservoir feature vector. Individual DESN
feature coefficients are computational coordinates; forecast-scale endpoint
paths, widths, and input-scenario contrasts are the more direct scientific
summaries. For dynamic
roots, the same midpoint--gap representation applies to the state ordinates,
for example \(M_t=(\eta_{1t}+\eta_{2t})/2\) and
\(W_t=|\eta_{2t}-\eta_{1t}|\). A dynamic lower/upper chart, if requested, must
use one complete-path assignment per retained draw, preserving the state
trajectory rather than relabeling each time point separately.

\subsection{Fixed-Content Mean-Tilted Target}

The next proposition records the population score equations supported by the
mean-tilted loss. It is the supplement counterpart of
the fixed-content mean-tilt proposition in the main article.

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-content mean-tilted target]
\label{prop:supp-mean-tilt}
Under the regularity conditions in
Section~\ref{sec:supp-population-target}, a nondegenerate interior
stationary point \((m_\delta,h_\delta)\) satisfies
\[
\Pr(|Y-m_\delta|<h_\delta)=c
\]
and
\[
\E(Y\mid |Y-m_\delta|<h_\delta)=\mu+\delta.
\]
If \(F\) is continuous and strictly increasing on its support and \(\delta\)
lies strictly between the endpoint values of the admissible range, the unique
interior lower-tail index is the solution of
\[
M_c(u_\delta)=\mu+\delta.
\]
Endpoint tilts are understood as boundary limits and satisfy the corresponding
one-sided stationarity conditions.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let \(I_{m,h}(Y)=\ind(|Y-m|<h)\). Away from endpoint atoms,
\begin{align}
\frac{\partial}{\partial h}\E\{\ell_{c,\delta}(m,h;Y)\}
&=2h\{\Pr(I_{m,h}=1)-c\},\\
\frac{\partial}{\partial m}\E\{\ell_{c,\delta}(m,h;Y)\}
&=2\E[(m-Y)\{c-I_{m,h}(Y)\}]-2c\delta.
\end{align}
The first equation gives content \(c\). Substitution into the second gives
\(\E\{YI_{m,h}(Y)\}=c(\mu+\delta)\), which is the retained-mean equation.
Theorem~\ref{thm:supp-quantile-window} and strict monotonicity of \(M_c\)
give the quantile-window statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Profile radius and envelope derivative]
\label{lem:supp-profile-radius}
Suppose that the support closure of \(F\) is an interval
\([\ell,r]\), where either endpoint may be infinite, \(F\) is continuous and
strictly increasing and absolutely continuous between its support endpoints,
its density is positive and continuous on the support interior, and
\(\E(Y^2)<\infty\). Extend \(F\) by zero and one beyond finite support
endpoints. For every \(m\in\R\), there is a unique \(h_c(m)>0\) satisfying
\[
\Pr\{|Y-m|<h_c(m)\}=c.
\]
The map \(m\mapsto h_c(m)\) is continuous and is differentiable whenever its
two roots are interior to the support. Define
\[
\overline R_{c,\delta}(m)
=
\E\{\ell_{c,\delta}(m,h_c(m);Y)\}.
\]
Also let \(u_c(m)=F\{m-h_c(m)\}\). Then
\begin{equation}
\overline R_{c,\delta}'(m)
=2c\left[M_c\{u_c(m)\}-\mu-\delta\right],
\label{eq:supp-profiled-risk-derivative}
\end{equation}
where both roots are interior, with the corresponding one-sided derivatives
when a root crosses a finite support endpoint.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For fixed \(m\), the map
\[
H_m(h)=F(m+h)-F(m-h),\qquad h\ge0,
\]
is continuous, begins at zero, and converges to one. Interval support and
strict increase imply that \(H_m\) passes through every value in \((0,1)\)
strictly: any plateau can occur only before the expanding interval reaches
the support or after it contains the full support. Thus \(H_m(h)=c\) has one
solution. Continuity of this inverse in \(m\) follows by the same strict
crossing argument. When both roots are interior, the implicit-function
theorem applies because
\[
\frac{\partial H_m(h)}{\partial h}
=f(m+h)+f(m-h)>0.
\]

For fixed \(m\), write \(Z_m=(Y-m)^2\) and \(q=h^2\). The subgradient interval
of \(q\mapsto\E\{\rho_c(Z_m-q)\}\) is
\[
\left[\Pr(Z_m<q)-c,\ \Pr(Z_m\le q)-c\right].
\]
Continuity makes \(q=h_c(m)^2\) its unique minimizer. On a compact
neighborhood of \(m\), the partial midpoint score is dominated by a constant
multiple of \(1+|Y|\), which is integrable. Differentiation under the
expectation is therefore valid away from the probability-zero endpoints.
The standard two-sided profiling inequalities, evaluated once at
\(h_c(m)\) and once at \(h_c(m+s)\), together with continuity of \(h_c\),
give the envelope derivative
\begin{align*}
\overline R_{c,\delta}'(m)
&=
2\E\!\left[(m-Y)
\{c-\ind(m-h_c(m)<Y<m+h_c(m))\}\right]-2c\delta\\
&=
2c\left[M_c\{u_c(m)\}-\mu-\delta\right].
\end{align*}
The same inequalities give one-sided derivatives at a finite-support
transition.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Global profiled-risk identification]
\label{thm:supp-profiled-risk}
Under the conditions of Lemma~\ref{lem:supp-profile-radius}, set
\(u_c(m)=F\{m-h_c(m)\}\), so \(u_c(m)\in[0,1-c]\). For
\[
\delta_-:=M_c(0)-\mu<\delta<
\delta_+:=M_c(1-c)-\mu,
\]
the expected loss has a unique global minimizer over finite ordered roots. Its
index \(u_\delta\in(0,1-c)\) satisfies
\[
M_c(u_\delta)=\mu+\delta.
\]
Nondegeneracy gives \(\delta_-<0<\delta_+\), so MPI is an interior
member. The two raw root-label permutations represent the same ordered
interval.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write \(a_c(m)=m-h_c(m)\) and \(b_c(m)=m+h_c(m)\). Continuity gives
\[
F\{b_c(m)\}-F\{a_c(m)\}=c,
\]
so \(F\{b_c(m)\}=u_c(m)+c\). When \(0<u_c(m)<1-c\),
\[
a_c(m)=Q\{u_c(m)\},\qquad
b_c(m)=Q\{u_c(m)+c\},
\]
and therefore
\[
m=m_c\{u_c(m)\}
:=\frac{Q\{u_c(m)\}+Q\{u_c(m)+c\}}{2}.
\]
The map \(m_c(u)\) is strictly increasing. If a root extends beyond a finite
support endpoint, \(u_c(m)\) is clamped at \(0\) or \(1-c\); hence \(u_c\) is
globally nondecreasing.

Lemma~\ref{lem:supp-profile-radius} gives
\eqref{eq:supp-profiled-risk-derivative}. Strict monotonicity of \(Q\) also
gives, for \(v>u\),
\[
M_c(v)-M_c(u)
=\frac{1}{c}\int_0^c\{Q(v+s)-Q(u+s)\}\,ds>0.
\]
For an interior admissible tilt, the profiled derivative is therefore negative
before the unique solution of \(M_c(u)=\mu+\delta\) and positive afterward.
The profiled risk has a unique global midpoint minimizer, and the preceding
fixed-\(m\) argument gives its unique positive half-width. This proves joint
global identification of the ordered roots.
\end{proof}

The admissible population tilt range is
\[
M_c(0)-\mu\le\delta\le M_c(1-c)-\mu,
\]
and both endpoint values are finite under the assumed second moment. Interior
tilts correspond one-to-one with finite-root quantile windows. At
\(\delta_- = M_c(0)-\mu\), the lower boundary member is
\([Q(0),Q(c)]\). If \(Q(0)=-\infty\), it is a semi-infinite limiting interval
with a finite population-risk infimum. If \(Q(0)\) is finite, every pair
\((a,Q(c))\) with
\(a\le Q(0)\) has the same population loss, so a support constraint is needed
to choose a unique inactive lower root. The upper boundary has the symmetric
qualification. For
\(\delta<\delta_-\) or \(\delta>\delta_+\), the unrestricted population risk
is unbounded below along the corresponding escaping-root direction and has no
finite global target.

MPI and equal-tailed recovery use
\[
\delta_{\mathrm{MPI}}=0,\qquad
\delta_{\mathrm{ET}}=M_c\{(1-c)/2\}-\mu,
\]
whereas shortest-contiguous recovery is generally set-valued. Define
\[
\Delta_{\mathrm{SH}}
=
\{M_c(u)-\mu:u\in S_{\mathrm{SH}}\}.
\]
If \(S_{\mathrm{SH}}\) is a singleton, its element and tilt are denoted
by \(u_{\mathrm{SH}}\) and \(\delta_{\mathrm{SH}}\). These identities establish
population representation. Finite-sample recovery-tilt estimation requires an
external selection protocol.

Implicit differentiation yields
\[
\frac{du_\delta}{d\delta}
=\frac{c}{W_c(u_\delta)}
\]
and
\[
\frac{dW_c(u_\delta)}{d\delta}
=\frac{c\,W_c'(u_\delta)}{W_c(u_\delta)}.
\]
Hence profiling population width along the interior admissible tilt path
identifies any interior shortest-contiguous member. Boundary members use the
one-sided closure described above, and the statement does not extend
the target to disconnected highest-density regions.

\subsection{Cornish--Fisher Tilt Approximations}
\label{sec:supp-cf-tilt}

The recovery identities also give a simple initialization approximation. Let
\(Z=(Y-\mu)/\sigma\) have small standardized skewness \(\gamma_1\), and let
\(z_p=\Phi^{-1}(p)\). The formal first-order Cornish--Fisher expansion
\citep{CornishFisher1937}
\[
Q_Z(p)
 \approx
z_p+\frac{\gamma_1}{6}(z_p^2-1)
\]
approximates the quantile function of \(Z\) around the standard Normal law.
For content \(c\), define
\[
q_c=\Phi^{-1}\{(1+c)/2\},\qquad
K_c=q_c\phi(q_c)/c.
\]
Integrating the expansion over the equal-tailed probability window gives
\[
d_{\mathrm{ET}}^{\mathrm{CF}}
\approx
-\frac{\gamma_1}{3}K_c,
\qquad d=\delta/\sigma.
\]
Applying the same expansion to the shortest-window first-order condition
\(W_c'(u)=0\) and then evaluating the retained-window mean gives
\[
d_{\mathrm{SH}}^{\mathrm{CF}}
\approx
-\gamma_1K_c.
\]
Thus the shortest-oriented first-order tilt is three times the equal-tailed
one on the standardized scale. The sign convention is useful for checking
numerical calculations: right skewness gives negative recovery tilts, while left
skewness gives positive recovery tilts.

These displays are used as first-order approximations, not as a universal remainder
theorem. An \(O(\epsilon^2)\) statement is defensible only for an explicitly
indexed near-Normal family \(F_\epsilon\) whose quantile function and
derivative admit a uniform expansion
\[
Q_\epsilon(p)
=z_p+\epsilon\kappa_3(z_p^2-1)/6+r_\epsilon(p),
\qquad
\sup_p\{|r_\epsilon(p)|+|\partial_pr_\epsilon(p)|\}=O(\epsilon^2),
\]
on a neighborhood of the central window, with
\(\gamma_1(F_\epsilon)=\epsilon\kappa_3+O(\epsilon^2)\). Small skewness by
itself does not control higher cumulants or the quantile remainder.

For a known population law these formulas use the true \(\gamma_1\) and
\(\sigma\). A finite-sample plug-in version may replace them by the adjusted
Fisher--Pearson skewness \(\widehat\gamma_1\) and training standard deviation
\(\widehat\sigma\):
\[
\widehat d_{\mathrm{SH}}^{\mathrm{CF}}
=
-\widehat\gamma_1K_c,\qquad
\widehat d_{\mathrm{ET}}^{\mathrm{CF}}
=
\widehat d_{\mathrm{SH}}^{\mathrm{CF}}/3,\qquad
\widehat\delta=\widehat\sigma\,\widehat d.
\]
The plug-in quantities are fixed tilt choices for initialization and external
validation. A posterior treatment of tilt, or reuse of
the MPI hierarchical-scale update at nonzero tilt, would require a separate
model. Strong skewness, finite-support boundary solutions, and heavy-tailed
cases can move the first-order shortest approximation outside the admissible
finite-root tilt range. For this reason, the numerical summaries include adjusted
skewness, excess kurtosis, standardized sixth moment, probability-window
clipping, boundary proximity, and bootstrap sensitivity summaries. The main
article's deterministic Cornish--Fisher figure and population comparison table
illustrate both near-Normal accuracy and material departures under stronger
skewness or support-boundary geometry. These calculations assess the
approximation, while empirical validation and held-out selection rules require
separate study. The table reports the population values used to produce
the display.

The tilt indexes the estimand and is initially treated like a fixed quantile
level. A scalar tilt generally defines only a restricted global target in a
regression model; pointwise equal-tailed or shortest recovery generally
requires \(\delta(x)\). Sampling \(\delta\) as an ordinary unknown would mix
different interval functionals. Data-driven tilt selection therefore requires
a separate selection rule based on independent test data. Under
positive affine transformation \(Y^\star=r+sY\), the corresponding target uses
\(\delta^\star=s\delta\), as stated in
Section~\ref{sec:supp-population-target}.

These population equations do not establish finite-sample propriety under
arbitrary root priors. For a fixed learning rate and a finite-dimensional
Gaussian root prior, the MPI loss is nonnegative and the tilt is
linear in the roots. The resulting generalized posterior is proper because a
Gaussian density has finite exponential moments in every linear direction. This covers
the initial ridge fixed-design model and finite-horizon Gaussian state model.
Nonzero tilt under heavier-tailed shrinkage priors requires a separate
coercivity or propriety result.

\section{Empirical Balance and Static Uncertainty}
\label{sec:supp-static-theory}

\subsection{Fractional Empirical Balance}

Finite-sample score balance is fractional because observations may lie exactly
on fitted roots. For intercept-only roots and fixed \(\delta\), define
\[
R_n(a,b)
=\sum_{i=1}^n
\left[
\rho_c\{(y_i-a)(y_i-b)\}
-c\delta(a+b-2y_i)
\right],
\qquad a<b.
\]
Let \((\widehat a,\widehat b)\) be an interior subgradient-stationary point.
For each observation, choose
\[
s_i\in\partial\rho_c(\widehat e_i),
\qquad
\widehat e_i=(y_i-\widehat a)(y_i-\widehat b),
\]
where \(s_i=c-1\) if \(\widehat e_i<0\), \(s_i=c\) if
\(\widehat e_i>0\), and \(s_i\in[c-1,c]\) if \(\widehat e_i=0\). Set
\(\widehat q_i=c-s_i\). Then
\(\widehat q_i=1\) strictly inside the fitted interval,
\(\widehat q_i=0\) strictly outside, and
\(\widehat q_i\in[0,1]\) only at fitted roots.

\begin{theorem}[Exact fractional empirical balance]
\label{thm:supp-fractional-balance}
At any interior subgradient-stationary intercept-only MTI solution,
there are allocations \(\widehat q_i\in[0,1]\) with the membership convention
above such that
\[
\sum_{i=1}^n\widehat q_i=nc,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^n\widehat q_i y_i
=nc(\overline y+\delta).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The endpoint subgradient equations are
\[
\sum_{i=1}^n s_i(\widehat b-y_i)-nc\delta=0,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^n s_i(\widehat a-y_i)-nc\delta=0.
\]
Subtracting them and using \(\widehat b-\widehat a>0\) gives
\(\sum_i s_i=0\), hence \(\sum_i\widehat q_i=nc\). Substituting this identity
back into either endpoint equation gives
\[
\sum_{i=1}^n(\widehat q_i-c)y_i=nc\delta,
\]
which is the retained-mean balance.
\end{proof}

If \(B_n=\#\{i:\widehat e_i=0\}\), then the theorem immediately implies
\[
P_n(\widehat a<Y<\widehat b)\le c\le
P_n(\widehat a\le Y\le\widehat b),
\qquad
\left|P_n(\widehat a<Y<\widehat b)-c\right|\le B_n/n.
\]
For iid intercept-only data, Massart's sharp DKW inequality
\citep{Massart1990DKW} gives, with probability at least \(1-\alpha\),
\[
\left|\Pr_F(\widehat a<Y<\widehat b)-c\right|
\le
\frac{B_n}{n}+
\sqrt{\frac{2\log(2/\alpha)}{n}}.
\]
The bound is conservative and high probability. Exact nominal future coverage
and restricted-regression extensions require separate arguments.

For linear roots in a globally ordered chart, the same subgradient selection
gives
\begin{align}
\sum_i X_i\left[(c-\widehat q_i)(\widehat b_i-y_i)-c\delta_i\right]&=0,\\
\sum_i X_i\left[(c-\widehat q_i)(\widehat a_i-y_i)-c\delta_i\right]&=0.
\end{align}
Subtracting yields the width-weighted calibration equation
\[
\sum_i X_i\widehat w_i(c-\widehat q_i)=0,
\qquad \widehat w_i=\widehat b_i-\widehat a_i.
\]
This is the finite-sample analogue of the restricted population score. Pointwise
conditional coverage requires correct specification and a sufficiently rich
predictor class.

\subsection{Fixed-Dimensional Independent-Sample Uncertainty}

The static asymptotic statements used for uncertainty calibration are
fixed-dimensional iid random-design statements. Let \(Z=(X,Y)\) be iid with
bounded \(X\), fixed \(c\), fixed externally specified \(\delta(X)\), and fixed
learning rate \(\omega_{\mathrm R}\). Work on an ordered chart with
\[
a_\theta(X)=X^\top\beta_L,\qquad
b_\theta(X)=X^\top\beta_U,\qquad
\inf_X\{b_{\theta_0}(X)-a_{\theta_0}(X)\}>0
\]
in a neighborhood of the unique population minimizer \(\theta_0\). Assume
finite second response moment, positive continuous conditional endpoint
densities, positive-definite curvature, global risk separation, and a prior
that is positive and continuous near \(\theta_0\). These assumptions exclude
crossing raw linear roots after pointwise sorting, deterministic fixed-design
triangular arrays, serial dependence, selected DESN reservoirs, growing
dimension, random \(\kappa\), and same-sample tilt selection.

Let \(w_0(X)=b_{\theta_0}(X)-a_{\theta_0}(X)\) and
\[
p_0(X)=\Pr\{a_{\theta_0}(X)<Y<b_{\theta_0}(X)\mid X\}.
\]
Writing the endpoint score vector as \(\psi_0(Z)\), define
\[
J=\operatorname{Var}\{\psi_0(Z)\}.
\]
The expected-risk Hessian is
\[
H=
\begin{pmatrix}
\E\{XX^\top w_0(X)f_{Y\mid X}(a_{\theta_0}\mid X)\}
&
\E\{XX^\top[c-p_0(X)]\}\\
\E\{XX^\top[c-p_0(X)]\}
&
\E\{XX^\top w_0(X)f_{Y\mid X}(b_{\theta_0}\mid X)\}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Under the standard nonsmooth M-estimation conditions for the MPI
loss, the empirical minimizer satisfies
\[
\sqrt n(\widehat\theta-\theta_0)
\Rightarrow
\Normal(0,H^{-1}JH^{-1}).
\]
Under the corresponding generalized-posterior concentration and local
asymptotic-quadratic conditions \citep{Miller2021GeneralizedPosteriors},
the fixed-rate generalized posterior satisfies the local Bernstein--von
Mises approximation
\[
\sqrt n(\theta-\widehat\theta)\mid\vect y
\approx
\Normal\{0,(\omega_{\mathrm R}H)^{-1}\}.
\]
Thus the generalized posterior is centered at the same first-order target
but has curvature covariance, not generally the frequentist sandwich
covariance.

The full joint covariance is matched by a scalar learning rate if and only if
\[
J=\omega_{\mathrm R}^{-1}H.
\]
Otherwise different endpoint, midpoint, width, or coefficient contrasts may
prefer different scalar rates. If \(H\) and \(J\) are positive definite and
consistent estimators \(\widehat H,\widehat J\) are available, the
open-faced-sandwich post-processing transformation
\citep{Shaby2014OpenFacedSandwich}
\[
\theta^{\mathrm{adj}}
=\widehat\theta+
\widehat\Sigma_F^{1/2}\widehat\Sigma_G^{-1/2}
(\theta-\widehat\theta),
\quad
\widehat\Sigma_F=\widehat H^{-1}\widehat J\widehat H^{-1},\quad
\widehat\Sigma_G=(\omega_{\mathrm R}\widehat H)^{-1},
\]
has the desired first-order covariance for static coefficient and
differentiable endpoint-function summaries. This is a calibration of
generalized-posterior draws. It is not equivalent to declaring an ordinary
response likelihood, and it is not a dynamic-state or DESN forecasting
theorem.

\section{Width-Regularized Interval Loss}
\label{sec:supp-rqr-w}

The width-regularized variant in \citet{PouplinEtAl2024RQR} has a different
population score system from the MPI loss. For comparison, they use the RQR-W
population objective
\[
\E\left[\rho_q\{(Y-a)(Y-b)\}\right]
+\frac{\lambda_W}{2}(b-a)^2,
\qquad \lambda_W\ge0.
\]
In the present terminology, RQR-W is a width-regularized modification of the
MPI loss, not the MPI loss itself.
At a nondegenerate interior stationary point, subtracting the two endpoint
score equations gives
\[
\Pr(a<Y<b)=q-2\lambda_W.
\]
Thus \(q=c+2\lambda_W\) restores content \(c\), subject to
\(0\le\lambda_W<(1-c)/2\). The midpoint equation then gives
\[
\E(Y\mid a<Y<b)
=\mu+\frac{2\lambda_W}{c}(\mu-m),
\qquad m=\frac{a+b}{2}.
\]
Except in special symmetric cases, RQR-W therefore changes the
mean-preserving placement equation. It is a coverage-corrected compromise
between MPI risk and squared width, not a tie-breaker among
unidentified MPI minimizers.

More generally, if a differentiable width penalty \(P(w)\), \(w=b-a\), is
added to a check loss at level \(q\), the coverage equation is
\[
\Pr(a<Y<b)=q-\frac{2P'(w)}{w}.
\]
A correction independent of the distribution and realized width is possible
only when \(P'(w)/w\) is constant, which yields a quadratic penalty up to an
additive constant. This explains both the form of RQR-W and why its path does
not generally coincide with the shortest-contiguous path.

The computation part supports the main article's fixed-target generalized

\section{Pseudo-AL Augmentation and Full Conditionals}
\label{sec:supp-pseudo-al}

The Gibbs sampler is obtained by augmenting the product-residual loss factor in
the generalized posterior. Response likelihoods belong to separately specified
response-distribution models. For
\(e_i=(y_i-\eta_{1i})(y_i-\eta_{2i})\), the MPI contribution is
\(\ell_c(\eta_{1i},\eta_{2i};y_i)=\rho_c(e_i)\), as in
\eqref{eq:supp-mpi-product-check}. The fixed-rate generalized posterior
therefore updates the prior by
\[
\exp\left\{-\omega_{\mathrm R}\sum_{i\in I_{\mathrm{obs}}}
\ell_c(\eta_{1i},\eta_{2i};y_i)\right\}.
\]
The following normal--exponential identity provides a latent-variable
representation adapted from the standard
asymmetric-Laplace quantile-regression augmentation
\citep{KozumiKobayashi2011Gibbs}. Setting
\[
r_i=y_i^2,\qquad
m_i^{\mathrm R}=y_i(\eta_{1i}+\eta_{2i})-\eta_{1i}\eta_{2i}
\]
gives \(r_i-m_i^{\mathrm R}=e_i\). With
\[
\sigma_{\mathrm R}=\omega_{\mathrm R}^{-1},\qquad
\xi_c=\frac{1-2c}{c(1-c)},\qquad
\phi_c=\frac{2}{c(1-c)},
\]
the normalized asymmetric-Laplace kernel in the pseudo-residual is
\begin{align}
&\frac{c(1-c)}{\sigma_{\mathrm R}}
\exp\{-\rho_c(e_i)/\sigma_{\mathrm R}\}\notag\\
&\quad=\int_0^\infty
\Normal\!\left(e_i\mid\xi_cv_i,\phi_c\sigma_{\mathrm R}v_i\right)
\Exp\!\left(v_i\mid\mathrm{rate}=1/\sigma_{\mathrm R}\right)\,dv_i.
\label{eq:pseudo-al-mixture}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:pseudo-al-mixture} augments the single-observation factor of
the generalized posterior with a positive latent scale. Its left-hand side is
evaluated at a residual product containing \(y_i\), so it is a density in the
abstract pseudo-residual. Missing sites are excluded from \(I_{\mathrm{obs}}\) and
have no latent scale.

The latent-scale full conditional uses the \(\GIG(\nu,a,b)\) convention
proportional to \(v^{\nu-1}\exp\{-(av+b/v)/2\}\):
\[
v_i\mid\cdot\sim
\GIG\left\{
\frac12,\
\frac{1}{2\sigma_{\mathrm R}c(1-c)},\
\frac{c(1-c)e_i^2}{2\sigma_{\mathrm R}}
\right\}.
\]
At \(e_i=0\), this reduces to
\(v_i\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(1/2,\mathrm{rate}=a/2)\). For \(e_i\ne0\), the
calculation uses the reciprocal identity
\[
v_i^{-1}\sim\mathrm{IG}\left(\sqrt{a/b_i},a\right),
\]
where the inverse-Gaussian parameters are mean and shape.

\section{Static Endpoint Regression and ECM}
\label{sec:supp-static-regression}

\subsection{Gaussian Root-Coefficient Updates}

Static regression is the base computation for ridge endpoint regression and
fixed DESN features. Let
\(\mat W_v=\diag\{(\phi_c\sigma_{\mathrm R}v_i)^{-1}:i\in I_{\mathrm{obs}}\}\).
Conditional on the second root, define
\[
\mat A_1=\diag(\vect y_{\mathrm{obs}}-\vect\eta_{2,\mathrm{obs}})
\mat X_{\mathrm{obs}},\qquad
\vect z_1=\vect y_{\mathrm{obs}}\circ
(\vect y_{\mathrm{obs}}-\vect\eta_{2,\mathrm{obs}})
-\xi_c\vect v_{\mathrm{obs}}.
\]
For Gaussian prior canonical parameters \((\mat Q_{01},\vect h_{01})\),
\[
\mat Q_1=\mat Q_{01}+\mat A_1^\top\mat W_v\mat A_1,\qquad
\vect h_1=\vect h_{01}+\mat A_1^\top\mat W_v\vect z_1.
\]
The Gaussian full conditional is
\[
\vect\beta_1\mid\cdot\sim
\Normal(\mat Q_1^{-1}\vect h_1,\mat Q_1^{-1}).
\]
The second root uses the same update after exchanging labels. A proper ridge
prior enters through the same Gaussian canonical form. The MPI
conditionally Gaussian shrinkage specification uses the
regularized-horseshoe Nishimura--Suchard augmentation
(RHS-NS), with separate
states for the two roots; this supplement does not assert an RHS-NS hierarchy
beyond that conditional representation or nonzero-tilt propriety for the
random-scale representation.

\subsection{Fixed-Rate Mean-Tilt Shift}

Let \(\vect\delta_{\mathrm{obs}}\) be fixed and define
\[
L_{n,c,\vect\delta}
=\sum_{i\in I_{\mathrm{obs}}}\left[
\rho_c(e_i)-c\delta_i(\eta_{1i}+\eta_{2i}-2y_i)
\right].
\]
For a fixed learning rate \(\omega_{\mathrm R}\), the full-conditional precision
\(\mat Q_1\) above is unchanged, while its canonical vector becomes
\begin{equation}
\vect h_1^{\mathrm{MT}}
=\vect h_1+
\omega_{\mathrm R}c\mat X_{\mathrm{obs}}^\top\vect\delta_{\mathrm{obs}}.
\label{eq:mean-tilt-canonical-shift}
\end{equation}
Root 2 receives the same shift. The latent GIG update is unchanged because the
tilt contains no \(v_i\). Setting \(\vect\delta=\vect 0\) recovers MPI
exactly.

Equation~\eqref{eq:mean-tilt-canonical-shift} is the algebraic identity used by
the fixed-rate ridge sampler. Validation must verify exact
zero-tilt equivalence, unchanged
precisions and latent-scale conditionals, root-exchange invariance, affine
equivariance with a correspondingly transformed learning rate, and
intercept-only agreement with direct numerical quadrature.

For finite \(I_{\mathrm{obs}}\), fixed \(\omega_{\mathrm R}\), and proper Gaussian
root priors, the nonzero-tilt generalized posterior is proper. Indeed, the
MPI generalized-posterior factor is at most one and the remaining tilt
factor is the exponential of a finite linear functional of
\((\vect\beta_1,\vect\beta_2)\); a Gaussian prior
has a finite moment-generating function in every finite direction. This
argument covers ridge priors. It does not establish the corresponding result
for the horseshoe-family random-scale representation.

\subsection{Fixed-Target ECM Mode Algorithm}

The same pseudo-asymmetric-Laplace augmentation gives a deterministic
expectation/conditional-maximization algorithm for a fixed generalized-posterior
mode \citep{DempsterLairdRubin1977EM,MengRubin1993ECM}. It is a computational
method for the generalized-posterior kernel
\(\exp\{-\omega_{\mathrm R}L_{n,c,\vect\delta}\}\pi_0(\vartheta)\),
not an ordinary likelihood EM algorithm for responses. Related
asymmetric-Laplace quantile-regression computations use the same
normal--exponential idea in a response working-likelihood setting
\citep{ZhaoLian2016EMBayesianQR}; here the augmented residual is the MTI
pseudo-residual product.

For a GIG variable under the convention above,
\[
E(V^r)=\left(\frac ba\right)^{r/2}
\frac{K_{\nu+r}(\sqrt{ab})}{K_\nu(\sqrt{ab})}.
\]
With \(\nu=1/2\) and \(r=-1\), \(K_{-1/2}=K_{1/2}\). Therefore, for
\(e_i\ne0\),
\[
\tau_i
=E(v_i^{-1}\mid\cdot)
=\sqrt{\frac{a}{b_i}}
=\frac{1}{c(1-c)|e_i|}.
\]
The first latent moment,
\[
E(v_i\mid\cdot)
=c(1-c)(|e_i|+2\sigma_{\mathrm R}),
\]
is useful diagnostically. The ECM weight is \(\tau_i\), which distinguishes
ECM from the current coordinate-Gaussian variational Bayes (VB) approximation;
the latter plugs in \(E(v_i)\) and recomputes that mean between root updates.

Holding \(\vect\beta_2\) fixed, write
\[
\mat A_2=\diag(\vect y_{\mathrm{obs}}-\vect\eta_{2,\mathrm{obs}})
\mat X_{\mathrm{obs}},\qquad
\vect c_2=\vect y_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\,2}
-\vect y_{\mathrm{obs}}\circ\vect\eta_{2,\mathrm{obs}}.
\]
With ridge prior precision \(\mat P_1\), the first conditional maximization
solves
\[
\mat\Lambda_1
=\mat P_1+
\frac{1}{\phi_c\sigma_{\mathrm R}}
\mat A_2^\top\diag(\vect\tau)\mat A_2,
\]
\[
\vect h_1
=
\frac{1}{\phi_c\sigma_{\mathrm R}}
\mat A_2^\top\{\diag(\vect\tau)\vect c_2-\xi_c\vect 1\}
+\omega_{\mathrm R}c\mat X_{\mathrm{obs}}^\top\vect\delta_{\mathrm{obs}},
\qquad
\vect\beta_1^{+}=\mat\Lambda_1^{-1}\vect h_1.
\]
Then, using \(\vect\beta_1^{+}\) and the same outer E-step weights
\(\vect\tau\),
\[
\mat A_1^{+}=\diag(\vect y_{\mathrm{obs}}-\vect\eta_{1,\mathrm{obs}}^{+})
\mat X_{\mathrm{obs}},\qquad
\vect c_1^{+}=\vect y_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\,2}
-\vect y_{\mathrm{obs}}\circ\vect\eta_{1,\mathrm{obs}}^{+},
\]
\[
\mat\Lambda_2
=\mat P_2+
\frac{1}{\phi_c\sigma_{\mathrm R}}
(\mat A_1^{+})^\top\diag(\vect\tau)\mat A_1^{+},
\]
\[
\vect h_2
=
\frac{1}{\phi_c\sigma_{\mathrm R}}
(\mat A_1^{+})^\top\{\diag(\vect\tau)\vect c_1^{+}-\xi_c\vect 1\}
+\omega_{\mathrm R}c\mat X_{\mathrm{obs}}^\top\vect\delta_{\mathrm{obs}},
\qquad
\vect\beta_2^{+}=\mat\Lambda_2^{-1}\vect h_2.
\]
The update is ECM because the augmented criterion is quadratic in one complete
root block when the other is fixed, but quartic in both root blocks jointly.

At \(e_i=0\), \(\tau_i\) is infinite. The numerical ECM calculation therefore uses a
prespecified safeguarded generalized ECM/MM variant with a response-product-scale
floor, exact observed-objective backtracking after each accepted full cycle,
and deterministic multi-start selection by the exact penalized objective. The
reported endpoint mode is a deterministic fixed-target generalized-posterior
summary. Posterior draws require the Gibbs sampler, and tolerance actions
require the scan-calibrated decision rule.

\subsection{MPI-Only RHS-NS Prior Specification}
\label{sec:supp-rhs-ns}

The MPI fixed-design sampler also supports a conditionally Gaussian RHS-NS
prior specification based on the Nishimura--Suchard representation
\citep{CarvalhoPolsonScott2010HS,PiironenVehtari2017RHS,
NishimuraSuchard2023SSS}. For root \(k\), let
\[
\vect\psi_k
=\{\vect\lambda_k^2,\vect\nu_k,\tau_k^2,\xi_k,\zeta_k^2\}
\]
denote its local, auxiliary, global, and shoulder-scale state. This representation
requires the first design column to be the intercept
and excludes its coefficient from shrinkage. For each remaining coefficient,
\(j=2,\ldots,p\), the conditional prior precision is
\begin{equation}
d_{kj}
=\frac{1}{\tau_k^2\lambda_{kj}^2}+\frac{1}{\zeta_k^2}.
\label{eq:supp-rhs-ns-precision}
\end{equation}
The first coefficient instead receives its separately specified fixed Gaussian
precision. Substituting the resulting
\(\mat Q_{0k}(\vect\psi_k)\) into the Gaussian full conditional above gives the
coefficient update; \(\vect\psi_k\) is then updated conditional
on the newly drawn \(\vect\beta_k\). Root 1 and root 2 have separate,
identically specified states. Any exchangeable label swap must therefore move
\((\vect\beta_k,\vect\psi_k)\) together.

Equation~\eqref{eq:supp-rhs-ns-precision} specifies the conditional
prior representation. The marginal RHS-NS prior is inherited from the
Nishimura--Suchard representation rather than rederived here. Prior-state construction
and updating follow that reference construction.
The calculations here use the \texttt{rhs\_ns} specification, separate from the generic
\texttt{rhs} specification. It is MCMC-only and
restricted here to MPI, \(\vect\delta=\vect 0\). The proper-Gaussian argument
for the nonzero-tilt generalized posterior has not been extended through its
random-scale mixture, so neither MTI-RHS-NS propriety nor a corresponding
sampler is claimed.

\subsection{Endpoint Regression with Fixed DESN Features}
\label{sec:supp-desn}

Let \(\mat X_{\mathrm{DESN}}\) denote the complete deterministic DESN feature
design, including any specified intercept column
\citep{Jaeger2001EchoState,GallicchioMicheliPedrelli2018DeepESNDesign,
McDermottWikle2018DeepESNUQ}. The reservoir seed, depth, units, lag window,
spectral radius, leak rate, input scaling, washout, preprocessing, tuning

With \(\mat X=\mat X_{\mathrm{DESN}}\), MPI endpoint regression with fixed DESN

conditionals above. It
introduces neither a new loss nor a separate Gibbs sampler. A proper ridge
prior supports the fixed-rate nonzero-tilt full conditionals considered here; the
RHS-NS representation and matched
evidence remain MPI only.

A future design \(\mat X_{\mathrm{DESN,fut}}\) must be generated by the same
fixed feature construction, without using held-out responses to refit the
features. Generalized-posterior summaries are ordered endpoint functions,
widths, and midpoints formed from coefficient draws. The training and future
feature matrices provide deterministic endpoint features; response simulation

\section{MPI-Only Hierarchical Loss Scale}
\label{sec:supp-learned-scale}

A hierarchical inverse-loss scale is a separate MPI generalized-posterior
convention. Let \(s_L>0\) be a specified reference loss scale and set
\(\sigma_{\mathrm R}=s_L/\kappa\). With
\(L_{n,c}(\vartheta)=
\sum_{i\in I_{\mathrm{obs}}}\rho_c(e_i)\), the hierarchical generalized posterior is
\begin{equation}
\pi(\vartheta,\kappa\mid\vect y)
\propto
\pi(\vartheta)\pi_\kappa(\kappa)
\kappa^{n_{\mathrm{obs}}}
\exp\{-\kappa L_{n,c}(\vartheta)/s_L\},
\label{eq:normalized-scale-target}
\end{equation}
where \(\vartheta\) denotes both roots and, when present, their proper
root-specific prior or evolution states. For
\(\kappa\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(a_\kappa,b_\kappa)\) in shape--rate form,
\begin{equation}
\kappa\mid\vartheta,\vect y
\sim\mathrm{Gamma}\left(a_\kappa+n_{\mathrm{obs}},
b_\kappa+L_{n,c}(\vartheta)/s_L\right).
\label{eq:kappa-collapsed}
\end{equation}

For any proper MPI root and prior-state law \(\pi(\vartheta)\), the joint
generalized posterior in \eqref{eq:normalized-scale-target} is proper. Indeed,
\(L_{n,c}(\vartheta)\geq0\), so its exponential factor is at most one,
and the remaining upper bound integrates to the finite
\(n_{\mathrm{obs}}\)th moment of the Gamma prior. This argument includes the
specified MPI RHS-NS representation when its complete prior-state law and update
are retained. It does not cover nonzero mean tilt, whose tilted loss need not
be nonnegative.

We call \eqref{eq:normalized-scale-target} the
\emph{pseudo-residual-normalized hierarchical-scale generalized posterior}. The
factor \(\kappa^{n_{\mathrm{obs}}}\) normalizes an asymmetric-Laplace kernel in
the abstract pseudo-residual \(e_i\). It is an analyst-specified
hierarchical generalized-posterior specification; \(\kappa\) is not an
ordinary response parameter. Same-data estimation or calibration of this scale
requires assumptions beyond fixed-rate generalized-posterior coherence
\citep{LeeLiuNicholls2025LearningRate,WuMartin2023LearningRateComparison}.
Fixed-rate sensitivity analyses are therefore primary. If \(s_L\) is estimated
from the training responses, the calculation rule and resulting value are fixed
before MCMC; this is a data-dependent empirical-Bayes
specification.

For reproducibility, the input name \texttt{lambda} in the accompanying code
refers to the manuscript's \(\kappa\) in this calculation; it is unrelated to
the RQR-W penalty \(\lambda_W\).

This hierarchical-scale generalized posterior applies to MPI only. The
mean-tilted loss can be negative, and parameter-independent additive constants
at fixed
\(\omega_{\mathrm R}\) become consequential when the learning rate is random.
The Gamma full conditionals below must not be reused for MTI without
a separately normalized, proper joint generalized posterior. The initial
mean-tilted sampler is therefore restricted to fixed learning rates.

If one conditions additionally on \(\vect v\), the full conditional under the
augmented generalized posterior is
\begin{align}
\kappa\mid\vartheta,\vect v,\vect y
\sim\mathrm{Gamma}\Bigg(&a_\kappa+\frac{3n_{\mathrm{obs}}}{2},\notag\\
&b_\kappa+\frac{1}{s_L}\sum_{i\in I_{\mathrm{obs}}}
\left[v_i+\frac{(e_i-\xi_cv_i)^2}{2\phi_cv_i}\right]\Bigg).
\label{eq:kappa-augmented}
\end{align}

The sensitivity pure-loss generalized posterior omits
\(\kappa^{n_{\mathrm{obs}}}\). Its collapsed shape is
\(a_\kappa\), and a compatible augmented representation has shape
\(a_\kappa+n_{\mathrm{obs}}/2\). These alternatives define different
generalized posteriors; they are not two representations of the same posterior
distribution.

\begin{algorithmblock}{MPI random-\(\kappa\) partially collapsed scan}
\label{alg:supp-learned-kappa}
\item \textbf{Generalized posterior and applicability.} Use
\eqref{eq:normalized-scale-target}, restricted to MPI
\((\delta=0)\). It applies to a proper Gaussian/ridge fixed design, the
MPI RHS-NS representation defined above, or a specified fixed-joint dynamic
model whose root and prior-state updates are otherwise valid.
\item \textbf{Inputs and fixed quantities.} Freeze
\(c,s_L,a_\kappa,b_\kappa,I_{\mathrm{obs}}\), the root priors, all design or
evolution quantities, iteration controls, and the label-swap rule.
\item \textbf{Initialization.} Choose finite raw roots, \(\kappa>0\), positive
initial latent scales at observed sites, and valid MPI-only prior or
evolution states.
\item \textbf{One complete sweep.} Repeat the following mandatory
partial-collapse order for every warm-up and sampling iteration:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=1.8em,itemsep=0.15em,topsep=0.15em]
\item draw \(\kappa\) from \eqref{eq:kappa-collapsed} using the current roots;
\item immediately refresh every \(v_i\), \(i\in I_{\mathrm{obs}}\), from its GIG
conditional using the new \(\kappa\);
\item update root 1 conditional on root 2 and the refreshed scales;
\item update root 2 conditional on the new root 1 and refreshed scales;
\item update every specified MPI-only prior or evolution state; and
\item if exchangeability permits, swap the two complete raw-root blocks and
their associated states together.
\end{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Stored quantities and label handling.} Retain ordered
endpoints, widths, midpoints, losses, \(\kappa\), the target specification,
missingness indicators, and label-swap indicators. Omit the swap unless the complete
root-specific blocks are exchangeable.
\item \textbf{Loss-scale interpretation.} This is the MPI-only hierarchical inverse-loss
scale convention. Its \(\kappa\) is neither a response variance nor an
automatic coverage-calibration parameter.
\item \textbf{Target and applicability.} The displayed order is an exact
partially collapsed sampler for the specified generalized posterior in its
validated fixed-joint modes. It applies to MPI and must not
be generalized to nonzero tilt by copying the Gamma update.
\end{algorithmblock}
Store draws only according to the MCMC retention rule. The collapsed
\(\kappa\) draw must be followed immediately by the latent-scale refresh;
skipping that refresh would define a different, invalid sampler.

\section{Dynamic Endpoint-State Computation}
\label{sec:supp-dynamic}

The dynamic sampler keeps the interval target fixed while replacing static
coefficients by root-specific state trajectories. For root \(k=1,2\), consider
the dynamic linear model (DLM) representation
\citep{GoncalvesMigonBastos2020DQLM}
\[
\eta_{kt}=f_t^\top\theta_{kt},\qquad
\theta_{k0}\sim\Normal(m_0,C_0),\qquad
\theta_{kt}=G_t\theta_{k,t-1}+\epsilon_{kt},\qquad
\epsilon_{kt}\sim\Normal(0,W_{kt}).
\]
Equivalently, define
\[
\alpha_t=
\begin{pmatrix}\theta_{1t}\\\theta_{2t}\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
G_t^{\mathrm{stk}}=
\begin{pmatrix}G_t&0\\0&G_t\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
W_t^{\mathrm{stk}}=
\begin{pmatrix}W_{1t}&0\\0&W_{2t}\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then \(\alpha_t=G_t^{\mathrm{stk}}\alpha_{t-1}+\varepsilon_t\) is one stacked
Gaussian state prior. It does not yield one jointly Gaussian state-path full
conditional. Writing
\(\eta_{1t}=f_t^\top\theta_{1t}\) and
\(\eta_{2t}=f_t^\top\theta_{2t}\), the augmented generalized posterior contains
\[
\left\{(y_t-\eta_{1t})(y_t-\eta_{2t})-\xi_cv_t\right\}^2.
\]
Expansion produces \(\eta_{1t}^2\eta_{2t}^2\), so the joint log posterior is
quartic rather than quadratic in the stacked state. A conventional joint FFBS
draw is therefore
unavailable. Holding \(\eta_{2t}\) fixed makes the expression affine in
\(\eta_{1t}\), and holding \(\eta_{1t}\) fixed does the same for
\(\eta_{2t}\). Alternating the two
root-specific FFBS draws is thus an exact blocked Gibbs sampler for the
specified generalized posterior, not an approximation obtained by fitting the
roots separately. A
general non-Gaussian joint path sampler could target the same generalized
posterior, but would give up the closed-form Gaussian FFBS blocks.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\input{figures/rqr_dlm_blocked_state_schematic.tex}
\caption{\textbf{Blocked state update for MPI-DLM.}
The two root paths have a stacked Gaussian prior, but their joint contribution
to the augmented generalized posterior is quartic. Conditional on either complete root path
and the latent scales, the other path has an exact Gaussian full conditional.
The resulting sequential FFBS steps are exact blocked-Gibbs updates for the
specified fixed-joint generalized posterior. A fixed nonzero mean tilt adds observed-time
canonical information sites to the same root-specific FFBS blocks; it does not
create a simultaneous joint Gaussian path draw.}
\label{fig:supp-dlm-blocked-state}
\end{figure}

Conditional on \(\theta_{2,1:T}\), the observed latent scales, and the specified
inverse loss scale, root 1 has the following linear Gaussian full-conditional
representation:
\begin{align}
z_{1t}&=H_{1t}\theta_{1t}+\varepsilon_{1t},\\
z_{1t}&=y_t(y_t-\eta_{2t})-\xi_cv_t,\\
H_{1t}&=(y_t-\eta_{2t})f_t^\top,\\
\varepsilon_{1t}&\sim\Normal(0,V_t),\qquad
V_t=\phi_c\sigma_{\mathrm R}v_t.
\end{align}
Root 2 is obtained by exchanging labels. For fixed rate,
\(\sigma_{\mathrm R}=\omega_{\mathrm R}^{-1}\); the MPI-only hierarchical-scale
generalized posterior instead uses \(s_L/\kappa\).

For either conditional Gaussian system, write \(m_{t-1},C_{t-1}\) for filtered
moments. The forward recursion is
\begin{align}
a_t&=G_tm_{t-1}, & P_t&=G_tC_{t-1}G_t^\top,\\
R_t&=P_t+W_t, & q_t&=H_tR_tH_t^\top+V_t,\\
A_t&=R_tH_t^\top/q_t, & m_t&=a_t+A_t(z_t-H_ta_t),\\
C_t&=R_t-A_tq_tA_t^\top.
\end{align}
For fixed-rate MTI-DLM, define the observed-time canonical site
\[
g_t=\omega_{\mathrm R}c\delta_tf_t.
\]
This canonical update never divides by
\(y_t-\eta_{\mathrm{other},t}\) and remains well defined when that quantity is
zero.
Treat the displayed measurement-update mean as \(m_t^{(0)}\), then apply
\begin{equation}
m_t=m_t^{(0)}+C_tg_t
\label{eq:supp-dlm-tilt-filter-shift}
\end{equation}
before propagating to time \(t+1\); \(C_t\) is unchanged. At a missing
response, the measurement update, latent scale, and tilt site are all omitted.
Equivalently, if the zero-tilt conditional path has precision
\(Q_k^{\mathrm{path}}\) and mean \(m_k^{(0)}\), the tilted conditional mean is
\begin{equation}
m_k^{(0)}+\{Q_k^{\mathrm{path}}\}^{-1}g,
\label{eq:supp-dlm-tilt-path-shift}
\end{equation}
with \(g\) formed by stacking the observed \(g_t\) values and zeros elsewhere.
Equations~\eqref{eq:supp-dlm-tilt-filter-shift} and
\eqref{eq:supp-dlm-tilt-path-shift} are numerically equivalent reference
identities for fixed \(\mat W_t\) or a precomputed evolution template. They are
used for fixed-rate fixed-\(\mat W_t\) and precomputed-template dynamic
specifications. Random-rate, adaptive-discount, and shared component-scale tilted
extensions are not included in the present analysis.

Starting from \(\theta_T\sim\Normal(m_T,C_T)\), the backward draw for
\(t=T-1,\ldots,1\) uses
\begin{align}
B_t&=C_tG_{t+1}^\top R_{t+1}^{-1},\\
m_t^\star&=m_t+B_t(\theta_{t+1}-a_{t+1}),\\
C_t^\star&=C_t-B_tR_{t+1}B_t^\top,\\
\theta_t\mid\theta_{t+1},\cdot&\sim\Normal(m_t^\star,C_t^\star).
\end{align}

For fixed evolution covariances, the calculation integrates
\(\theta_{k0}\) out of the root-path update. Thus
\[
a_{k1}=G_1m_0,\qquad
R_{k1}=G_1C_0G_1^\top+W_1,
\]
and backward sampling terminates at \(t=1\). The component-scale extension
samples \(\theta_{k0}\) separately because its first innovation is required by
the conjugate scale update.

Missing responses skip the measurement update. The numerical calculation uses
Cholesky factorization and does not silently turn an indefinite covariance into
a positive one by taking absolute singular values. Under the default numerical
convention, a failed factorization or negative-eigenvalue projection stops the fit;
an exact positive-semidefinite draw with zero eigenvalues remains admissible.
The numerical calculation uses a prespecified jitter sequence and reports every covariance
adjustment. When an adjustment is required for a backward prior covariance,
the adjusted matrix is used consistently in the gain, smoothing covariance,
and path-draw covariance. Fixed-generalized-posterior fits with nonzero
covariance adjustments are excluded from exact-update summaries.
Symmetry and eigenvalue materiality are evaluated relative to the actual
matrix scale, including for matrices with entries below one. Jitter is likewise
matrix-relative except for an exactly zero matrix, for which use of the
prespecified sequence is reported as an absolute conservative alternative.

\section{Dynamic Discount Factors and Component Scales}
\label{sec:supp-discounts}

Discount factors affect the state prior and therefore the generalized posterior
being sampled. Let component discounts be \(\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_J\), with
\(0<\gamma_j\le1\), and let their state dimensions sum to \(p\). Define the
\(p\times p\) matrix \(D\) to have value
\((1-\gamma_j)/\gamma_j\) throughout the within-component block \(j\) and zero
between components. This reproduces the public \texttt{df}/\texttt{dim.df}
construction in exdqlm 1.1.0. The covariance recursion is
\[
P_t=G_tC_{t-1}G_t^\top,\qquad
W_t=D\circ P_t,\qquad
R_t=P_t+W_t,
\]
where \(\circ\) denotes an elementwise product. Thus a within-component
covariance block is inflated by \(1/\gamma_j\), whereas cross-component
covariances are not inflated.

We distinguish three covariance specifications:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.2em]
\item \texttt{fixed\_W}: \(W_{1:T}\) is supplied and fixed. This yields exact
Gaussian root-path full conditionals under the specified state prior.
\item \texttt{discount\_template}: a reference design and reference observation
variance generate \(W_{1:T}\) once through the recursion above. The sequence is
then fixed before MCMC. This also yields exact Gaussian root-path full
conditionals under the resulting prior, conditional on the precomputed template. If
the reference quantities are estimated from the training responses, the
resulting prior specification is
empirical Bayes and should be interpreted as such.
\item \texttt{adaptive\_discount}: \(W_t\) is recomputed inside each conditional
filter. In MTI, \(H_t\) and \(V_t\) depend on the other root, \(v_t\), and
\(\kappa\), so the implied evolution covariance also depends on those blocks.
The alternating updates omit terms that would generally arise from
such dependence in a fixed joint density. This specification is therefore exploratory
and is not described as an exact Gibbs update for a common generalized
posterior.
\end{enumerate}
The same discounts are used for both roots unless a future model deliberately
breaks their prior exchangeability.

\subsection{Compatibility Conditions for Adaptive Conditional Discounts}

The experimental designation can be made precise. Consider \(T=2\), \(p=1\),
\(f_t=G_t=1\), and a nonzero discount inflation
\(d_\gamma=(1-\gamma)/\gamma\).
For the root-1 conditional filter, the time-1 covariance and time-2 innovation
variance include
\[
C_{11}=\frac{R_{11}V_1}
{(y_1-\theta_{21})^2R_{11}+V_1},
\qquad
w_1(\theta_{21})=d_\gamma C_{11}.
\]
The conditional log density therefore contains
\[
-\frac12\log w_1(\theta_{21})
-\frac{(\theta_{12}-\theta_{11})^2}{2w_1(\theta_{21})},
\]
whose mixed derivative is generically
\[
\frac{\partial^2}{\partial\theta_{21}\partial\theta_{12}}
\log\pi(\vect\theta_1\mid\vect\theta_2,\cdot)
=
\frac{(\theta_{12}-\theta_{11})w_1'(\theta_{21})}
{w_1(\theta_{21})^2}\ne0.
\]
In the advertised root-2 conditional, the adaptive variance depends on
\(\theta_{11}\), not \(\theta_{12}\); its time-1 pseudo-observation does not
involve \(\theta_{12}\), and its time-2 pseudo-observation does not involve
\(\theta_{21}\). Hence the corresponding mixed derivative is zero. A positive
twice-differentiable joint density would require equality of these derivatives.
Except in degenerate cases such as \(d_\gamma=0\), the two conditional adaptive FFBS
kernels are therefore incompatible with a common smooth joint density while
retaining their advertised Gaussian forms.

\subsection{Exact Shared Component Scales for MPI}

Partition the state into \(J\) blocks of dimensions \(d_j\), let \(S_j\)
select block \(j\), and fix positive-definite templates \(Q_{jt}\). Define
\[
W_t(\vect q)=\operatorname{blockdiag}
\{q_1Q_{1t},\ldots,q_JQ_{Jt}\},
\qquad
q_j\sim\operatorname{IG}(a_j,b_j),
\]
where the inverse-Gamma density is proportional to
\(q_j^{-a_j-1}\exp(-b_j/q_j)\). The same \(q_j\) is shared by the two roots.
For
\[
d_{kjt}=S_j\{\theta_{kt}-G_t\theta_{k,t-1}\},
\]
the full conditional is
\[
q_j\mid\cdot\sim\operatorname{IG}\left(
a_j+Td_j,
b_j+\frac12\sum_{k=1}^2\sum_{t=1}^T
d_{kjt}^\top Q_{jt}^{-1}d_{kjt}
\right).
\]
The \(Td_j\) shape increment includes two roots, each contributing
\(Td_j/2\).

Because the fixed-\(W\) FFBS calculation integrates out the time-zero
state, the component-scale sampler restores it after each root-path draw. Its
conditional moments are
\begin{align}
C_{k0}^\star
&=\{C_0^{-1}+G_1^\top W_1(\vect q)^{-1}G_1\}^{-1},\\
m_{k0}^\star
&=C_{k0}^\star\{C_0^{-1}m_0+
G_1^\top W_1(\vect q)^{-1}\theta_{k1}\},
\end{align}
and
\[
\theta_{k0}\mid\theta_{k1},\vect q
\sim\Normal(m_{k0}^\star,C_{k0}^\star).
\]

The transition used here first reduces scale--trajectory dependence by
integrating one root path. Conditional on root 2 and the current latent scales,
the root-1 pseudo-observation equation has
\[
z_{1t}=y_t(y_t-\eta_{2t})-\xi_cv_t,\qquad
\vect h_{1t}=(y_t-\eta_{2t})\vect f_t,
\]
with observation variance \(V_t\); missing observations are omitted from the
filter update. Let \(\ell_{1,\mathrm K}(\vect q)\) denote the resulting
Gaussian Kalman-filter log marginal after integrating
\((\vect\theta_{1,1:T},\vect\theta_{10})\) under the initial law
\(\Normal(m_0,C_0)\). For the conditioned root, define
\[
E_{2j}=\frac12\sum_{t=1}^T
d_{2jt}^\top Q_{jt}^{-1}d_{2jt}.
\]
On the log scale \(x_j=\log q_j\), the partially collapsed log posterior for
the component scales is, up to a constant,
\begin{equation}
\log\pi(\vect x\mid
\vect\theta_{2,0:T},\vect v,\ldots)
=
\ell_{1,\mathrm K}(\exp\vect x)
-\sum_{j=1}^J\left[
\left(a_j+\frac{Td_j}{2}\right)x_j+
(b_j+E_{2j})e^{-x_j}
\right].
\label{eq:supp-component-scale-partial-collapse}
\end{equation}
Only the conditioned root contributes the \(Td_j/2\) innovation
normalization and \(E_{2j}\) term; the integrated root contributes through
\(\ell_{1,\mathrm K}\). Coordinate slice transitions leave
\eqref{eq:supp-component-scale-partial-collapse} invariant. Conditional on
the accepted scales, FFBS then redraws root 1 and its time-zero state. The
sampler next exchanges the root indices: it constructs
\((z_{2t},\vect h_{2t})\) conditional on the refreshed root 1, replaces
\((\ell_{1,\mathrm K},E_{2j})\) in
\eqref{eq:supp-component-scale-partial-collapse} by
\((\ell_{2,\mathrm K},E_{1j})\), updates \(\vect q\), and redraws root 2 and
its time-zero state. Each marginal scale transition followed by its
conditional path redraw is invariant for the same augmented generalized
posterior; hence their root-symmetric composition is invariant. Each finite
slice sweep is a Markov transition rather than an independent conditional
draw.

The centered inverse-Gamma update can mix slowly when a component is nearly
static because its scale is strongly coupled to the realized state
innovations. The confirmatory sampler therefore uses
ancillarity--sufficiency interweaving
\citep{PapaspiliopoulosRobertsSkold2007,YuMeng2011ASIS}. Define
\[
u_{kjt}=d_{kjt}/\sqrt{q_j}.
\]
Conditional on \(\theta_{k0}\), the complete path is reconstructed
recursively from
\[
\theta_{kt}
=G_t\theta_{k,t-1}
+\sum_{j=1}^J S_j^\top\sqrt{q_j}\,u_{kjt}.
\]
The two-root change of variables contributes
\(\prod_j q_j^{Td_j}\), which cancels the
\(\prod_j q_j^{-Td_j}\) scale factor from the two Gaussian innovation
densities. On the log scale \(x_j=\log q_j\), the remaining prior and
Jacobian contribution is
\[
-\sum_{j=1}^J\{a_jx_j+b_j\exp(-x_j)\}.
\]
Holding \(\vect u_1,\vect u_2,\theta_{10},\theta_{20}\), and the MTI latent
scales fixed, the noncentered log conditional is therefore, up to a constant,
\[
\log\pi(\vect x\mid\cdot)
=-\sum_{j=1}^J\{a_jx_j+b_j\exp(-x_j)\}
-\frac12\sum_{t:y_t\ {\rm observed}}
\frac{\{e_t(\vect x)-\xi_cv_t\}^2}{V_t},
\qquad
V_t=\phi_c\sigma_{\mathrm R}v_t,
\]
where \(e_t(\vect x)\) is evaluated after reconstructing both root paths.
This expression is the log density of the augmented generalized posterior in
the noncentered parameterization. Response likelihoods belong to a separately
specified response-distribution model.

For the MPI hierarchical-scale specification considered here,
\(\sigma_{\mathrm R}=s_L/\kappa\). For fixed-rate MPI or MTI,
\(\sigma_{\mathrm R}=\omega_{\mathrm R}^{-1}\). In the latter nonzero-tilt
case, the noncentered full conditional would additionally contain
\begin{equation}
\omega_{\mathrm R}c
\sum_{t\in I_{\mathrm{obs}}}\delta_t
\{\eta_{1t}(\vect x)+\eta_{2t}(\vect x)-2y_t\}.
\label{eq:supp-tilted-component-scale-asis}
\end{equation}
Although the centered inverse-Gamma full conditional given the reconstructed
paths is unchanged, the ASIS slice density is therefore not the MPI
expression:
the tilt depends on \(\vect q\) through the reconstructed states. The current
component-scale calculation is defined only for \(\delta=0\), targets the
MPI generalized posterior, and excludes nonzero tilt. Its tilted propriety
and interweaving invariance, including
\eqref{eq:supp-tilted-component-scale-asis}, require separate validation.

Each \(x_j\) is updated by a univariate stepping-out slice step with the other
log scales fixed. The finite stepping-out budget uses the standard randomized
left--right allocation, and the shrinkage step retains only points above the
sampled slice height. One interweaving cycle first draws \(\vect q\) from the
centered inverse-Gamma full conditional, computes the standardized innovations,
applies two noncentered coordinate sweeps, and reconstructs both paths. The
centered Gibbs update and the noncentered reparameterized update each leave the
same joint generalized posterior invariant, so their composition is also
invariant. The time-zero states are held fixed during the noncentered step.
Conditional shape and rate values reported in numerical diagnostics are recomputed from
the final retained paths.

One MPI sweep that leaves the specified augmented generalized
posterior invariant
updates collapsed \(\kappa\) when it is random,
refreshes \(\vect v\), applies the root-1 partially collapsed scale/path
transition in \eqref{eq:supp-component-scale-partial-collapse}, applies its
root-2 counterpart conditional on the refreshed root 1, applies the centered
component-scale and noncentered updates in the interweaving transition, and
optionally swaps the two complete root labels. Static \(q_j=0\) components are
separate degenerate models and are not part of the inverse-Gamma support.

This part separates external tolerance actions from ordinary Bayesian

\section{Response-Distribution Bayesian Shortest Intervals}
\label{sec:supp-bayes-uq}

The hybrid Bayesian--scan (HBS) action needs posterior statements about the
population distribution \(F\), whereas the MTI generalized posterior concerns
loss-defined root parameters after a target is fixed. The response-distribution
calculations below are therefore intentionally separate from the MTI
generalized posterior. The direct DP model is
\[
F\sim\mathrm{DP}(a,H),\qquad
Y_i\mid F\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}F,
\]
with fixed concentration \(a>0\) and fixed base probability measure \(H\)
\citep{Ferguson1973DP}. Data-dependent choices of \(H\) are empirical Bayes
and are not used for strict Bayesian claims.
Related Bayesian nonparametric interval work includes Dirichlet-process
quantile inference and tolerance-interval calibrations
\citep{HjortPetrone2007NonparametricQuantile,
ChoiPourmohamadSanso2025DPTolerance,
PourmohamadRichardsonSanso2026CalibratedBNP}.

\begin{proposition}[Direct DP fixed-interval content law]
\label{prop:supp-dp-content-law}
For a fixed closed interval \(I=[l,u]\), let
\(N_n(I)=\sum_{i=1}^n\ind(Y_i\in I)\). Under the direct DP model,
\[
F(I)\mid D_n\sim
\mathrm{Beta}\{aH(I)+N_n(I),a(1-H(I))+n-N_n(I)\}.
\]
Consequently \(p_n(I;c)=\Pr\{F(I)\ge c\mid D_n\}\) is available exactly from a
Beta survival probability.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Dirichlet-process conjugacy gives
\[
F\mid D_n\sim \mathrm{DP}(a+n,H_n),\qquad
H_n=\frac{aH+\sum_{i=1}^n\delta_{Y_i}}{a+n}.
\]
For any measurable partition \((I,I^c)\), the defining finite-dimensional
distribution of a Dirichlet process gives
\[
\{F(I),F(I^c)\}\mid D_n\sim
\mathrm{Dirichlet}\{(a+n)H_n(I),(a+n)H_n(I^c)\}.
\]
The first coordinate of this two-cell Dirichlet vector is Beta with parameters
\((a+n)H_n(I)=aH(I)+N_n(I)\) and
\((a+n)H_n(I^c)=a(1-H(I))+n-N_n(I)\).
\end{proof}

The Bayesian-only order-statistic action searches
\[
A_n=\{[Y_{(j)},Y_{(\ell)}]:1\le j\le \ell\le n\}
\]
and returns the first minimum-width member of
\[
\{I\in A_n:p_n(I;c)\ge 1-\gamma\}.
\]
This action quantifies posterior uncertainty about interval content under the
response-distribution model. Distribution-free tolerance certification requires
the scan-calibrated action.

The hybrid Bayesian--scan action adds the scan count:
\[
\widehat T_n^{\mathrm{HBS}}
=\min_{\mathrm{width},\,\mathrm{first\ tie}}
\{I\in A_n:N_n(I)\ge k_{n,c,\alpha},\
p_n(I;c)\ge 1-\gamma\}.
\]
The count \(k_{n,c,\alpha}\) is calibrated outside the Bayesian posterior and is
held fixed. The posterior threshold can be redundant, binding, or infeasible
within \(A_n\). The calculation distinguishes these cases without relaxing
either constraint.

Posterior shortest-target functionals are obtained by drawing a distribution
from the posterior DP using a truncated stick-breaking representation
\citep{Sethuraman1994ConstructiveDP}. For each weighted discrete draw
\(\{(A_m,W_m)\}\), the retained interval is the first minimum-width closed
support interval satisfying \(\sum_{m:A_m\in I}W_m\ge c\). This computes
posterior functionals of the population shortest interval under the drawn
distribution. It does not resample the scan count and does not create an
endpoint coverage theorem.

The smooth response-distribution alternative is a truncated Gaussian
Dirichlet-process mixture
\citep{EscobarWest1995DPM,IshwaranJames2001GibbsDPM,
IshwaranJames2002ApproximateDP}. With
sticks \(V_k\), weights \(\pi_k=V_k\prod_{\ell<k}(1-V_\ell)\),
\(V_K=1\), component indicators \(z_i\), and Gaussian kernels,
\[
Y_i\mid z_i=k,\mu_k,\lambda_k\sim
\Normal(\mu_k,\lambda_k^{-1}),\qquad
\Pr(z_i=k)=\pi_k.
\]
The DPM calculation uses Normal--Gamma component priors and Gibbs updates for
\((z_i)\), \((\mu_k,\lambda_k)\), and the sticks. For any fixed interval, the
content \(F(I)\) is evaluated analytically for each retained mixture draw and
posterior content probabilities are Monte Carlo averages over retained draws.
Shortest-interval functionals minimize mixture quantile width over lower-tail
probability on a deterministic grid with optional local refinement.

The deterministic DPM-ECM path is a MAP-style numerical comparison. It
uses fractional responsibilities, Gaussian-kernel component updates, and
monotone backtracking for the penalized objective. Posterior uncertainty
quantification and tolerance actions are supplied by the DPM posterior sampler
and scan-calibrated decision rules, respectively.

The Bayesian bootstrap \citep{Rubin1981BayesianBootstrap} is retained as a
weighted-empirical sensitivity calculation. It is useful for sensitivity
comparisons but is reported only as sensitivity evidence, without the
fixed-prior direct DP interpretation.

width at comparable coverage, and population-reference endpoint recovery when the population
roots are available. The equal-tailed interval score is retained as a
secondary, target-specific comparison. Simulation estimands, data-generating
factors, Monte Carlo precision, failure handling, and reporting rules are
prespecified \citep{MorrisWhiteCrowther2019Simulation}.

The available validation evidence is deepest for the MPI special case
\((\delta=0)\). Fixed-rate nonzero MTI has numerical checks for the
Gaussian information shift, zero-tilt equivalence, dense conditional
references, and the prespecified population-tilt illustrations in
Section~\ref{sec:supp-oracle-tilt-diagnostics}. Comparative scientific
evidence still requires an externally evaluated tilt-selection protocol and a
repeated-sample calibration study.
Detailed reproducibility information, diagnostic criteria, and decisions about which
computations support manuscript claims are maintained with the companion
code archive rather than in the statistical supplement.
